1.1 Patristic Genealogies: From Origen's Tripartite Anthropology to Cassian's Fourfold Schema

For nearly a millennium, Western Christendom read Sacred Scripture through the hermeneutical lens of the Quadriga—the fourfold sense of Scripture encompassing the literal/historical (sensus litteralis sive historicus), the allegorical/christological (sensus allegoricus), the tropological/moral (sensus tropologicus vel moralis), and the anagogical/eschatological (sensus anagogicus).01 Far from representing an arbitrary flight of subjective fantasy, the medieval Quadriga functioned as an intricate semiotic and theological architecture designed to coordinate the historical particularity of Israel's covenant with the dogmatic, ethical, and eschatological realities of the Church.02

Its conceptual genealogy begins in Alexandria with Origen (ca. 185–254), who in De Principiis (IV.2.4) mapped scriptural meaning onto Pauline trichotomous anthropology (1 Thess 5:23).03 Just as the human person consists of body (σῶμα), soul (ψυχή), and spirit (πνεῦμα), Scripture exhibits a corporeal literal sense accessible to the simple, a moral sense edifying the soul, and a spiritual sense disclosing heavenly mysteries (μυστήριον) to the mature. Origen argued that divine providence intentionally embedded apparent grammatical incongruities or historical stumbling blocks (σκάνδαλα) within the literal text to compel readers to ascend from the earthly letter to spiritual contemplation. In the Latin West, Augustine of Hippo (354–430) supplied the semiotic foundation in De Doctrina Christiana by distinguishing between proper signs (signa propria, words denoting the things for which they were instituted) and transferred or figurative signs (signa translata, where the signified thing itself points beyond to another reality).04 Crucially, Augustine subordinated all polysemy (plures sensus) to the regula caritatis—the criterion that any valid interpretation must cultivate the double love of God and neighbor (Doctr. chr. I.35.39–36.40)—while insisting in De Genesi ad Litteram that spiritual readings must never dissolve the historical veracity of the narrative (ad litteram, quam verba sonant).05

The decisive structural transition from Origen's threefold model to the standardized fourfold Quadriga occurred in the monastic conferences of John Cassian (ca. 360–435).06 In Collationes Patrum XIV.8, Cassian partitioned contemplative biblical knowledge (theorica) into historical interpretation (historica interpretatio) and spiritual knowledge (spiritalis scientia), subdividing the latter into allegoria, tropologia, and anagoge. To illustrate how a single biblical referent simultaneously sustains all four registers without contradiction, Cassian advanced the classic paradigm of Jerusalem: historically, Jerusalem is the earthly Judean city; allegorically, it signifies the Church militant of Christ; tropologically, it denotes the rational human soul (anima rationalis) striving for virtue; and anagogically, it points to the heavenly city of God, the Church triumphant.

1.2 Gregory the Great's Architectural Metaphor and the Institutionalization of the Glossa Ordinaria

Early medieval monasticism consolidated Cassian's taxonomy through the towering authority of Pope Gregory I (ca. 540–604).07 In the prefatory epistle to his Moralia in Iob (Epistula missoria ad Leandrum 3), Gregory articulated the celebrated architectural metaphor that governed medieval exegesis for centuries: first, the expositor lays the solid foundation of history (fundamenta historiae); next, through typological signification, one erects the mental structure into a fortress of faith (fabricam mentis ad arcem fidei); finally, through moral instruction, one adorns the edifice with the coloring of virtue (colore virtutum).08 Expounding Ezekiel's scroll written within and without (intus et foris, Ezek 2:10), Gregory identified the exterior writing with the literal history and the interior writing with spiritual and heavenly promise, weaving profound psychological introspection into the tropological sense.

By the twelfth century, as intellectual life migrated from monastic scriptoria to the cathedral schools of Laon and the emerging University of Paris, this patristic heritage was codified into an academic reference apparatus: the Glossa Ordinaria.09 Initiated by Anselm of Laon (d. 1117) and expanded by Gilbert de la Porrée and Peter Lombard before reaching standardized form around 1220, the Glossa surrounded the Latin Vulgate text with both interlinear lexical notes (glossa interlinearis) and marginal patristic excerpts (glossa marginalis) drawn from Jerome, Augustine, Gregory, and Bede. Through the Glossa Ordinaria—and crystallized in the thirteenth-century elegiac distich attributed to the Dominican friar Augustine of Dacia—the Quadriga operated as the formal epistemological matrix of medieval university theology, structuring how generations of masters coordinated historical data, christological dogma, moral formation, and eschatological hope.

1.1 教父釋經譜系：從俄利根的三重人類學到卡西安的四重架構

在長達近千年的時期內，西方拉丁基督教會主要透過「四重解經法」（Quadriga）的詮釋透鏡來閱讀聖經——該體系涵蓋字面／歷史意義（sensus litteralis sive historicus）、寓意／基督論意義（sensus allegoricus）、道德／轉化意義（sensus tropologicus vel moralis），以及靈意／末世意義（sensus anagogicus）。01 中世紀的四重解經法絕非任意主觀幻想的馳騁，而是一套精密的記號學與神學建築學，旨在將以色列聖約的歷史特殊性，與教會群體的教義信仰、倫理實踐及末世盼望協調統一起來。02

其思想譜系可追溯至亞歷山太的俄利根（Origen，約 185–254）。俄利根在《論基本教義》（De Principiis IV.2.4）中，將聖經的意義層次對應於保羅的三分人類學（帖前 5:23）。03 正如人由身體（σῶμα）、魂（ψυχή）與靈（πνεῦμα）構成，聖經亦具有供單純信徒閱讀的肉身字面義、造就魂之德行的道德義，以及向成熟者揭示天上奧秘（μυστήριον）的屬靈義。俄利根主張，神聖護理刻意在字面文本中安插看似語法不諧或歷史矛盾的「絆腳石」（σκάνδαλα），以迫使讀者從屬地的字句躍升至屬靈的默觀。在拉丁西方教會，希坡的奧古斯丁（Augustine of Hippo，354–430）則在《論基督教教義》（De Doctrina Christiana）中奠定了記號學基礎，區分「本義記號」（signa propria，直接指涉其所約定之實物的語詞）與「轉義／喻義記號」（signa translata，所指之物本身再指向另一實在）。04 奧古斯丁將一切多重意義（plures sensus）置於「愛德準則」（regula caritatis）的規範之下——任何合法詮釋必須促進愛神與愛鄰舍的雙重之愛（Doctr. chr. I.35.39–36.40）——同時在《創世記字義註釋》（De Genesi ad Litteram）中堅持，屬靈解經絕不可消解敘事本身的歷史真實性（ad litteram, quam verba sonant）。05

從俄利根的三重架構過渡到標準化四重解經法的關鍵轉折，發生在約翰・卡西安（John Cassian，約 360–435）的修道院靈修神學中。06 卡西安在《教父對話錄》（Collationes Patrum XIV.8）中將默觀知識（theorica）劃分為歷史詮釋（historica interpretatio）與屬靈知識（spiritalis scientia），並將後者細分為寓意（allegoria）、道德（tropologia）與末世靈意（anagoge）。為闡明單一聖經名詞如何同時承載這四重維度而不相矛盾，卡西安提出了經典的「耶路撒冷」典範：就歷史而言，耶路撒冷是地上猶太人的城市；就寓意而言，它預表地上爭戰的基督教會；就道德而言，它指涉在聖化中追求美德的理性靈魂（anima rationalis）；就末世而言，它指向天上上帝之城與凱旋的教會。

1.2 大貴格利的建築學隱喻與《標準註釋》（Glossa Ordinaria）之制度化

早期中世紀修道院傳統透過教宗大貴格利一世（Gregory I，約 540–604）的權威鞏固了卡西安的分類法。07 在其鉅著《約伯記道德註釋》（Moralia in Iob）致利安德的序言（Epistula missoria ad Leandrum 3）中，貴格利提出了影響深遠的建築學隱喻：首先，釋經者必須安放歷史的堅實地基（fundamenta historiae）；其次，透過預表性指涉，將心靈結構豎立為信仰的堡壘（fabricam mentis ad arcem fidei）；最後，藉著道德訓誨的恩典，為這座建築披上美德的色彩（colore virtutum）。08 在闡釋以西結書內外寫字的書卷（intus et foris，結 2:10）時，貴格利將外層書寫等同於字面歷史，內層書寫則等同於屬靈與天上的應許，並將細膩的心理省察融入道德詮釋之中。

到了十二世紀，隨著學術重心從修道院抄經室轉移至拉昂主教座堂學校與興起中的巴黎大學，這套教父遺產被編纂為標準化的學院教學工具——《標準註釋》（Glossa Ordinaria）。09 此彙編由拉昂的安瑟倫（卒於 1117 年）發起，經波瓦捷的吉爾伯特與彼得・倫巴德擴充，約於 1220 年定型。它將拉丁武加大譯本置於頁面中央，周圍環繞行間語詞註解（glossa interlinearis）與頁邊教父摘錄（glossa marginalis），彙集耶柔米、奧古斯丁、貴格利與比德的見解。透過《標準註釋》——並凝鍊於十三世紀道明會士達契亞的奧古斯丁所歸屬的對句格言——四重解經法成為中世紀大學神學的正式認識論矩陣，系統性地協調歷史事實、基督論教義、倫理塑造與末世盼望。

2.1 Hugh of St. Victor: Bridging Monastic Lectio Divina and Scholastic Historia

During the twelfth-century Renaissance, the Parisian Abbey of Saint Victor—founded as a community of Augustinian Canons Regular—became the premier European laboratory for recalibrating the relationship between monastic spirituality and emerging scholastic science.10 Its towering intellectual architect, Hugh of St. Victor (ca. 1096–1141), diagnosed a pervasive pathology in contemporary monastic exegesis: eager preachers routinely bypassed the grammatical and historical surface of Scripture to indulge in unanchored allegorical speculation.11

In his theoretical treatises—most notably the Didascalicon de studio legendi (Book VI) and De scripturis et scriptoribus sacris—Hugh reasserted the methodological primacy of the literal-historical sense.12 Drawing upon a triad of senses—historia (the narrative sequence of events), allegoria (the doctrinal mystery of Christ and the Church), and tropologia (moral transformation)—Hugh argued in De arca Noe morali (1.100.2, reflecting on the six wings of the seraphim in Isaiah 6:2) that Scripture kindles love of God and neighbor precisely when each level retains its proper order.13 In the Didascalicon, Hugh structured the entire pedagogical curriculum so that students mastered the seven liberal arts (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric, arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, and music) as a propaedeutic to reading the sacred page, insisting that historical and geographical competence prevents theological construction from collapsing into arbitrary subjectivism. Crucially, in De scripturis et scriptoribus sacris (5.13–15), Hugh rebuked students who disdained the literal text as intellectually beneath them: though the outward letter "may seem to you like dirt" (quasi lutum tibi videatur), it is the indispensable mortar and foundation of theology. Hugh practiced this discipline in his exegetical notes on books traditionally subjected to unrestrained allegorization—such as the Psalms, Lamentations, Joel, and Obadiah—insisting that the interpreter must first master geography, chronology, and grammatical syntax before ascending to doctrinal allegory.14

2.2 Andrew of St. Victor: Radicalizing the Literal Sense and Consulting Jewish Exegetes

If Hugh laid the theoretical cornerstone for historical exegesis, his pupil Andrew of St. Victor (ca. 1110–1175)—who later served as abbot of Wigmore in England—executed a methodological revolution.15 While Hugh regarded historia as the necessary lower tier supporting allegory and tropology, Andrew took the unprecedented step of bracketing spiritual exposition and dogmatic quaestiones almost entirely from his biblical commentaries (covering the Heptateuch, Samuel, Kings, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and the Prophets).16

Andrew concentrated exclusively on the sensus historicus vel litteralis, refusing to dissolve difficult Old Testament narratives into premature Christian allegories. Recognizing that the Latin Vulgate frequently obscured Semitic idioms and Hebrew syntax, Andrew revitalized Jerome's patristic quest for the Hebraica veritas ("Hebrew truth") through direct, sustained consultation with contemporary Northern French Jewish scholars—the heirs of Rashi's school.17 Whenever the Vulgate diverged from the Masoretic Hebrew or presented syntactic ambiguities, Andrew recorded Jewish grammatical explanations alongside Latin traditions, even defending Jewish historical readings of prophetic texts (such as Isaiah 7:14, where he carefully distinguished the immediate sign of the young woman's child in Ahaz's court from later christological typology) on philological grounds against polemical distortions—a boldness that later drew sharp criticism from his fellow Victorine Richard of St. Victor (d. 1173).18 By treating Jewish exegetes not merely as polemical adversaries but as indispensable custodians of Hebrew grammar, realia, and historical context, Andrew forged a vital link between twelfth-century literalism, thirteenth-century mendicant Hebraism, Nicholas of Lyra, and the sixteenth-century Reformation. Crucially, the Victorine program also reshaped medieval hermeneutical pedagogy: by insisting that historia encompasses not merely isolated lexical glosses but the causal sequence of events, geographical topography, and political realia of ancient Israel, Hugh and Andrew elevated literal exegesis from an elementary grammatical exercise into a rigorous historical discipline. Even when later scholastics disagreed with Andrew's reticence toward christological allegory in the Old Testament, the Victorine insistence that spiritual interpretation must never contradict the plain grammatical-historical sense became an enduring axiom of Western theological education. In sum, the Abbey of St. Victor transformed literal exegesis from a neglected preliminary into the structural guarantor of theological coherence, demonstrating that careful philological attention to Hebrew idioms, geographical realia, and historical sequence is not an enemy of spiritual contemplation, but its indispensable anchor. Moreover, by cultivating direct intellectual exchange with Northern French rabbis in Paris and Champagne, Andrew of St. Victor modeled a rare medieval openness to Jewish grammatical science that challenged Christian interpreters to verify their Old Testament citations against the Masoretic text rather than relying uncritically on Latin translations or secondhand patristic catenae. Through this Victorine synthesis of liberal arts propaedeutics, historical realism, and Hebrew philology, twelfth-century Parisian scholarship laid the methodological groundwork upon which thirteenth-century mendicant theologians and fourteenth-century Christian Hebraists would build.

2.1 聖維克多的休格：銜接修道院聖言誦讀與經院歷史釋經

在十二世紀文藝復興的浪潮中，作為奧古斯丁律法修道會團體而創立的巴黎聖維克多修道院（Abbey of St. Victor），成為歐洲重新校準修道院靈修與新興經院科學之間關係的首要實驗室。10 該學派的奠基思想家聖維克多的休格（Hugh of St. Victor，約 1096–1141）精準診斷了當代修道院解經的普遍弊病：急切的講道者往往繞過聖經的語法與歷史表層，逕自沉浸於毫無根基的寓意臆測之中。11

在其理論著作——特別是《論讀書之法》（Didascalicon de studio legendi，第六卷）與《論聖經與神聖作者》（De scripturis et scriptoribus sacris）——中，休格重新確立了字面歷史意義的方法論優先性。12 休格採用三重意義架構——歷史（historia，事件的敘事序列）、寓意（allegoria，基督與教會的教義奧秘）與道德（tropologia，倫理轉化）——並在《論挪亞方舟的道德意義》（De arca Noe morali 1.100.2，默想以賽亞書 6:2 撒拉弗的六個翅膀）中指出，唯當每個層次各守其序位時，聖經方能點燃人對上帝與鄰舍的愛。13 更為關鍵的是，休格在《論聖經與神聖作者》（5.13–15）中嚴詞斥責那些輕視字面文本的學生：外在的字句「在你看來或許如同泥土」（quasi lutum tibi videatur），但它卻是神學不可或缺的灰漿與地基。休格在《詩篇》、《耶利米哀歌》、《約珥書》與《俄巴底亞書》等歷來飽受過度寓意化之書卷的註釋筆記中躬行此道，堅持釋經者必須先掌握地理、年表與語法句法，方能攀升至教義寓意。14

2.2 聖維克多的安德烈：字面釋經之激進化與猶太拉比對話

若說休格為歷史釋經奠定了理論基石，那麼他的門生聖維克多的安德烈（Andrew of St. Victor，約 1110–1175）——後來出任英格蘭威格莫爾修道院院長——則掀起了一場方法論革命。15 休格仍將歷史（historia）視為支撐寓意與道德的下層基礎，而安德烈卻史無前例地將屬靈闡釋與教義問題（quaestiones）幾乎完全從其聖經註釋（涵蓋摩西七書、撒母耳記、列王紀、箴言、傳道書與眾先知書）中括除出去。16

安德烈專注於文本的「歷史或字面意義」（sensus historicus vel litteralis），拒絕將艱澀的舊約敘事消融於過早的基督教寓意之中。鑑於拉丁武加大譯本時常掩蓋閃語慣用語與希伯來文句法，安德烈復興了耶柔米對《希伯來真理》（Hebraica veritas）的尋索，直接且持續地向當代法國北部的猶太學者——即拉施（Rashi）學派的繼承者——請益。17 每當武加大譯本與馬所拉希伯來文本出現分歧或句法歧義時，安德烈便將猶太人的語法解釋與拉丁傳統並陳，甚至在語文學基礎上為先知書（如以賽亞書 7:14）的猶太歷史解讀辯護，反對護教性的曲解——這份大膽之舉後來招致其同門聖維克多的理查（Richard of St. Victor，卒於 1173 年）的嚴厲批評。18 然而，安德烈對語文學精確度與猶太—基督教釋經對話的堅定委身，在十二世紀字面主義、十三世紀托缽修會希伯來學、里拉的尼古拉與十六世紀宗教改革之間，鑄造了不可或缺的歷史鏈環。總而言之，聖維克多修道院將字面釋經從備受輕忽的預備階段，轉化為神學連貫性的結構保證；藉著將博雅教育、歷史地理實存與希伯來語文學緊密結合，休格與安德烈證明了對希伯來慣用語與歷史序列的嚴謹考據，絕非屬靈默觀的敵對者，而是其不可或缺的錨點。

All the spiritual senses are founded upon one—namely, the literal sense (omnes sensus fundantur super unum, scilicet litteralem)—from which alone any theological argument can be drawn (ex solo sensu litterali potest trahi argumentum). 「一切屬靈意義皆建基於唯一之字面意義之上（omnes sensus fundantur super unum, scilicet litteralem），且唯獨從字面意義方能引申出神學論證（ex solo sensu litterali potest trahi argumentum）。」

3.1 Summa Theologiae I, q. 1, a. 10: Dual Authorship, Signification by Res, and Dogmatic Proof

In the opening quaestio of the Summa Theologiae (De Sacra Doctrina), Thomas Aquinas (1224/5–1274) integrated Aristotelian causality and Augustinian semiotics to furnish medieval Christendom with its most rigorous philosophical defense of scriptural polysemy.19 Responding to the scholastic objection that multiple senses in Holy Scripture generate logical equivocation and undermine theological demonstration (ST I, q. 1, a. 10), Aquinas formulated a profound metaphysical distinction between verbal signification and providential-ontological signification.20

Human authors, Aquinas observed, can only arrange words (voces) to signify concepts and external realities (res). That primary signification whereby written words directly signify historical realities constitutes the literal or historical sense (sensus litteralis sive historicus).21 God, however, is not merely the principal author of Scripture's words, but the sovereign Creator and providential Lord of history itself. Consequently, God possesses the unique power not only to adapt words to convey meanings, but to order historical persons, events, and institutions (res ipsae) so that one reality prefigures another. This second-order signification—whereby the things signified by words in turn signify other spiritual realities (res significatae per voces iterum res alias significant)—constitutes the spiritual or mystical sense (sensus spiritualis vel mysticus), subdivided into the allegorical (Old Testament realities signifying Christ), the moral/tropological (Christ's deeds signifying how believers ought to act), and the anagogical (earthly realities signifying eternal glory).22

To prevent this spiritual superstructure from destabilizing theological science (sacra doctrina), Aquinas enforced two strict epistemological rules:23

  • Subordination and Sufficiency: Every spiritual sense presupposes and rests upon the literal sense (omnes sensus fundantur super unum, scilicet litteralem), and nothing necessary to faith is conveyed under the spiritual sense that is not elsewhere openly taught in the literal sense.
  • Exclusivity of Theological Argumentation: Because spiritual senses depend upon discerning similitudes between things (similitudines rerum), they lack the demonstrative necessity required for scientific theology; hence, ex solo sensu litterali potest trahi argumentum—theological proof may be drawn solely from the literal sense.

3.2 Epistemological Accommodation, Metaphor, and the Multivoiced Literal Sense

Equally decisive for the history of hermeneutics is Aquinas's treatment of metaphor in Summa Theologiae I, q. 1, a. 9 and a. 10 ad 3.24 In Article 9, Thomas defended Scripture's use of corporeal metaphors on the basis of epistemological accommodation: because human cognition naturally ascends from sensible phenomena to intellectual realities, divine wisdom fittingly clothes transcendent truths in material imagery so that both the learned and the simple may apprehend them.25 Crucially, in Article 10 ad 3, Aquinas clarified that metaphorical, parabolical, and figurative expressions belong entirely to the literal sense, not the spiritual sense.26 When Scripture speaks of the "arm of God," the literal sense is not a physical limb, but that which is directly intended by the words—namely, God's operative power. Thus, the sensus litteralis encompasses both the proper literal sense and the figurative literal sense intended by the author.

Furthermore, as Stephen E. Fowl has demonstrated in his analysis of Aquinas's commentary on John 1:1 and Quaestiones disputatae de potentia Dei (q. 4, a. 1), Aquinas did not reduce the literal sense to modern univocal minimalism.27 Because the literal sense is "what the author intends" (id quod auctor intendit), and the primary author is God, who comprehends all truth in one eternal act of intellect, it is not unfitting if even the literal sense of a passage sustains a bounded plurality of truthful meanings (plures sensus sub sensu litterali)—provided each reading respects the literary context and does not entail falsehood or violate the rule of faith. Thus, in expounding "In the beginning was the Word" (In principio erat Verbum, John 1:1), Aquinas willingly accommodated multiple orthodox patristic readings of principium—the causality of the Word in creation, consubstantiality within the Father, and co-eternity—as concurrent dimensions of the literal sense. At the same time, Aquinas's own Pauline commentaries (e.g., on Ephesians 1:1 and Galatians) demonstrate a remarkably concrete attention to historical occasion, epistolary genre (greeting, narrative, exhortation, conclusion), and authorial purpose, confirming that his scholastic theory of sacra doctrina was anchored in disciplined, text-attentive exegesis rather than abstract speculation. In his commentary on Ephesians 1:1, for example, Aquinas situates the epistle geographically in Asia Minor and cross-references Paul's ministry in Acts 19:1 before analyzing the theological logic of the Apostle's blessing. In this way, Aquinas harmonized Aristotelian scientific rigor with patristic spiritual depth: he preserved the devotional richness of the Quadriga for preaching and contemplation while establishing a strict epistemological firewall that prevented ungrounded allegorical fancies from dictating church dogma. Consequently, later Catholic and Protestant interpreters alike remained indebted to Aquinas's semiotic clarification that figurative and metaphorical speech-acts belong to the literal sense, and that authentic spiritual or typological meaning must always be rooted in divinely ordered historical realities (res). By distinguishing between human verbal agency and divine providential ordering of history, Thomas Aquinas rescued the fourfold Quadriga from arbitrary subjectivism while preserving its theological capacity to trace redemptive-historical continuity across the Old and New Testaments. His insistence that nothing necessary for Christian faith is hidden in the spiritual senses without being plainly stated in the literal sense elsewhere in Scripture provided a lasting epistemological safeguard for both scholastic disputation and pastoral proclamation.

3.1 《神學大全》第一部第一題第十節：雙重作者、實物指涉與教義論證

在《神學大全》（Summa Theologiae）開篇「論神聖教義」（De Sacra Doctrina）的辯論中，多瑪斯・阿奎那（Thomas Aquinas，1224/5–1274）融合亞里斯多德的四因說與奧古斯丁記號學，為中世紀基督教會提供了對聖經多重意義最嚴密的哲學辯護。19 面對經院哲學「多重意義將導致語義含混並瓦解神學論證」的質疑（ST I, q. 1, a. 10），阿奎那在語言符號指涉與神聖護理本體指涉之間，確立了一項精闢的形上學區分。20

阿奎那指出，人類作者只能組織語詞（voces）來指涉概念與外在事物（res）。這種由書寫語詞直接指涉歷史實物的第一層指涉，即構成聖經的字面或歷史意義（sensus litteralis sive historicus）。21 然而，上帝不僅是聖經語詞的首要作者，更是歷史本身的創造主與護理之主。因此，上帝擁有獨一無二的能力：祂不僅能調度語詞以傳達意義，更能調度歷史人物、事件與制度（res ipsae），使一個歷史實物預表並指向另一個實物。這種第二層指涉——即語詞所指涉的實物本身又轉而指涉其他屬靈實物（res significatae per voces iterum res alias significant）——便構成了屬靈或奧秘意義（sensus spiritualis vel mysticus），並進一步細分為寓意（舊約實物指向基督）、道德（基督的作為指向信徒當行之道）與末世靈意（地上實物指向永恆榮耀）。22

為防止這套屬靈意義結構動搖作為「科學」之神學（sacra doctrina）的穩定性，阿奎那立下了兩條嚴格的認識論鐵律：23

  • 從屬性與完備性： 一切屬靈意義皆預設並建基於字面意義之上（omnes sensus fundantur super unum, scilicet litteralem）；凡屬靈意義中所蘊含之關乎救恩信仰的真理，皆已在別處的字面意義中公開明言。
  • 神學論證之排他性： 由於屬靈意義仰賴辨識事物之間的相似性（similitudines rerum），缺乏演繹必然性，故「唯獨從字面意義方能引申出神學論證」（ex solo sensu litterali potest trahi argumentum），絕不能僅憑寓意或道德解經來確立教義。

3.2 認識論調適、隱喻歸屬與「多聲部」的字面意義

同樣對詮釋學史具決定性貢獻的，是阿奎那在《神學大全》I, q. 1, a. 9 及 a. 10 ad 3 中對隱喻與修辭的精準定位。24 在第九節中，阿奎那以認識論調適（epistemological accommodation）為聖經使用物質隱喻辯護：既然人類認知自然地從感官事物攀升至理智真理，神聖智慧便適宜地將超越真理披上物質意象的外衣，使智者與單純人皆能按其本性領受。25 更為關鍵的是，阿奎那在第十節第三異議答覆中闡明：隱喻、比喻與修辭語言完全屬於字面意義的範疇，而非屬靈意義。26 當聖經提及「上帝的膀臂」時，其字面意義並非指肉身肢體，而是語詞所直接意圖表達的實在——即上帝大能的作為。因此，sensus litteralis 同時涵蓋本義的字面義與作者直接意圖的修辭字面義。

此外，正如史蒂芬・福爾（Stephen E. Fowl）剖析阿奎那《約翰福音註釋》（論約 1:1）與《論上帝的大能》（De potentia Dei q. 4, a. 1）時所揭示的，阿奎那並未將字面意義簡化為現代單一扁平的字義主義。27 既然字面意義即「作者所意圖者」（id quod auctor intendit），而首要作者乃在單一永恆理智行動中洞察萬有的上帝，那麼同一段經文的字面意義本身亦可能容納受正典與真理邊界所規範的多重真確含義（plures sensus sub sensu litterali），只要該解讀不違背語境、不致謬誤且符合信仰準則即可。同時，阿奎那在《保羅書信註釋》（如論以弗所書 1:1）中亦展現了對寫作處境、書信體裁與使徒意圖的細緻考察，在亞里斯多德科學嚴謹性與教父屬靈深度之間取得了平衡——既保留四重解經法於講道與默想中的靈修價值，又為教會教義設立了堅實的認識論防線。

Late Medieval Reconfiguration: Nicholas of Lyra, Christian Hebraism, and the Duplex Sensus Litteralis 晚期中世紀之重構：里拉的尼古拉、基督教希伯來學與「雙重字面意義」

Si Lyra non lyrasset, Lutherus non saltasset. ("If Lyra had not played his lyre, Luther would not have danced.") 「倘若尼古拉・德・里拉未曾撥響他的里拉琴，馬丁・路德便無從起舞。」（Si Lyra non lyrasset, Lutherus non saltasset.）

4.1 Christian Hebraism and the Prologus Secundus of the Postillae Perpetuae

By the turn of the fourteenth century, the balance between the literal foundation and the spiritual senses had fractured in late medieval preaching and commentary. Despite Aquinas's theoretical safeguards, popular expositors and Franciscan postillators frequently allowed mystical, moralizing, and allegorical glosses to suffocate the grammatical and historical coherence of the Old Testament.28 Into this hermeneutical crisis stepped the Franciscan scholar Nicholas of Lyra (ca. 1270–1349), who became regent master of theology at the University of Paris in 1309 and composed the monumental verse-by-verse commentary Postillae Perpetuae in Universam Sacram Scripturam.29

In his programmatic Prologus Secundus ("Second Prologue: On the Intention of the Author and the Mode of Proceeding"), Lyra lamented that the literal sense had been virtually buried under a proliferation of arbitrary mystical interpretations.30 Echoing Gregory the Great and Hugh of St. Victor, Lyra insisted that just as a building whose foundation is undermined inevitably collapses, so spiritual exposition detached from grammatical-historical bedrock degenerates into absurdity. To restore the literal sense, Lyra deployed an extraordinary command of Biblical Hebrew and post-biblical rabbinic literature—above all the commentaries of Rashi (Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki, 1040–1105), whom Lyra respectfully cited throughout the Postillae as Rabbi Salomon.31 Although Lyra also authored a separate Postilla Moralis to preserve edifying spiritual application, his Postilla Litteralis systematically corrected Latin Vulgate obscurities against the Masoretic Hebrew text and rabbinic philology, earning his work an unprecedented status as the standard late medieval reference commentary.32

4.2 The Duplex Sensus Litteralis and the Trajectory Toward the Reformation

Lyra's most enduring theoretical innovation was his formulation of the duplex sensus litteralis ("double literal sense").33 The acute exegetical dilemma confronting post-Thomistic theologians was how to interpret Old Testament messianic prophecies and royal narratives—such as Nathan's oracle to David in 2 Samuel 7:12–14 ("I will be a father to him, and he shall be a son to me"; cf. 1 Chr 22:10)—that possessed an unmistakable immediate historical referent (Solomon) while functioning in the New Testament (e.g., Heb 1:5) as authoritative christological proof-texts.34 Traditional exegetes simply assigned the Solomon referent to the sensus litteralis and relegated the christological fulfillment to the sensus allegoricus. Lyra recognized that this move created a fatal dogmatic contradiction: under Aquinas's rule (ex solo sensu litterali potest trahi argumentum), an allegorical reading could not sustain doctrinal proof of Christ's divine sonship.

To resolve this impasse without abandoning historical integrity, Lyra posited that a single prophetic or historical text may possess two distinct dimensions within the literal sense itself:35

  • Sensus litteralis historicus: The immediate, primary historical referent intended in the ancient covenantal setting (e.g., Solomon as David's royal successor building the Jerusalem temple).
  • Sensus litteralis propheticus vel mysticus: The fuller, divinely intended christological fulfillment embedded within the literal design of prophetic discourse (e.g., Jesus Christ as the eternal Son of God building the eschatological temple).

By relocating typological-christological fulfillment inside the sensus litteralis, Lyra safeguarded the doctrinal authority of Old Testament prophecy while honoring Hebrew grammar and Israelite history. In practice, this meant that an Old Testament passage did not lose its historical integrity once Christ arrived; rather, the immediate covenantal referent and the eschatological messianic fulfillment functioned as two coordinated moments within a single divine speech-act. Moreover, Lyra's rigorous critique of corrupt Latin manuscripts, his insistence on comparing the Vulgate against the Masoretic Text and Aramaic Targumim, and his willingness to adopt Rashi's grammatical solutions over conventional patristic allegories marked a watershed in Western biblical scholarship. Printed alongside the Glossa Ordinaria in early Renaissance Bibles—including the monumental Basel and Antwerp editions—Lyra's Postillae served as the primary exegetical bridge between high medieval scholasticism and early modern humanism, directly shaping Martin Luther's early lectures on the Psalms and Genesis and inspiring the famous sixteenth-century epigram: Si Lyra non lyrasset, Lutherus non saltasset.36 Importantly, Lyra's duplex sensus litteralis also exposed an internal tension that would soon fracture the medieval synthesis: once the prophetic-christological meaning was relocated inside the literal sense, the remaining three tiers of the Quadriga—allegory, tropology, and anagogy—increasingly appeared redundant for doctrinal exegesis. When sixteenth-century Reformers subsequently rejected the fourfold schema in favor of a single, unified grammatical-historical sense that included christological typology, they were extending the trajectory that Nicholas of Lyra had already set in motion. By demonstrating that a Christian scholar could learn Hebraic grammar and contextual exegesis from Rashi without surrendering christological conviction, Lyra redefined late medieval biblical interpretation and prepared the methodological ground for the Renaissance ad fontes movement and the Protestant Reformation. Even when subsequent humanists and Reformers critiqued aspects of scholastic theology, they continued to rely heavily on Lyra's philological notes, his Hebrew-informed corrections of the Vulgate, and his insistence that prophetic fulfillment must be argued from the literal texture of the biblical canon rather than detached allegorical speculation.

4.1 基督教希伯來學與《全聖經連續註釋》（Postillae Perpetuae）第二序言

到了十四世紀初葉，晚期中世紀講道與註釋中的字面根基與屬靈意義之間的平衡已然失調。儘管多瑪斯・阿奎那在理論上設下了認識論防線，通俗解經家與部分托缽修會註釋者仍經常任憑奧秘、道德化與寓意註解窒息舊約文本的語法與歷史連貫性。28 面對這場詮釋危機，方濟各會學者里拉的尼古拉（Nicholas of Lyra，約 1270–1349）挺身而出。他於 1309 年出任巴黎大學神學教授，並撰寫了逐節解析全本聖經的鉅著《全聖經連續註釋》（Postillae Perpetuae in Universam Sacram Scripturam）。29

在其綱領性的《第二序言》（Prologus Secundus，「論作者之意圖與進路」）中，里拉痛陳字面意義已被繁瑣武斷的神秘詮釋所掩埋。30 呼應大貴格利與聖維克多的休格，里拉強調：正如地基受損的建築終必傾圮，脫離文法與歷史根基的屬靈闡釋亦必淪為荒謬。為重建字面意義的權威，里拉展現了同時代基督徒學者中極為罕見的聖經希伯來文與拉比文獻造詣——其中最核心的資源便是拉施（Rashi，1040–1105）的聖經註釋，里拉在《註釋》中屢屢尊稱其為「所羅門拉比」（Rabbi Salomon）。31 雖然里拉亦另外撰寫《道德註釋》（Postilla Moralis）以保留靈修應用，但他的《字面註釋》（Postilla Litteralis）系統性地參照馬所拉希伯來文本與拉比語文學，校勘拉丁武加大譯本的晦澀之處，使其著作躍升為晚期中世紀最具權威性的標準參考註釋。32

4.2 「雙重字面意義」（Duplex Sensus Litteralis）與邁向宗教改革的軌跡

里拉最具深遠影響的理論創新，在於提出「雙重字面意義」（duplex sensus litteralis）。33 後多瑪斯時期神學家面臨一個尖銳的釋經兩難：如何詮釋那些既有明確當前歷史指涉（如拿單向大衛所說的預言，撒下 7:12–14「我要作他的父，他要作我的子」；參代上 22:10，指向所羅門），又在新約（如來 1:5）中被援引為權威性基督論證據的舊約彌賽亞預言？34 傳統解經家往往將所羅門歸入字面意義（sensus litteralis），而將基督論的應驗歸入寓意意義（sensus allegoricus）。然而里拉敏銳察覺，此舉在阿奎那的「唯獨字面意義能引申神學論證」原則下，將導致致命的教義矛盾——若基督論應驗僅屬寓意，便無法作為證明基督神子身分的有效神學證據。

為在不犧牲歷史誠信的前提下化解此僵局，里拉主張單一先知或歷史文本在字面意義本身之內即可包含兩個向度：35

  • 歷史的字面意義（Sensus litteralis historicus）： 古代聖約處境中直接意圖的歷史指涉（例如大衛王室繼承人所羅門建造耶路撒冷聖殿）。
  • 先知或奧秘的字面意義（Sensus litteralis propheticus vel mysticus）： 聖靈從起初即嵌入先知言說之字面結構中的更豐滿基督論應驗（例如永恆神子耶穌基督建造末世聖殿）。

藉著將預表性基督論應驗重新安頓於 sensus litteralis 之內，里拉既捍衛了舊約預言的教義權威，又尊重了希伯來語法與以色列歷史。在文藝復興早期的印刷聖經中，里拉的《註釋》常與《標準註釋》並列印行，並直接塑造了馬丁・路德早期《詩篇》與《創世記》的講授，從而催生了十六世紀那句膾炙人口的拉丁格言：Si Lyra non lyrasset, Lutherus non saltasset（若非里拉撥琴，路德無從起舞）。36

● TALMUDIC CANON · B. SHABBAT 63A (PESHAT PRIORITY) 塔木德釋經公理 · 《巴比倫塔木德・安息日篇》63a

Ein mikra yotze midei peshuto (אֵין מִקְרָא יוֹצֵא מִידֵי פְּשׁוּטוֹ) — "A scriptural verse never departs from its plain, contextual meaning," even when midrashic derash derives legal or homiletic lessons from it. 「經文永不脫離其字面與語境原義」（אֵין מִקְרָא יוֹצֵא מִידֵי פְּשׁוּטוֹ, Ein mikra yotze midei peshuto）——即便米德拉什（derash）從中引申律法或講道教訓，文本的字面意義依然不可磨滅。

5.1 The PaRDeS Architecture and the Geonic-Andalusian Awakening of Peshat

In parallel with Christian scholasticism, medieval Judaism cultivated an equally sophisticated multi-tiered hermeneutic known as PaRDeS (פַּרְדֵּס, "orchard" or "Paradise"), formalized as a mnemonic acronym at the close of the thirteenth century by Bahya ben Asher.37 Unlike exclusionary modern historicism, classical rabbinic parshanut treats these four strata as complementary lenses, physically embodied on the folio pages of the Miqra'ot Gedolot (Rabbinic Bible), where the Masoretic text and Aramaic Targum sit surrounded by rival medieval commentators:38

  • Peshat (פְּשָׁט): The plain, contextual, grammatical-syntactic sense of the verse in its immediate and canonical literary setting.
  • Remez (רֶמֶז): The "hinted" allegorical, philosophical, or symbolic sense (cultivated later by Ralbag, Isaac Arama, and Obadiah Sforno, with Philonic antecedents).
  • Derash (דְּרָשׁ): From d-r-sh ("to inquire/search out"), the homiletical and legal exposition bridging Written and Oral Torah—subdivided into Midrash Halakhah (normative law) and Midrash Aggadah (narrative and ethical theology).
  • Sod (סוֹד): The esoteric Kabbalistic reading treating letters and tags as a symbolic cipher of divine emanation via gematria (numerology) and notarikon (acrostics).39

Although the Talmudic sages articulated the maxim ein mikra yotze midei peshuto ("a verse never departs from its plain sense," b. Shabbat 63a), derash remained the dominant mode of Jewish study until the tenth and eleventh centuries.40 The emergence of peshat as a self-conscious philological discipline was catalyzed by Jewish engagement with Arabic linguistics and Greek rationalism in the Islamic world—most decisively through Saadiah Gaon (882–942), whose Arabic Torah translation and Hebrew grammatical treatises established the triliteral root system and uncovered weak consonants (alef, vav, yod).41

5.2 Five Master Commentators: Northern France, Andalusia, and the Provençal-Catalan Synthesis

Between the eleventh and thirteenth centuries, five master exegetes defined classical rabbinic parshanut across two regional schools and their southern synthesis:

1. Rashi (Solomon ben Isaac, 1040–1105, Troyes): The preeminent commentator of Ashkenazic Jewry, Rashi crafted a concise synthesis blending contextual peshat with carefully curated rabbinic midrashim. As Sarah Kamin and Menahem Banitt demonstrate, Rashi's methodological distinction between "what our rabbis expounded" (darshu rabboteinu) and "its plain sense" (peshuto) aimed to "set each word upon its proper base" (leyashev davar al opnav), utilizing Spanish lexicons (Menahem ben Saruq and Dunash ibn Labrat), Mishnaic comparisons, Old French glosses (la'az), and pointed anti-christological polemics (e.g., on Psalms 2 and 21).42

2. Rashbam (Samuel ben Meir, ca. 1080–1160): Rashi's grandson pushed Northern French peshat to its most radical autonomy. Rashbam explicitly opposed peshat to derash, maintaining that the plain grammatical sense retains independent validity even when it diverges from normative halakhah, while pioneering literary analysis of narrative foreshadowing and poetic "staircase parallelism" (convincing Rashi himself to revise his commentary on Exodus 15:6).43

3. Abraham ibn Ezra (1089–1164, Spain): Operating out of the Judeo-Arabic Andalusian renaissance, Ibn Ezra applied uncompromising Hebrew grammar, astronomy, and rationalist philology, fiercely rejecting unanchored midrashic embellishments and opaque liturgical poetry (piyyutim, cf. his commentary on Eccl 5:1).44

4. Radak (David Kimhi, ca. 1160–1235, Narbonne): Bridging Andalusian philology and Northern French tradition, Radak authored the foundational lexicon Sefer ha-Shorashim and grammar Mikhlol, weaving Maimonidean rationalism with sensitivity to Rashi and sharp anti-Christian polemic.

5. Ramban (Moses ben Nahman / Nahmanides, 1194–1270, Gerona): Rejecting Ibn Ezra's purely grammatical rationalism as spiritually arid, Ramban forged a holistic synthesis uniting Andalusian philology, Rashi's midrashic piety, deep psychological character analysis, Kabbalistic sod, and covenantal typology—epitomized by his famous dictum ma'aseh avot siman le-vanim ("the deeds of the Patriarchs are a sign for their descendants").45

5.1 PaRDeS 四層架構與革尼期—安達盧西亞的字義釋經覺醒

與基督教經院哲學平行，中世紀猶太教亦發展出一套同樣精密的多層次詮釋體系，即著名的「PaRDeS」（פַּרְדֵּס，意為「果園」或「樂園」）——此首字母縮略詞於十三世紀末由聖經註釋家巴希亞・本・阿舍爾（Bahya ben Asher）正式定型。37 不同於排他性的現代歷史主義，古典拉比釋經傳統（parshanut）將這四個層次視為相輔相成的透鏡，並具體體現於《大聖經註釋本》（Miqra'ot Gedolot）的版面設計中——馬所拉希伯來文本與亞蘭文他爾根（Targum）居於中央，周圍並列不同學派的中世紀註釋家：38

  • 字義層（Peshat, פְּשָׁט）： 經文在語法、句法、詞彙與直接文學語境中的平直原義。
  • 暗示層（Remez, רֶמֶז）： 隱藏於表層之下的寓意、哲學或象徵意義（後來在十四至十六世紀由拉爾巴格、亞拉馬與斯福諾發揚，遠承斐洛傳統）。
  • 講論層（Derash, דְּרָשׁ）： 源於動詞字根 d-r-sh（「尋求、探究」），指連結成文妥拉與口傳妥拉的講道與律法推論——細分為律法米德拉什（Midrash Halakhah）與敘事／倫理米德拉什（Midrash Aggadah）。
  • 奧秘層（Sod, סוֹד）： 卡巴拉神秘主義詮釋，將希伯來字母與冠飾視為神聖流溢的密碼，運用數字命理（gematria）與首字母縮寫法（notarikon）加以破譯。39

儘管塔木德賢哲早已立下「經文永不脫離其字面意義」（ein mikra yotze midei peshuto，《巴比倫塔木德・安息日篇》63a）的公理，直至十至十一世紀以前，derash 始終是猶太研經的主流模式。40 Peshat 之所以能崛起為一門自覺的語文學科，乃受惠於伊斯蘭世界中的阿拉伯語文學與希臘理性主義刺激——特別是薩阿迪亞・加翁（Saadiah Gaon，882–942）的阿拉伯文妥拉譯本與希伯來語法論著，確立了三輔音字根系統並揭示了弱輔音（alef, vav, yod）的音變規律。41

5.2 五大釋經宗師：法國北部、安達盧西亞與普羅旺斯—加泰隆尼亞之綜合

在十一至十三世紀之間，五大釋經宗師橫跨兩大區域學派及其南部綜合傳統，鑄就了古典拉比釋經的巔峰：

1. 拉施（Rashi，所羅門・本・以撒，1040–1105，特魯瓦）： 阿什肯納茲猶太教最崇高的註釋宗師，以簡潔精煉的文風將語境 peshat 與精選的拉比 midrashim 融為一體。誠如莎拉・卡敏（Sarah Kamin）與梅納赫姆・巴尼特（Menahem Banitt）所指出，拉施區分「我們拉比所闡釋的」（darshu rabboteinu）與「經文之字面義」（peshuto），旨在「使每個語詞安頓於其正當之處」（leyashev davar al opnav），同時善用西班牙希伯來詞典（梅納赫姆・本・薩魯克與杜納什・伊本・拉布拉特）、米書拿希伯來語對比、古法語註解（la'az），以及針對基督教基督論釋經的護教論戰（如詩篇第二篇與二十一篇）。42

2. 拉什巴姆（Rashbam，撒母耳・本・梅厄，約 1080–1160）： 拉施的外孫將法北 peshat 學派推向最徹底的自主性。他明確將 peshat 與 derash 對立起來，主張即便字面語法意義與規範性的 halakhah 律法傳統有所出入，peshat 仍保有獨立的真理效力；同時，他敏銳辨識出聖經敘事伏筆與希伯來詩歌「階梯式平行體」（staircase parallelism），甚至促使外祖父拉施修訂自己對出埃及記 15:6 的註釋。43

3. 亞伯拉罕・伊本・以斯拉（Abraham ibn Ezra，1089–1164，西班牙）： 出身穆斯林西班牙猶太—阿拉伯學術文藝復興，伊本・以斯拉將嚴格的希伯來語法、天文學與理性主義語文學引入釋經，嚴厲批判脫離文本的米德拉什臆想與晦澀不合語法的禮儀詩歌（piyyutim，見傳 5:1 註釋）。44

4. 拉達克（Radak，大衛・金希，約 1160–1235，納博訥）： 銜接安達盧西亞語文學與法北傳統，拉達克編纂了詞典《字根書》（Sefer ha-Shorashim）與語法《全書》（Mikhlol），將邁蒙尼德理性哲學、對拉施的尊重與銳利的反基督教護教結合。

5. 拉姆班（Ramban，納赫馬尼德斯，1194–1270，赫羅納）： 批評伊本・以斯拉純粹語法理性主義過於乾涸，拉姆班開創了全人整合的新典範，融合安達盧西亞語文學、拉施的米德拉什虔敬、深層人物心理剖析、卡巴拉奧秘（sod）與聖約歷史預表論——凝鍊於其名言「先祖的行事乃是後裔的預兆」（ma'aseh avot siman le-vanim）。45

Medieval Christian scholars explicitly recognized Rashi's running Torah and Talmudic commentaries as the Jewish counterpart to their own Glossa Ordinaria, designating Rashi's corpus the Glossa Hebraica or Glossa Salomonis. 中世紀基督教學者明確將拉施（Rashi）的聖經與塔木德註釋視為猶太教版的《標準註釋》（Glossa Ordinaria），並尊稱其著作為「希伯來註釋」（Glossa Hebraica）或「所羅門註釋」（Glossa Salomonis）。

6.1 The Twelfth-Century Parallel Revival of Literalism: Smalley, Touito, and Scholastic Epistemology

A landmark achievement of modern medieval historiography—pioneered by Beryl Smalley (The Study of the Bible in the Middle Ages) and deepened by Elazar Touito—is the demonstration that the eleventh- and twelfth-century rise of Northern French peshat did not unfold in a cultural vacuum.46 Rather, Jewish pashtanim (Rashi, Rashbam, Joseph Kara) and Christian Victorines (Hugh and Andrew of St. Victor) inhabited a shared intellectual climate in Northern France characterized by a simultaneous turn toward grammar, historical context, and empirical causality.47

Touito highlights striking semantic correspondences between the technical vocabularies of both traditions: twelfth-century Christian exegetes employed the triad sensa (sense), littera (letter), and sententia (reasoned meaning) as direct conceptual counterparts to the Hebrew terms pitaron (solution/sense), perush (commentary/letter), and nimmuq (rationale).48 Moreover, the methodological debate over how literal exegesis relates to traditional authority mirrored contemporary scholastic disputes over universals:49 whereas extreme Realism (Anselmian) tended to collapse historical particularity into eternal spiritual truth, and Nominalism (Roscellinus) stressed discrete textual particulars, the Conceptualism of Peter Abelard—which validated both contextual linguistic sense and traditional functional truth—paralleled Rashi's and Rashbam's dual validation of contextual peshat alongside normative rabbinic halakhah. Institutionally, Christian Hebraists acknowledged this kinship by dubbing Rashi's corpus the Glossa Hebraica or Glossa Salomonis, the Jewish mirror of the Latin Glossa Ordinaria.50

6.2 Structural Convergence and Theological Divergence: Quadriga Versus PaRDeS

Comparing the Christian Quadriga and Rabbinic PaRDeS reveals both profound structural symmetry and irreducible theological divergence:51

  • Literal Foundation (Sensus Litteralis vs. Peshat): Both traditions converged on the axiom that grammar, syntax, and historical context form the non-negotiable baseline (omnes sensus fundantur super unum / ein mikra yotze midei peshuto), even though Jewish peshat prioritized Hebrew philology earlier and more consistently than Latin Christendom prior to Nicholas of Lyra.52
  • Allegory and Typology (Allegoria vs. Remez & Ma'aseh Avot): Christian allegory was fundamentally christocentric and ecclesiological—grounded in Aquinas's ontology of providential res prefiguring Christ—whereas Jewish remez leaned toward Maimonidean philosophical allegory, and Ramban's ma'aseh avot siman le-vanim anchored typology in Israel's ongoing covenantal history.
  • Moral and Legal Application (Tropologia vs. Derash): Christian tropology traced the individual soul's interior ascent in virtue and charity, whereas rabbinic derash sustained the communal covenantal life of Israel through Midrash Halakhah (legal observance) and Aggadah.53
  • Eschatological and Mystical Ascent (Anagogia vs. Sod): Anagogy oriented Christian hope toward the beatific vision and heavenly Jerusalem, whereas Kabbalistic sod deciphered the Hebrew letters of Torah as a living cosmic blueprint of the divine sefirot.

For contemporary seminary exegesis, Epoch 3 offers an enduring hermeneutical corrective: neither tradition treated the literal sense as an antiquarian dead end, yet both learned—through painful polemic and philological rigor—that spiritual, moral, and theological syntheses remain credible only when disciplined by the grammatical-historical voice of the text itself.54 When modern interpreters dismiss medieval exegesis as naive allegorizing, they overlook the hard-won semiotic precision of Hugh and Andrew of St. Victor, Thomas Aquinas, Nicholas of Lyra, Rashi, Rashbam, and Ramban—scholars who demonstrated that deep theological fidelity and rigorous original-language philology are not adversaries, but mutually reinforcing dimensions of responsible scriptural interpretation. Indeed, the parallel twelfth-century turn toward sensus litteralis and peshat reveals a recurring hermeneutical law across epochs: whenever religious communities confront textual ambiguity, polemical challenge, or philosophical renewal, they are driven back to the grammatical, lexical, and historical particularity of the biblical text as the indispensable anchor of all theological meaning. Both the Christian Quadriga and Rabbinic PaRDeS thus stand as monuments to an enduring conviction: that Sacred Scripture is simultaneously a human linguistic artifact demanding philological rigor and a divine communicative word inviting lifelong theological, moral, and eschatological engagement.

6.1 十二世紀字面釋經之平行復興：斯莫利、圖伊托與經院認識論

現代中世紀史學的一項里程碑式成就——由貝麗爾・斯莫利（Beryl Smalley）在其名著《中世紀的聖經研究》中所開創，並由以拉撒・圖伊托（Elazar Touito）所深化——在於證實了十一至十二世紀法北 peshat 學派的興起並非孤立的文化現象。46 相反地，猶太字面釋經家（拉施、拉什巴姆、約瑟・卡拉）與基督教聖維克多學派（休格與安德烈）共同生活於法國北部的知識氛圍中，同步經歷了一場轉向語法、歷史語境與經驗因果律的學術覺醒。47

圖伊托指出兩大傳統的技術詞彙之間存在驚人的語義對應：十二世紀基督教釋經家所採用的三元術語 sensa（意義）、littera（字面）與 sententia（理據），正直接對應於希伯來文術語 pitaron（解答／意義）、perush（註解／字面）與 nimmuq（理由）。48 此外，關於字面釋經如何與傳統權威並存的爭論，亦精確映照了當時經院哲學的「共相之爭」：49 極端唯實論（安瑟倫式）傾向將歷史特殊性消融於永恆屬靈真理，而唯名論（羅瑟林式）則強調離散的文本細節；介於兩者之間，彼得・阿伯拉爾（Peter Abelard）的概念論（Conceptualism）同時肯定語境語言意義與傳統功能真理的合法性，恰好與拉施及拉什巴姆雙重肯定語境 peshat 與規範性 halakhah 的立場相互輝映。在制度層面，基督教希伯來學者亦承認此一親緣關係，將拉施的註釋集稱為「希伯來註釋」（Glossa Hebraica）或「所羅門註釋」（Glossa Salomonis），視為猶太版的《標準註釋》。50

6.2 結構趨同與神學分歧：四重解經法（Quadriga）與 PaRDeS 之對照

將基督教四重解經法（Quadriga）與拉比樂園釋經法（PaRDeS）並置比較，既顯露深層的結構對稱，亦揭示不可化約的神學分歧：51

  • 字面根基（Sensus Litteralis 與 Peshat）： 兩大傳統共同確立「語法與歷史語境乃不可動搖的基礎」（omnes sensus fundantur super unum / ein mikra yotze midei peshuto），儘管猶太 peshat 在里拉的尼古拉之前便已更早且更一貫地貫徹希伯來語文學。52
  • 寓意與預表（Allegoria 與 Remez / Ma'aseh Avot）： 基督教寓意本質上是以基督與教會為中心（建基於阿奎那護理之 res 預表基督）；猶太 remez 則傾向邁蒙尼德式的哲學寓意，而納赫馬尼德斯的「先祖行事為後裔預兆」則將預表錨定於以色列的聖約歷史。
  • 道德與律法應用（Tropologia 與 Derash）： 基督教道德義追蹤個人靈魂在美德與愛德中的內在攀升；拉比 derash 則透過 Midrash Halakhah（律法實踐）與 Aggadah 維繫以色列群體的聖約生活。53
  • 末世與神秘攀升（Anagogia 與 Sod）： 末世靈意引領基督徒盼望榮福直觀與天上耶路撒冷；卡巴拉 sod 則將妥拉的希伯來字母破譯為神聖質點（sefirot）流溢的宇宙藍圖。

對當代神學研究院釋經而言，第三時期留下了恆久的詮釋學鏡鑑：兩大傳統皆未將字面意義視為枯燥的考古終點，卻都在論戰與語文學的錘鍊中體認到——唯有受文本自身文法與歷史聲音所約束的屬靈、道德與神學綜合，方能保有真實的學術與信仰公信力。54

SYNTHESIS & NEXT HORIZON 全章總結與下一篇章

From Medieval Duplex Sensus Litteralis to the Reformation Grammatico-Historical Turn. 從中世紀「雙重字義」邁向宗教改革之文法歷史釋經轉向。

When the Victorines, Thomas Aquinas, and Nicholas of Lyra anchored Christian theology in the literal sense by consulting Rashi's peshat, they inadvertently forged the philological weapons that Martin Luther and John Calvin would wield in the sixteenth century to dismantle the allegorical Quadriga and proclaim Scriptura sui ipsius interpres. 當聖維克多學派、多瑪斯・阿奎那與里拉的尼古拉透過參照拉施（Rashi）的字義釋經（peshat）而將基督教神學重新錨定於字面意義時，他們實則為十六世紀的馬丁・路德與約翰・加爾文鑄造了語文學的利器，使宗教改革得以拆解寓意化的四重解經上層建築，並宣告「以經解經」（Scriptura sui ipsius interpres）的文法歷史原則。
