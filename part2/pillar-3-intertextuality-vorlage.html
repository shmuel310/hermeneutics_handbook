01 · WITNESSES AND RECONSTRUCTION 01 · 文本見證與底本重建

Witnesses, Versions, and Reconstructed Parents 文本見證、譯本與底本重建

● EVIDENTIAL DISTINCTION 證據層次

A surviving witness, an edited text, and a reconstructed parent are different kinds of evidence. 現存見證、校勘本與重建的底本屬於不同層次的證據。

1.1 Witnesses and reconstructions

Textual criticism begins by identifying what is actually preserved. A manuscript is a particular physical witness to a text at a particular point in its transmission; a modern critical edition is an editorial construction from available witnesses. Neither should be confused with an author's original manuscript. The Masoretic Text is a transmitted Hebrew textual tradition, while the designation Septuagint can refer to a collection of Greek translations and their complex subsequent history. The texts of individual books have different histories, and Greek manuscripts may preserve revisions as well as earlier forms.01

A Vorlage is the source text used by a translator or copyist. It might itself be preserved in a manuscript, but often the term refers to a hypothetical parent reconstructed from an ancient translation. An inference about that parent must be distinguished from the observable reading of a manuscript. When a Greek version differs from a medieval Hebrew witness, the difference may reflect a different Hebrew source, the translator's interpretation or idiom, a later change in either tradition, or more than one of these processes. A single comparison rarely settles which account is correct.02

1.2 Translation technique and controls

Before retroverting a Greek reading into Hebrew, examine how the translator handles the same construction and vocabulary elsewhere in the same book. A consistently literal rendering offers a different evidential basis from a flexible paraphrase. Even repeated correspondences do not give every Greek term a unique Hebrew equivalent. Context, word order, idiom and the state of the Greek text must all be considered. A proposed parent text is stronger when an independent ancient Hebrew witness preserves a comparable reading, but even agreement between witnesses does not by itself establish a direct line of copying.03

The Judean Desert manuscripts provide direct evidence for Hebrew textual diversity before the medieval codices. They also preserve readings close to later traditions. Their significance should not be flattened into either a claim of uniformity or a claim that every variant is equally ancient. The date of a surviving manuscript and the age of a reading are related questions, not identical ones. Literary editions, scribal errors and interpretive changes require different explanations; a critic must state which explanation is proposed and why.04

1.3 What a critical edition permits the reader to see

A critical edition is a research tool, not a transparent photograph of the past. Its running text is accompanied by editorial judgments and, in many editions, an apparatus that lists selected variants. An apparatus has limits: it may not record every spelling difference, cite every manuscript, or explain the entire history of each witness. Before drawing a conclusion from a symbol, the reader must consult the edition's introduction and learn what its abbreviations and selection principles mean. When the apparatus omits a witness, determine whether the omission reflects an editorial convention, an unavailable reading, or the actual state of the manuscript. The same symbol can be misread when transferred from one edition to another without checking the key.30

The scholar who uses several editions should keep their editorial purposes distinct. A diplomatic text aims to represent a particular witness according to defined transcription conventions; an eclectic text selects readings from multiple witnesses. Each can be valuable, but neither supplies the history of all copies on its own. In teaching, students can compare an edition's base text and its apparatus, then write separately what the editor prints, what a specified witness attests, and what the student infers. Such an exercise makes it clear that a witness cannot be counted twice simply because it is mentioned in two modern editions.31

1.4 Reading translation as an ancient act of interpretation

A translation is not a mechanical substitution of one word for another. The translator must choose a Greek construction, decide how to express relationships made explicit or implicit in the source, and work within the resources of the target language. Some features of a translation may reflect regular habits rather than a deliberate theological alteration at each occurrence. Other choices may invite a stronger interpretive explanation. The relevant question is not whether interpretation is present, but what evidence justifies a particular account of the translator's decision. Compare similar expressions across the same translated book before assigning a unique significance to a single rendering.36

Nor is a literal or free label sufficient by itself. A translation can be close to its source in the sequence of clauses and less close in its treatment of idioms; it can reproduce some lexical distinctions consistently while expressing others in several ways. The researcher should say which feature is being measured, how frequently it occurs, and whether the comparison is based on a secure source text. A statistic without examples can conceal uncertainty about the Hebrew parent or a mixture of different translation practices. Conversely, a striking example should not stand in for a documented pattern. Combining quantitative observations with contextual examination yields a more useful account of translation technique.37

1.1 見證與重建

文本批判首先辨認實際保存的是甚麼。抄本是在特定傳遞階段見證經文的實物；現代校勘本則是編者根據現存見證作出的編輯成果。兩者都不等同作者親筆原稿。馬所拉文本是傳承下來的希伯來文文本傳統；「七十士譯本」可指由多部希臘譯本組成、後來又經歷複雜傳遞的文獻集合。各卷書的翻譯和抄傳歷史不盡相同，希臘文抄本既可能保存較早形式，也可能保存修訂。01

Vorlage 指譯者或抄寫者所使用的底本。底本有時本身保存於抄本中，但學者使用這個詞時，也可能指根據古譯本重建、如今不存的假設性原文。因此，必須清楚區分抄本可以觀察到的讀法，以及對其母本所作的推論。希臘譯文與中世紀希伯來文見證不同，可能源於不同的希伯來文底本，也可能反映譯者的理解或希臘語習慣、兩種傳統後來的傳抄改變，或多種因素共同作用。單靠一組對照，通常不足以判定哪種解釋正確。02

1.2 翻譯技巧與驗證界線

把希臘文讀法反向重建為希伯來文之前，應先研究該卷譯者在其他地方如何處理相同詞彙和句法。譯法持續貼近原文，與譯者自由改寫，提供的證據力度不同；即使某一組詞反覆對應，也不代表每個希臘詞只有一種可能的希伯來原詞。上下文、詞序、習語與希臘文本身的傳抄狀態都必須納入判斷。若獨立的古代希伯來文見證保存相近讀法，底本重建便有較強支持；但見證一致本身仍不證明兩份文獻直接互相抄寫。03

猶太曠野出土抄本直接證明，在中世紀大型經卷以前，希伯來經文已有不同形式，也保存與後期傳統相近的讀法。不能把這些證據簡化成「文本完全一致」，也不能反過來宣稱每個異文都同樣古老。現存抄本的年代與某一讀法形成的年代彼此相關，卻不是同一問題。不同文學版本、抄寫失誤及詮釋性修改，各自需要不同解釋；研究者應交代所提出的原因以及支持該判斷的證據。04

1.3 校勘本讓讀者看見甚麼

校勘本是研究工具，不是過去的透明照片。正文伴隨編輯判斷，許多版本亦提供列出部分異文的校勘註。校勘註有其界線：它未必記錄每個拼寫差異、引述所有抄本，或解釋每個見證完整的傳遞歷史。根據符號作結論以前，讀者必須查閱該版本的導論，了解縮寫與選錄原則。若校勘註沒有列出某份見證，應辨別這是編輯慣例、讀法不可取得，還是抄本實際狀況；不能未看符號表，便把一套版本的記號解釋直接套用到另一套。30

使用多個版本時，研究者也應區分其編輯目的。摹寫本按既定轉寫慣例呈現特定見證；折衷本則從多份見證選擇讀法。兩者各有價值，但都不能獨自交代所有抄本的歷史。教學時，可讓學生對照正文與校勘註，分開寫出編者印行的文字、指定見證實際保存的讀法，以及學生自己的推論。這也說明：同一份抄本即使見於兩個現代版本，也不能因此被當成兩個獨立見證。31

1.4 把翻譯看作古代的詮釋行動

翻譯不是把一個詞機械地替換為另一個詞。譯者必須選擇希臘文句式，決定如何表達原文中明示或隱含的關係，並在目標語言的資源之內工作。譯文某些特徵可能反映譯者的常規習慣，而不是每次都刻意作出神學改動；另一些選擇則可能需要更強的詮釋解釋。問題不只是有沒有詮釋，而是甚麼證據足以支持對某一翻譯決定的具體說明。在賦予單一譯法特殊意義以前，應先比較同卷書內相似表達的處理方式。36

「直譯」或「意譯」的標籤本身也不足夠。譯文可能在子句次序上貼近原文，處理習語時卻較自由；某些詞彙區分可能一貫保留，另一些則有多種譯法。研究者應指出正在量度甚麼特徵、出現頻率如何，以及比較是否建立於可靠的原文基礎上。沒有例子的統計可能掩蓋希伯來底本的不確定性或混合的翻譯習慣；反過來，一個醒目的例子也不能代替已經記錄的模式。將數量觀察與上下文分析結合，才能更有用地描述翻譯技巧。37

02 · COMPARISON 02 · 文本比較

A Controlled Method for Comparing Texts 受控的經文比較方法

● METHOD 方法

Record the evidence first; explain relationships and theological interpretation as separate judgments. 先記錄文本證據，再分別判斷文本關係與神學詮釋。

2.1 A controlled comparison

Begin with the later text in its own literary setting. Determine whether a writer introduces an earlier passage explicitly or whether a proposed relationship depends on less conspicuous linguistic parallels. Record the form of the later text and its variants before selecting an antecedent. An introductory formula can identify an act of quotation, but it does not establish which surviving manuscript the writer used.05

Next compare the Hebrew witness, relevant Greek witnesses and any other accessible ancient evidence in parallel. Note additions, omissions, word order and grammatical changes. If two readings agree, ask whether their agreement is distinctive or whether independent translation from a similar source could also explain it. If they differ, list possible causes before judging the author's intent. The surviving manuscripts postdate the composition of many biblical works; identifying a likely textual tradition is often more defensible than naming an exact lost copy.06

2.2 Worked comparison and limits

Isaiah 7:14 in the Masoretic tradition describes הָעַלְמָה, commonly rendered “the young woman.” Matthew 1:23 quotes a Greek formulation with ἡ παρθένος. The two forms are an invitation to careful comparison, not a licence to assign identical semantic ranges to Hebrew and Greek words. Compare each term in its own language and literary context; then examine the history and textual form of the Greek translation and Matthew's quotation. A translation choice cannot be reconstructed merely by consulting a modern dictionary gloss.07

A responsible report separates three questions: what each surviving text says, which textual relationships are likely, and what theological role the quotation plays in its new setting. Confidence can vary across these questions. It is possible to recognize an explicit citation while remaining uncertain whether the writer used a specific Greek manuscript, quoted from memory, or employed another form of the tradition. Publish the uncertainty rather than filling the gap with a hypothetical manuscript treated as fact.08

2.3 Reading both contexts without collapsing them

A comparison can be arranged in four passes. On the first pass, read the Hebrew passage with its immediate literary setting and describe the roles of speakers and addressees. On the second, read the Greek form as a Greek text before deciding why it differs. On the third, compare the later author's quotation or allusion with the forms available in the editions and witnesses actually consulted. On the fourth, analyze the function of the wording in the later argument. These stages are analytically distinct even though practical research may move back and forth among them. A finding at one stage may require revision of an earlier hypothesis, and a report should make that revision visible.32

In the Isaiah and Matthew example, begin by stating the limited observation already established: the respective texts use different words in different languages, and Matthew introduces a quotation. Do not assume that a difference between two modern translations represents an ancient textual variant; a difference can arise because the translators chose different English equivalents. Conversely, a shared English word can conceal a difference in the underlying languages. Only after the original texts are displayed can the reader ask which particular distinctions matter for the argument. Theological readings should attend to the surrounding narrative rather than demand that one lexical gloss carry the full weight of the interpretation.33

2.4 Documenting a comparison for review

A documented comparison allows a second researcher to reconstruct the first researcher's work. Record the passage reference, the edition and its date, and the reading adopted. For a manuscript, identify its siglum and whether the actual image, a transcription, or an edition's apparatus was consulted. For a version, identify the language and the edition representing it. If a reading is taken from a secondary source because a primary witness is inaccessible, say so explicitly. This provenance is not a substitute for analysis, but it determines what the reader can verify and which claims must remain provisional.38

A comparison table should avoid mixing observations and explanations in a single cell. Write “the Greek text has this phrase” separately from “the translator added this phrase.” The first is a report of an attested form; the second is a historical claim requiring comparison with plausible source texts and the translator's habits. Record uncertainties such as a damaged word or uncertain transcription in the row where they matter. When two scholars reach different conclusions, state whether the disagreement concerns the evidence itself, its relative weight, or the explanation of an agreed difference. This makes scholarly disagreement precise and prevents apparent disagreements caused by different questions from being exaggerated.39

2.1 受控的文本比較

首先在後期文本自己的文學脈絡中閱讀它。判斷作者是否明示引述較早的經文，還是所建議的關聯依賴較隱晦的語言相似處。選定前文之前，先記錄後期文本的形式及其異文。引文公式可以表明作者正在引用經文，但並不能證明作者使用了哪一份現存抄本。05

接著並列比較希伯來文見證、相關希臘文見證，以及其他可取得的古代證據；記錄增添、省略、詞序與語法變化。兩個讀法一致時，應問這個一致是否足夠獨特，抑或從相近來源獨立翻譯也能解釋它。兩者不同時，應先列出可能原因，然後才判斷作者意圖。現存抄本往往晚於聖經作品的形成；因此，指出一個可能的文本傳統，通常比斷定作者使用某一份已失傳的抄本更有根據。06

2.2 比較例子與推論界線

馬所拉傳統中的以賽亞書七章十四節使用 הָעַלְמָה，一般可譯作「年輕女子」；馬太福音一章二十三節的希臘文引文則使用 ἡ παρθένος。這兩個形式邀請讀者仔細比較，卻不允許我們假定希伯來文和希臘文詞彙的語義範圍完全相同。應先分別在各自語言及文學上下文中研究詞語，再考察希臘譯文和馬太引文的文本形式及其歷史。單查現代字典中的一個譯詞，不能重建古代譯者的選擇。07

審慎的報告應分開三個問題：各現存文本實際寫甚麼、哪些文本關係較可能，以及引文在新語境中有甚麼神學作用。對這三個問題的信心程度可以不同。即使明確認出一段引文，仍可能無法確知作者使用哪一份希臘文抄本、是否憑記憶引用，或採用了傳統的另一形式。研究者應公開交代不確定性，而不是把假設性的抄本當作既定事實。08

2.3 同時閱讀兩個上下文而不把它們混同

文本比較可分四次閱讀。第一次，在希伯來文段落直接的文學處境中閱讀，描述說話者與受話者的角色。第二次，先把希臘文形式作為希臘文本閱讀，然後才判斷差異的原因。第三次，根據實際查閱的版本和見證，比較後期作者的引文或典故。第四次，分析該措辭在後期論證中的作用。即使實際研究需要在各階段來回，分析上仍應把這些步驟分開；某一階段的新發現可能要求修訂先前假設，報告也應公開這種修訂。32

以以賽亞和馬太為例，先陳述已經確立的有限觀察：兩段不同語言的文本使用不同詞語，而馬太明示引述。不要假定兩個現代譯本的差異一定代表古代異文；差異也可能由現代譯者選擇不同譯詞造成。反過來，相同的中文或英文詞語，也可能掩蓋原文之間的差別。只有並列原文之後，讀者才能追問哪些差異真正影響論證。神學閱讀應重視周圍敘事，不能要求一個字典義獨自承擔全部詮釋重量。33

2.4 為同行檢驗記錄比較過程

有紀錄的比較讓第二位研究者可以重建第一位研究者的工作。應記錄經文位置、所用版本及其日期、採納的讀法。使用抄本時，指出其代號，並說明實際查閱的是影像、轉寫本，還是版本校勘註；使用古譯本時，指出語言及所用版本。若無法取得第一手見證，而從次級文獻得知某讀法，也應明言。來源紀錄不能取代分析，但它決定讀者能驗證甚麼，以及哪些結論仍屬暫定。38

比較表應避免把觀察與解釋混在同一格。「希臘文本有此短語」和「譯者加上此短語」必須分開：前者報告已見的形式，後者則是歷史主張，需要比較可能的原文和譯者習慣。不確定之處，例如文字殘損或轉寫有疑問，應記在直接相關的欄位。兩位學者意見不同時，應說明分歧究竟關於證據本身、證據的相對權重，還是對共同承認之差異的解釋。這能使學術分歧具體化，也避免把問題不同造成的表面分歧誇大。39

03 · ECHOES 03 · 經文回響

Testing Claims of Scriptural Echoes 檢驗經文回響的主張

● INTERTEXTUAL CAUTION 互涉研究的提醒

An attractive reading is a hypothesis until the textual and contextual evidence is assessed. 有啟發性的解讀仍是待檢驗的假說，必須評估文字及上下文證據。

3.1 Citation, allusion, and echo

An explicit citation normally draws attention to its source with a formula or other clear signal. An allusion invokes an earlier text without the same formal introduction; an echo may be less conspicuous still. These terms do not impose universal numerical thresholds. A phrase can be memorable yet common, while several unusual words in a comparable sequence may furnish stronger evidence. A reader must ask whether the suggested relationship explains the new passage more adequately than shared language or a different antecedent does.09

3.2 Seven questions for an alleged echo

Richard Hays's influential criteria, presented and adapted by G. K. Beale, give a structured way to investigate rather than a mechanical score. Availability asks whether the earlier text was accessible; volume concerns the prominence and distinctiveness of overlap; recurrence considers other uses of the same source; and thematic coherence asks how the suggested antecedent relates to the later argument.10

Historical plausibility examines whether the proposed relationship fits the author and audience; history of interpretation considers how others have read it; and satisfaction asks whether the proposal illuminates the passage. These questions are not independent votes. Thematic fit and satisfaction involve judgment, while an attractive interpretation can also become circular if it is used as the sole reason for identifying the echo. Disagreement should therefore be reported with the evidence that produces it.11

Distinguish the identification of an antecedent from the account of what an author does with it. A strong case for dependence does not settle whether the later writer preserves, extends, recontextualizes, or contests the earlier passage. Compare both contexts and consider competing readings before claiming that an entire earlier chapter has been imported into the later text.12

3.3 How to compare competing hypotheses

Imagine that a later author uses a phrase found in several earlier passages. A weak argument cites whichever passage best supports a conclusion and ignores the others. A stronger one lists the candidates and asks whether any has a distinctive combination of wording, sequence, imagery, and argumentative role. If two candidates remain plausible, the answer need not be forced into a false choice. The author may have drawn on a shared scriptural tradition, combined expressions, or used a phrase whose source cannot now be securely identified. The evidence may justify a stronger conclusion about a broad tradition than about a single literary source.34

Reader recognition is also a graded historical claim. A proposed connection may have been available to some readers yet inaccessible to others. The fact that an interpretation can be constructed by a modern reader does not alone show what every ancient audience recognized. Conversely, the absence of an explicit quotation formula does not mean an audience familiar with scripture could not hear an allusion. A careful report specifies whether it argues for authorial use, plausible audience recognition, a literary resonance discernible today, or a combination. These claims overlap but are not interchangeable.35

3.4 Criteria are questions, not an automatic verdict

The seven questions are especially useful when they reveal different strengths and weaknesses in the same proposal. An earlier text may have been available but share only a common word with the later text. Another proposal may have a distinctive verbal overlap yet fit the later argument less clearly. The interpreter should explain how the evidence works together rather than announcing that a proposed echo has passed a numerical test. The criteria also have differing types: availability is a historical condition, while thematic coherence is a judgment about literary function. Treating both as identical measurements obscures how a conclusion was reached.40

The history of interpretation requires similar care. A long-standing reading deserves investigation, but repetition does not by itself establish historical dependence. A new reading is not automatically weak because it is new, although its proponent should ask why earlier interpreters did not notice it. Satisfaction is likewise a check on explanatory value rather than permission to select the most pleasing interpretation. A compelling theological synthesis can still depend on a weak identification of a source. Conversely, a strong verbal connection may yield more than one responsible account of its theological significance. Separating these questions preserves both literary precision and interpretive openness.41

3.1 引文、典故與回響

明示引文通常透過引文公式或其他清楚標記，提醒讀者注意其來源。典故沒有同樣明確的介紹，卻指向較早文本；回響則可能更加細微。這些術語沒有普遍適用的固定字數門檻。一個短語即使令人印象深刻，也可能只是常用語；相反，多個不尋常詞語以相近次序出現，可能構成較強證據。研究者應問：所建議的關聯是否比共同語言習慣或另一個前文，更能解釋後期經文？09

3.2 檢驗回響的七個問題

海斯（Richard Hays）提出、並由畢爾（G. K. Beale）介紹及調整的著名準則，提供有結構的查問方式，而不是機械式計分表。「可取得性」詢問較早文本是否可供作者使用；「音量」關注語詞重疊的顯著性與獨特性；「重現」考察作者是否在其他地方使用同一來源；「主題一致性」則詢問所建議的前文如何連繫後期論證。10

「歷史可能性」檢查這個關聯對作者及讀者是否可行；「詮釋歷史」考察其他讀者如何理解；「滿足度」則問這項建議能否照亮經文。這些問題不是互相獨立的投票。主題一致與滿足度涉及判斷，若只因一個解讀吸引便認定存在回響，便可能陷入循環論證。因此，報告學者分歧時，也應交代引發分歧的證據。11

辨認前文與解釋作者如何使用前文，是兩個不同的判斷。即使文本依賴有強力證據，也不能自動決定後期作者是在保留、延伸、重新置入語境，還是在挑戰先前經文。宣稱整章舊文都被帶進後期文本以前，應比較兩者上下文，並認真考慮競爭性解讀。12

3.3 比較不同假設

假設後期作者使用的短語出現在數段較早經文。薄弱的論證只引用最符合結論的一段，忽略其他候選；較強的論證則列出各選項，詢問哪一段同時具有獨特的措辭、次序、意象和論證作用。若兩個候選仍都合理，不必強行作出虛假的二選一。作者可能使用共同的經文傳統、組合不同表達，或採用來源如今無法確定的短語。證據對廣泛傳統的指向，可能比對單一文學來源的指向更有力。34

讀者能否辨識也是有程度差別的歷史主張。某種連結可能為部分讀者所熟悉，其他人卻未必察覺。現代讀者能建構某個解釋，並不單憑這一點就能證明每位古代聽眾都認出它；反過來，沒有明示引文公式，也不代表熟悉經文的聽眾不可能聽出典故。審慎報告須說明所論證的是作者使用、聽眾可能辨識、今日可見的文學共鳴，還是其中數者。這些主張雖然重疊，卻不能互換。35

3.4 準則是問題，不是自動裁決

七個問題特別有用之處，是它們能顯示同一假設的不同強弱。一段較早文本或許可供作者使用，卻只與後期文本共享一個常見詞；另一項建議或許有獨特詞語重疊，卻較難融入後期論證。解經者應交代各項證據如何共同作用，而不是宣稱某回響通過了數值測試。各準則的性質亦不相同：可取得性是歷史條件，主題一致性則涉及文學作用的判斷；把兩者視為完全相同的測量，會掩蓋結論如何形成。40

詮釋歷史也要同樣小心。流傳已久的解讀值得研究，但反覆出現本身不能證明歷史依賴。新解讀不會單因新穎而自動薄弱，但提出者應問為何較早的解經者沒有注意到它。「滿足度」同樣是檢查解釋能力，而不是選擇最悅耳答案的許可。富有感染力的神學綜合仍可能依賴薄弱的來源辨認；反過來，即使詞語聯繫有力，其神學意義也可能容許多種負責任的說明。分開這些問題，既保存文學分析的精確，也保存詮釋的開放性。41

04 · TEXTUAL HISTORIES 04 · 文本歷史

Textual Histories and Competing Explanations 文本歷史與相互競爭的解釋

● EVIDENCE IN PRACTICE 證據的實踐

State the evidence, the inference, and its limits. 交代證據、推論及其界線。

4.1 Trace the witnesses before tracing influence

Textual history poses a question prior to literary influence: which forms of the texts are attested, and in which witnesses? A later reader comparing a modern Hebrew Bible with a modern Greek New Testament sees two edited texts. Each editor has selected and interpreted manuscript evidence; the two printed pages do not themselves display all of the ancient alternatives. A responsible comparison therefore records the editions, apparatus entries, manuscripts and versions actually consulted. This distinction protects the interpreter from silently treating a modern editorial decision as a reading known to an ancient author. The critic also notes which witnesses contain the relevant verse: absence owing to damage is not evidence that a manuscript omitted the verse.13

Hebrew consonantal texts, vocalized medieval manuscripts, Greek translations, and later translations have different evidential functions. A medieval vocalization can preserve a tradition of reading but should not be projected without argument onto every earlier manuscript. A Greek translation gives access to a translator's rendering of a source, but is itself transmitted in Greek witnesses with their own variants. An ancient version may be useful for reconstructing a Hebrew parent only after the critic has weighed its translation habits and transmission. There is no single rule that an older manuscript always preserves an older reading: a late copy can preserve an early reading, while an early copy can contain a recent error.14

4.2 Compare explanations rather than labels

Suppose a Greek witness adds a phrase absent from a Hebrew witness. Several accounts are possible. The translator may have made an explanatory addition; the translator may have read a longer Hebrew parent; the Greek phrase may have entered during subsequent copying; or the Hebrew tradition may have lost a phrase preserved elsewhere. The proposal that most economically accounts for the distribution of readings depends on the evidence available in that particular case. The mere fact that a version is Greek does not prove it preserves a different Hebrew text. Equally, the fact that a reading appears in one Hebrew witness does not establish it as the only ancient form.15

Work through the alternatives in a table. The first column describes the observed reading and witness. The second records the date and character of the witness as far as established. The third names the explanation being proposed; the fourth states the evidence that could count against it. This last column prevents the reconstruction from becoming immune to correction. An independent Hebrew fragment agreeing with a Greek reading can strengthen the case for a shared Semitic form, but the relationship of those witnesses still needs argument. If a fragment is too damaged to preserve the decisive word, mark that limitation instead of counting it as agreement or disagreement.16

For a classroom exercise, have two readers independently identify the exact point at which a description of a witness becomes a historical reconstruction. Then ask each to state the minimum claim that remains secure if the favored reconstruction fails. This exercise separates observations, explanatory models, and theological interpretations. Such separation is not skepticism toward the texts; it makes the interpreter's reasons available for scrutiny and allows a good argument to survive correction at a different level of inquiry.17

4.3 A specific problem of translation and textual history

McLay's discussion of Amos 9:12 and its use in Acts 15 illustrates how an explanatory judgment is constructed. He notes differences between a Hebrew reading about possessing the remnant of Edom and a Greek formulation about the remaining people seeking. The OpenGNT text of Acts 15:17 reads οἱ κατάλοιποι τῶν ἀνθρώπων in a clause about seeking the Lord. These observations do not establish, by themselves, whether every difference arose from a different Hebrew parent, the translator's reading of consonants, translation choices, or later transmission. McLay argues that the Greek rendering can be closely related to the Hebrew form rather than requiring a wholly different Vorlage. That is an argument to examine against the witnesses, not a finding automatically settled for every word.28

Notice how a change of grammatical role matters: the remnant can function differently in the compared constructions, so identifying a common equivalent for a single word does not by itself explain the whole clause. A detailed comparison asks what happens to the subject, object, verb, and neighboring phrases. It also separates the form of the Greek biblical text from the form used in Acts, which must be checked in each textual tradition. The example illustrates the burden of proof for a retroversion: a proposed Hebrew form needs positive reasons and must be compared with what is known about the translator's work and surviving witnesses. These observations are an introduction to the comparison and do not substitute for a collation of the relevant Hebrew and Greek witnesses.29

4.4 A disciplined disagreement about witnesses

Imagine two scholars examining the same Greek and Hebrew readings. Both agree that the Greek text differs, but one attributes the divergence to a translator's choice while the other proposes a different Hebrew parent. They may be disagreeing about the probability of particular translation habits, not about whether the Greek words exist. To assess their arguments, isolate each proposed step. Does the first explanation require a rendering attested elsewhere in the translator's work? Does the second require a Hebrew expression for which independent evidence is available? Can either explanation account for the grammar of the whole clause rather than just an isolated word? An argument gains strength when it accommodates the details without multiplying unsupported assumptions.42

Neither model is necessarily refuted because it cannot be proved with certainty. Ancient evidence is often fragmentary, and the relevant source text may not survive. Distinguish a demonstrated reading from the best available explanation of that reading and from a speculative possibility. Explain why a preferred reconstruction is more plausible than its rivals and state which missing evidence could change the balance. A disciplined report may conclude that the evidence is insufficient to decide. Such a conclusion narrows the question accurately and protects subsequent theological work from an imagined certainty.43

When quoting a scholar, identify the scope of that scholar's claim. A proposal about one passage cannot automatically become a rule about all translators or all scriptural books. Different books exhibit different translation practices and have different histories of transmission. What counts as a strong inference in one case therefore needs fresh evaluation in another. Record the distinction between the scholar's stated conclusion and the present writer's independent application of it.44

4.1 在追查影響以前先追查見證

文本歷史首先提出一個先於文學影響的問題：各種文本形式實際保存於哪些見證之中？今天讀者比較現代希伯來文聖經和希臘文新約時，看到的是兩套經過編輯的文本。編者已經選擇並評估抄本證據；印刷頁面本身不會展示所有古代異文。審慎的比較因此應記錄實際使用的版本、校勘註、抄本及古譯本，避免不知不覺把現代編者的判斷當成古代作者必然讀過的文字。也須留意相關節次是否實際保存：抄本殘損而缺頁，不能證明它原來省略了該節。13

希伯來文輔音文本、中世紀加上元音的抄本、希臘譯本與後期譯本，各有不同的證據作用。中世紀元音可能保存一種誦讀傳統，但不可不加論證便投射到所有更早的抄本。希臘譯文可見譯者如何理解底本；然而希臘文自身亦經過抄傳，各見證之間可能有異文。利用古譯本重建希伯來文底本之前，必須先評估譯者習慣和譯本的傳抄歷史。抄本年代較早不必然證明某讀法較早：晚期抄本可能保留古老讀法，早期抄本也可能出現新近的錯誤。14

4.2 比較解釋，而不是只貼標籤

假設一份希臘文見證比一份希伯來文見證多出一個短語，可能的解釋不止一種。譯者可能為了說明而加字；譯者所讀的希伯來底本可能較長；希臘文短語可能在後來抄寫時加入；也可能是希伯來文傳統遺失了其他見證仍保存的短語。哪個解釋最能說明各讀法的分布，要按個案證據判斷。譯本是希臘文，並不自動證明它反映不同的希伯來文本；同樣，某讀法見於一份希伯來文抄本，也不證明那是古代唯一的形式。15

可用表格逐一檢驗各種假設：第一欄記錄觀察到的讀法和見證；第二欄記錄已知的抄本年代及性質；第三欄寫出所提出的解釋；第四欄寫出甚麼證據會削弱該解釋。最後一欄可防止重建變成無法修正的主張。若獨立希伯來文殘卷與希臘文讀法一致，確可加強共同閃語文本的可能性，但兩份見證的具體關係仍須論證。若殘卷恰好缺失關鍵字，應註明證據不足，不能把它算作支持或反對。16

課堂練習可讓兩位讀者獨立標出一段論證何時由描述見證轉成歷史重建，再要求各人說明：即使最喜歡的重建失敗，仍能確定甚麼最低限度的主張？此法區分觀察、解釋模型及神學詮釋。這並非懷疑文本，而是讓解經者公開理由，使論證的不同層次都能接受檢驗與修正。17

4.3 翻譯與文本歷史的具體問題

麥雷討論阿摩司書九章十二節及使徒行傳十五章的使用方式，有助說明解釋判斷如何形成。他指出，一種希伯來文讀法涉及得著以東餘民，希臘文表達則談及餘剩的人尋求。OpenGNT 所載使徒行傳十五章十七節在尋求主的子句中使用 οἱ κατάλοιποι τῶν ἀνθρώπων。單靠這些觀察，仍無法證明每一處差異究竟來自不同希伯來文底本、譯者對輔音的讀法、翻譯選擇，還是後來的抄傳。麥雷認為希臘譯文可以與希伯來文本密切相關，未必需要一個完全不同的底本。這個論證仍應對照見證檢驗，不代表每個字的問題都已自動解決。28

留意語法角色變化的重要性：所比較的句子中，「餘剩」一詞可有不同作用，因此只為某個字找到常見對應詞，並不足以解釋整個子句。詳細比較須問主語、賓語、動詞與附近短語如何改變，也要分辨希臘文聖經的形式和使徒行傳所使用的形式，並在各自文本傳統中查驗。這例子說明反向重建的舉證責任：提出希伯來文形式需要正面理由，也須與譯者的已知習慣及現存見證比較。這些觀察只是比較的入門，不能代替對相關希伯來文與希臘文見證的校對。29

4.4 如何有紀律地處理見證分歧

假設兩位學者研究同一組希臘文與希伯來文讀法。他們都承認希臘文不同；一人認為差異來自譯者選擇，另一人則提出不同的希伯來底本。雙方可能爭論的是特定翻譯習慣的可能性，而不是希臘字詞是否存在。評估時應拆開每個推論：第一種解釋所要求的譯法，是否見於同一譯者的其他作品？第二種解釋所要求的希伯來文表達，有沒有獨立證據？哪一種可以解釋整個子句的語法，而不是只解釋孤立詞語？能夠照顧細節而不增加無根據假設的論證，通常較有力。42

無法達到絕對確定，不代表任何一個模型必然被推翻。古代證據常常殘缺，相關底本也可能不存。應區分已證明的讀法、目前對讀法最合理的解釋，以及純粹推測的可能性；說明首選重建為何勝過其他解釋，也交代甚麼缺失證據可能改變判斷。嚴謹報告可以得出證據不足以決定的結論，這是在準確界定問題，也避免後續神學工作建立於想像的確定性上。43

引用學者時應標明其主張範圍。關於一段經文的建議，不能自動變成所有譯者或所有書卷的規則。不同書卷有不同翻譯習慣及傳抄歷史，因此在一個個案成立的強力推論，放到另一個個案仍須重新評估。報告要區分學者原來的結論和本研究者另作的應用。44

05 · TYPOLOGY AND CONTEXT 05 · 預表與上下文

Typology, Context, and Canonical Claims 預表、上下文與正典主張

● EVIDENCE IN PRACTICE 證據的實踐

State the evidence, the inference, and its limits. 交代證據、推論及其界線。

5.1 From resemblance to argued correspondence

Typological interpretation proposes a significant correspondence between an earlier event, institution, person, or pattern and a later one. The fact that two narratives share an image does not by itself establish that the later author intended a typological relationship. The interpreter should identify the proposed points of comparison, the relevant textual signals, and the differences that resist the analogy. A correspondence is stronger where distinctive verbal or structural connections, literary context, and the later work's own use of scriptural tradition mutually support the proposal. It is weaker when a broad motif is treated as a unique marker of dependence.18

Historical repetition and theological development must be argued rather than assumed. A later account may draw on an earlier pattern in a way that changes its setting, participants, and implications. The critic asks what each text does in its own argument before formulating a broader canonical claim. Earlier texts are not merely containers of predictions waiting to be decoded; they have literary and historical settings in which their words function. Nor should a later text be reduced to a duplicate of an earlier one. Each stage of the proposed relationship needs its own analysis.19

5.2 Context on both sides of an allusion

A useful two-column exercise places the immediate context of the alleged source beside the immediate context of the later passage. Highlight exact lexical overlap separately from shared themes. Then write a sentence explaining the role of the disputed phrase in each context. If the proposed antecedent concerns judgment while the later argument concerns restoration, the difference may be significant; it must not be erased simply to make a neat correspondence. A persuasive reading explains both continuity and transformation and identifies where interpretive judgment begins.20

Metalepsis is often invoked when a brief expression is thought to evoke a wider literary context. It should be treated as a proposal about what readers may reasonably be expected to recognize, not as a permit to import everything in the earlier book. The scope of the evoked context can be narrower or wider depending on textual cues and literary function. When a proposed reading depends on the entire earlier passage, the author must explain why that context rather than a different one is relevant and how it affects the new argument. Failure to specify the scope makes an allusion difficult to test.21

Report rival explanations fairly. A phrase may reflect a common scriptural idiom, a shared liturgical tradition, a specific written antecedent, or independent use of a familiar theme. These possibilities are not identical, and a common tradition does not automatically exclude a particular literary connection. An account that identifies a biblical source should explain why that source is more plausible than the alternatives. An account that denies a direct link should also explain any unusual convergence of vocabulary and argument. The goal is an evidence-sensitive judgment with a stated degree of confidence.22

5.3 The role of difference in a theological comparison

Comparison is sometimes presented as a search for matching features alone. Differences can be equally informative. If two scenes share a deliverance motif but place agency, audience, or outcome differently, the contrast may explain how a later writer adapts inherited language to a new argument. The interpreter should not suppress inconvenient details as exceptions, nor use every difference to rule out any meaningful relationship. The relevant question is whether similarities and differences together form a coherent account of the later passage. Explain why a particular feature matters to the text, rather than attaching equal weight to every possible resemblance.45

Scholars differ over whether typology is principally an analogy perceived in retrospect or also involves prospective significance in the earlier text. Beale discusses this debate and argues for a theological account that includes correspondence and escalation. A student need not settle the entire debate before reporting what a given argument requires. Does the proposal claim only that a later writer identifies a parallel? Does it additionally claim that the earlier author anticipated a later fulfillment? Those are different historical and theological claims, and they require different kinds of evidence. Naming the difference enables readers to evaluate the proposal without confusing a canonical conclusion with an independently established account of an earlier author's intention.46

In a seminar, assign one reader to formulate the strongest affirmative case for a proposed relationship and another to articulate the best alternative. Both should use the same textual evidence, acknowledge points favorable to the other account, and specify a potential defeater. The goal is not to manufacture controversy but to expose the inferential steps on which the conclusion depends. Afterward, the group can describe the range of reasonable conclusions and explain why it prefers one.47

5.1 從相似走向有論證的對應

預表詮釋主張，較早的事件、制度、人物或模式，與較後者之間存在具有神學意義的對應。兩個敘事使用相似意象，本身並不足以證明後期作者有意建立預表關係。解經者需要指出具體對應之處、相關文本線索，以及不符合類比的差異。若獨特的詞語或結構、文學脈絡與後期作品本身使用經文傳統的方式互相支持，對應便較有說服力；若把寬泛主題當作獨有的依賴證據，論證便較薄弱。18

歷史重現和神學發展必須論證，不能預設。後期敘事可能採用較早模式，卻改變場景、人物和含義。提出較廣泛的正典主張之前，研究者應先問每段文本在自己的論證中有甚麼作用。較早文本不只是等待後人解碼的預言容器；它有自身的文學與歷史處境。同樣，後期文本也不應縮減成較早文本的複製品。所主張的每一個關係階段，都需要個別分析。19

5.2 典故兩端的上下文

一個有用的雙欄練習，是把所建議來源的直接上下文與後期經文的直接上下文並列。精確相同的詞句和共同主題應分開標示，然後各用一句說明有爭議的短語在兩段文本中如何發揮作用。若建議來源關注審判，而後期論證關注復興，這個差異可能重要；不能為了整齊的對應而抹去。具有說服力的解讀既解釋延續，也解釋轉化，並指出何處開始涉及詮釋判斷。20

「轉喻式引述」常用來描述一個簡短表達被認為喚起較廣的文學上下文。這應理解為關於讀者合理辨識能力的假設，不是把較早書卷所有內容帶進來的通行證。受喚起的上下文可以較窄或較廣，取決於文本線索及其文學作用。若解讀依賴整段較早經文，就必須說明為何相關的是這個上下文而非另一個，以及它如何影響新的論證。沒有界定範圍，典故便難以檢驗。21

應公平交代競爭性的解釋。一個短語可能反映常見的經文用語、共同禮儀傳統、特定的書面前文，或獨立運用熟悉主題。這些可能性並不相同；共同傳統也不自動排除特定文學聯繫。主張某一經文為來源的人，應解釋它為何比其他候選者更合理；否認直接聯繫的人，也應解釋不尋常的詞語與論證匯合。目的是提出符合證據、並明示信心程度的判斷。22

5.3 差異在神學比較中的作用

比較有時被說成只是尋找相同特徵；其實差異也可能同樣重要。兩個場景若同有拯救主題，卻以不同方式安排行動者、對象或結果，差異便可能說明後期作者如何調整既有語言以服務新的論證。解經者不應把不方便的細節壓成例外，也不應以任何差異排除所有有意義的關係。真正的問題是：相同與不同之處能否共同構成對後期文本的連貫說明？應解釋某個特徵為何對文本重要，而不是給所有可能的相似處同等權重。45

學者對預表究竟主要是後見的類比，還是也涉及較早文本的前瞻意義，存在分歧。畢爾討論這個爭論，並提出包含對應與提升的神學解釋。學生毋須先解決整場辯論，才能報告某個論證實際要求甚麼：它只主張後期作者辨認出平行，還是進一步主張早期作者預期後來應驗？這是不同的歷史與神學主張，需要不同證據。清楚命名差異，可以避免把正典結論與對早期作者意圖的獨立歷史判斷混為一談。46

研討課可分派一位讀者提出某關係最強的肯定論證，另一位提出最合理的替代解釋。雙方應使用相同的文本證據，承認對對方有利的地方，並指出可能推翻自己主張的證據。目的不是製造爭論，而是顯明結論依賴哪些推論步驟；之後小組可描述合理結論的範圍，並說明為何偏向其中一種。47

06 · AUDIT AND SYNTHESIS 06 · 審核與綜合

A Reproducible Evidence Audit 可重複檢驗的證據審核

● EVIDENCE IN PRACTICE 證據的實踐

State the evidence, the inference, and its limits. 交代證據、推論及其界線。

6.1 Build an evidence ledger

A research report can be concise without concealing the work on which it depends. Begin with the textual claim: reproduce the relevant words, identify the edition and witness, and indicate whether a passage is quoted, alluded to, or merely compared. Then state the proposed relationship in a sentence whose strength matches the evidence. “The writer explicitly introduces this quotation” is a different claim from “the writer used a particular surviving manuscript,” and neither automatically decides how the earlier context functions in the new argument.23

In a second column identify each inference: the proposed parent text, the identity of the antecedent, the direction of influence, the interpretive function of the reference, and any canonical theological conclusion. Mark what directly attested evidence supports each one. In a third column record uncertainties: textual variants, the possibility of a common idiom, competing antecedents, or unavailable witnesses. Finally, record a plausible alternative and the observation that would distinguish it from the preferred account. The resulting ledger enables another reader to reproduce the reasoning rather than merely accept a conclusion.24

6.2 A sequence for teaching and research

First read the later passage continuously. Describe its grammar, structure, and argument before searching for parallels. Next identify possible verbal and thematic links, including those suggested by commentaries. Compare candidates against the original-language evidence and assess availability, distinctiveness, recurrence, and contextual fit. Then read the earlier text in its own setting. Ask whether a relationship is evident, probable, possible, or insufficiently supported. Use such labels as explanations of the argument, not as substitutes for it.25

Only after the initial comparison should the interpreter investigate manuscript relationships and translation technique in detail. In some cases textual evidence will alter the initial hypothesis; in others it will show that certainty about an exact Vorlage is unavailable. The researcher should distinguish the conclusions that do not depend on a contested reading from those that do. This protects a useful literary observation from being made unnecessarily dependent on an uncertain reconstruction. Conversely, a theological conclusion should not be stated more confidently than the text-critical and contextual argument allows.26

Finally formulate the theological synthesis in language that preserves the particularity of both contexts. Identify what the later author does with the earlier words, how that use contributes to the local argument, and where scholars dispute the scope or significance of the relationship. Cite the sources actually consulted, provide exact locators when they are verified, and distinguish a primary witness from a modern scholar's reconstruction. The aim is accountable interpretation: readers should be able to tell what was observed, what was inferred, and why the inference matters.27

6.3 Turning the ledger into a publishable argument

A final report should begin with a claim whose wording expresses its evidential strength. An explicit quotation permits a stronger identification than a possible echo with only generic imagery. Next give the evidence in an order a reader can follow: the later wording and context, the earlier candidate and context, distinctive connections, relevant textual variants, and competing accounts. Distinguish direct access to a witness from a report in a commentary. If the conclusion depends on a reconstructed Vorlage, show the reasoning rather than presenting that reconstruction as an observed manuscript.48

Footnotes should carry identifiable sources for claims that require support. An accurate bibliography entry is necessary but insufficient: the cited location must also address the specific claim. A note that establishes the existence of a manuscript is not automatically evidence for the author's use of it. Similarly, an authority's discussion of echo criteria does not independently prove a particular echo. Prefer a transparent note with a verified broader chapter reference over a precise page number whose accuracy is uncertain. Mark unresolved checks in working drafts and remove or substantiate them before publication.49

For teaching, ask a peer to reproduce the analysis using only the stated evidence. If the peer cannot tell which edition supplied a reading, whether a phrase is attested or reconstructed, or why another antecedent was rejected, revise the report. The value of the audit is not a display of caution for its own sake. It helps readers locate the actual disagreement, test a claim, and build on the work without confusing its firm observations with its provisional explanations. Those habits connect textual criticism, intertextual analysis, and theological synthesis in a transparent chain of reasoning.50

6.1 建立證據紀錄表

研究報告可以簡潔，卻不應隱藏結論所依賴的工作。首先陳述文本主張：列出相關文字，標明版本和見證，指出這是明示引文、典故，還是只作比較。接著用與證據相稱的語氣說明所建議的關係。「作者明示引述這段經文」不同於「作者使用某份現存抄本」；兩者都不自動決定較早上下文如何在新論證中發揮作用。23

第二欄逐項寫出推論：所重建的底本、前文的身分、影響方向、引用的詮釋作用，以及任何正典神學結論。標出每個推論得到哪些直接證據支持。第三欄記錄不確定之處，例如異文、共同慣用語的可能性、競爭性的前文，或未能取得的見證。最後記錄一個合理替代解釋，以及甚麼觀察可以區分它與首選解釋。這樣的紀錄表讓其他讀者可以重複檢驗推理，而不是只能接受結論。24

6.2 教學與研究的次序

首先連續閱讀後期經文，在搜尋平行文本之前描述其語法、結構與論證。接著辨認可能的詞語和主題連結，包括註釋書提出的候選；根據原文證據比較各選項，評估可取得性、獨特性、重現及上下文的一致程度。然後在較早文本自己的處境中閱讀它，判斷關係是明確、很可能、可能，還是證據不足。這些標籤應總結理由，不能取代理由。25

初步比較以後，解經者才詳細研究抄本關係與翻譯技巧。有時文本證據會改變原先假設；有時則顯示無法確知精確底本。研究者應區分哪些結論不依賴有爭議的讀法，哪些卻依賴它。這可避免把有價值的文學觀察，不必要地繫於不確定的重建；反過來，神學結論的確定性也不應超過文本批判與上下文論證所允許的程度。26

最後以保留兩個上下文特殊性的語言提出神學綜合。指出後期作者如何使用較早文字、這種用法如何推動本段論證，以及學者在關係範圍或意義上有甚麼分歧。引用實際查閱的來源，提供已核實的準確位置，並區分第一手見證與現代學者的重建。目的是可負責的詮釋：讀者應能看出甚麼是觀察、甚麼是推論，以及推論為何重要。27

6.3 將證據紀錄變成可發表的論證

最終報告應以語氣符合證據力度的主張開始。明示引文容許的辨認確定性，高於只靠一般意象提出的可能回響。接著按讀者容易追蹤的次序提供證據：後期文字及上下文、候選前文及上下文、獨特連結、相關異文和競爭解釋。應分辨直接查閱見證與從註釋書得知的報告；若結論依賴重建底本，必須展示推理，不可把重建當作現存抄本。48

註腳應為需要支持的主張提供可辨識來源。準確的書目資料是必要條件，卻仍不足夠；所引位置還須實際處理該主張。證明某抄本存在的註腳，並不自動證明作者使用過它。同樣，權威學者討論回響準則，也不會獨立證明某個具體回響。若精確頁碼未核實，寧可透明標明已核實的較廣章節，也不要給出似乎精確卻不可靠的位置。工作稿中的待查事項須在發表前查證或刪除。49

教學時可請同儕只根據文中交代的證據重做分析。若同儕無法判斷某讀法來自哪個版本、某短語是實際見證還是重建，或為何排除另一個前文，報告便須修訂。審核的價值並不是為了展示謹慎，而是幫助讀者找出真正分歧、檢驗主張，並在既有工作上繼續研究，而不把確定觀察與暫定解釋混同。這些習慣使文本批判、互涉分析和神學綜合形成透明的推理鏈。50
