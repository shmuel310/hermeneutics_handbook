01 · PERICOPAL SYNTHESIS 01 · 經文神學綜合

A proposition is an accountable reading 經文神學命題是可以接受檢驗的詮釋

A pericopal proposition is a provisional sentence that expresses the principal claim or movement of a defined unit. It is an interpretation, not an additional verse. Its usefulness lies in making the interpreter's judgment visible: another reader can ask whether its subject, action, grounds, and consequences actually correspond to the passage. A proposition that is eloquent but does not account for the passage's distinctive details has failed as an interpretation.01

Begin with boundaries. A paragraph break in a modern translation is useful evidence, but it is not decisive. Look for changes of speaker, participants, setting, topic, form, and conjunctions, and then test the proposed boundary against what precedes and follows. A passage may complete an earlier argument while also preparing a later one; its role as a transition should remain visible in the eventual sentence. The scope of a proposition must match the scope of its evidence. A claim about an entire book cannot be established merely by observing one paragraph.

The distinction between a passage's claims and its communicative force can clarify the work. An assertion, command, lament, and question do different things, even when they concern the same subject. Yet a narrative need not have a single easily recoverable imperative. Speech-act categories can prompt questions about what a text does, but they do not independently prove one historical intention. Compare possibilities and show the features on which the judgment rests.

A proposition begins with a question that the selected unit can actually address. David Downs recommends beginning research with close reading and a focused question, then using a preliminary survey of critical scholarship to discover additional issues. These steps allow an investigator to revise an initial hunch when it proves insufficient. For a passage-level proposition, the analogy is useful: first identify the interpretive problem, and only then compress an answer. A question such as “How does the writer move from diagnosis to instruction?” directs attention to relationships within a unit; it should not be confused with a claim already established.02

Questions and propositions are not interchangeable. A question opens a problem to investigation; a proposition gives an answer for others to evaluate. The latter needs evidence, not merely an attractive phrase or a list of prestigious names. A text-specific answer can be challenged by a counterexample within the same paragraph: a neglected qualification, an actor assigned the wrong action, or a change in voice may require a revision. The task is not to produce an unassailable slogan but to make the reasons and the limits of a reading explicit.03

Research resources should answer the question being asked. Downs distinguishes the role of a lexical reference work from a simple word-finding aid and emphasizes selecting sources appropriate to the research problem. For synthesis this distinction prevents a frequent shortcut: finding every occurrence of a term does not itself establish what the term contributes to one sentence. A concordance can locate passages for comparison; an argument about meaning must also consider how a word functions within its clause and discourse. When readers disagree, the precise construction and proposed alternatives should be stated.04

A working proposition should remain open during the research-and-writing process. Downs describes research and writing as a spiral: a draft may expose a gap in the evidence, leading to additional study and a revised outline. This does not require endless hesitation or make every hypothesis equally plausible. It means that provisional confidence should track the evidence available. Record what has been checked, what has been inferred, and what still needs examination; a reader can then see whether the next revision concerns a small detail or the organizing claim itself.05

A proposition also differs from the complete argument for it. In a research paper, the thesis announces what the writer will defend; the body must demonstrate the reading through primary evidence and engage relevant alternatives. The distinction matters for oral teaching as well. Repeating a sentence with increasing confidence does not establish it. A teacher should be able to show the observations, explain how they relate, and acknowledge evidence that requires qualification. This makes a concise formulation the doorway to an argument rather than an exemption from argument.06

經文神學命題嘗試用一句話表達一個有明確界限的段落之主要意思或推進方向。這句話是詮釋者的判斷，並非聖經另加的一節。它的價值在於把判斷公開：其他讀者可以檢查句子的主詞、行動、理由與結果，是否真正反映經文。如果一句話聽來動人，卻無法交代段落的具體內容，就不足以成為可靠的摘要。01

首先要判斷段落界限。現代譯本的分段可以提供線索，卻不能單獨決定界限。讀者應留意說話者、人物、場景、主題、文體以及連接詞的變化，再將暫定界限放回上下文檢驗。某一段可能既總結前文，又為後文作準備；最後的命題應容許這種過渡功能顯現。命題的範圍也必須與證據相稱：不能只憑一段經文，就斷言全書的宗旨。

區分段落所說的內容與它所施行的溝通作用，也能幫助解釋。陳述、命令、哀歌與問題即使涉及同一主題，也可能產生不同作用。但敘事不一定包含一項容易直接辨認的命令。言語行為的分類可以幫助提出問題，卻不能獨立證明某個歷史意圖。讀者應比較不同可能，並說明判斷所依據的經文特徵。

命題首先要從所選段落真正能回答的問題出發。David Downs 建議研究者先仔細閱讀文本並提出聚焦的問題，再初步查閱批判性學術著作，以發現其他值得研究的議題。若最初的想法不足以解釋證據，就應修訂。這個程序也有助於經文命題：先辨認詮釋問題，再把答案濃縮。「作者如何從處境的診斷轉向指示？」這類問題能引導讀者注意段落內部的關係，但不能誤當作已經證明的結論。02

問題和命題不能互換。問題把某個難題開放給研究；命題則提供一個可以受別人評估的答案。後者需要證據，而不是動聽的短句或知名學者名單。同一段中的反例可能挑戰命題：被忽略的限定語、行動主詞指派錯誤，或語態的改變，都可能要求修改。目的不是製造不可質疑的口號，而是清楚呈現解釋的理由及其限制。03

研究工具應適合所提出的問題。Downs 區分詞典等參考工具與單純搜尋字詞的工具，並強調應按研究問題選擇材料。這個區分有助於避免常見捷徑：找出某個詞所有出現的位置，本身並不能確立它在某一句中的作用。經文彙編可以幫助尋找比較材料；討論意思時，仍須考慮字詞在子句及整個語篇中的功能。若讀者有不同意見，就應說明具體句法與其他可能解釋。04

工作中的命題在研究與寫作期間應保持開放。Downs 把研究與寫作描述為螺旋：草稿可能暴露證據的缺口，促使研究者再作查考，並修訂原有大綱。這並不要求永遠猶疑，也不代表所有假設同樣可信；它的意思是，暫時的信心應與目前掌握的證據相稱。記下哪些已經核對、哪些是推論、哪些仍待研究，讀者便能看見下一次修改涉及的是小細節，還是整個組織性的主張。05

命題也不同於支持它的完整論證。研究論文中的論題說明作者要論證甚麼；正文則必須透過主要文本證據證明該讀法，並處理相關的其他解釋。這個區分對口頭教學同樣重要：即使越來越有信心地重複一句話，也不能證明它。教師應能指出具體觀察、解釋彼此的關係，並承認需要限制結論的證據。這樣，精簡命題才是進入論證的入口，而不是免除論證的藉口。06

Trace movement before compressing it 先追蹤推進，再濃縮命題

Write a sequence of short observations before writing the final sentence. Who acts? What changes? What reasons or consequences are supplied? Which clauses are dependent? A discourse marker can indicate a relation without mechanically determining all its nuances. The resulting sequence should be revisable: if a proposed transition ignores a verse or requires a repeated word to bear the same sense in every occurrence, return to the passage.

Matthew 9:35–38 offers a compact example of this procedure. The integrated Greek text presents Jesus going through cities and villages, with participles for teaching, proclaiming, and healing (v. 35). He sees the crowds and is moved with compassion because they are like sheep without a shepherd (v. 36). He speaks to his disciples of a plentiful harvest and few workers (v. 37), then commands them to ask the Lord of the harvest to send workers (v. 38). These observations provide a movement from Jesus' ministry, through his response to the crowds, to his instruction to the disciples. The neighbouring commissioning in chapter 10 invites a further inquiry into how prayer and mission relate; it should be examined in its own terms rather than assumed to mean that every reader automatically occupies the same narrative role as the Twelve.07

A candidate synthesis is: Seeing the crowds' need, Jesus directs his disciples to ask the Lord of the harvest for workers. Its strength is its close correspondence to the transition from compassion to instruction. Its limitation is that it compresses the teaching, proclamation, and healing of verse 35 into the surrounding account. A fuller formulation could include Jesus' ministry, while still identifying prayer as the explicit instruction. Neither version should be treated as the only possible formulation; compare their ability to account for the passage and its context.

Before following a passage’s movement, ask which text is being read. McLay distinguishes an eclectic edition, constructed through comparison of witnesses, from a diplomatic presentation of a particular witness. An edited text is therefore an argued scholarly reconstruction rather than a photograph of an author’s manuscript. For a classroom proposition, identify the edition and translation used; if a variant affects the line of thought, the interpreter should explain that effect instead of allowing an editorial decision to disappear from view.08

Textual difference does not automatically settle why two forms differ. McLay’s discussion of Hebrew and Greek witnesses distinguishes the observable difference from competing explanations involving interpretation by a translator, a different source text, or later transmission. A theological summary should preserve that distinction. One may describe what a surviving witness says while marking the history behind it as a hypothesis, and different hypotheses may have different consequences for the argument about literary development.09

A translator’s repeated practice can help evaluate an individual rendering. A single unusual correspondence may be accidental or reflect a contextual decision; a pattern across a book can provide a stronger basis for comparison. McLay’s treatment of translation technique encourages attention to the translator’s procedures rather than immediate reconstruction of a different Hebrew text. The analyst must still ask whether the passage under study belongs to the same textual and translation context as the examples used for comparison.10

An explicit introductory formula and a possible echo are not equally strong kinds of evidence. McLay limits the introductory analysis in his book mainly to explicit citations, in order to make the textual relationships easier to examine. An interpreter may reasonably investigate subtler echoes as well, but should explain the additional criteria used and identify the point at which a claim changes from direct citation to inferred allusion. That distinction can be maintained without denying theological connections between passages.11

McLay’s comparison of the Greek and Hebrew forms of Amos illustrates the need to keep evidence and explanation apart. He considers whether a difference is best explained by another source text or by the translator’s reading of a Hebrew form. In using this as a methodological example, the theological interpreter should attribute the proposed explanation to McLay and examine the witnesses before claiming independent confirmation. A plausible account of one textual difference does not automatically explain every difference in the quotation.12

The theological significance of a quotation should be considered after the relationship among the relevant textual forms has been described. This order is a safeguard rather than a claim that theological interests can be eliminated from research. An interpreter may have a preliminary theological question at the outset, but should be willing to adjust the proposed answer if the witnesses do not support the assumed wording. A transparent synthesis identifies what the textual comparison establishes and what remains an interpretive extension.13

A canonical proposition may depend on a typological reading, but scholars do not define typology identically. Beale describes a debate over whether typology is essentially analogy or also entails an element of foreshadowing. These alternatives have different implications for the earlier text and the later theological claim. A writer should identify the working definition rather than assuming that every reader accepts the same account of the relationship.14

In Beale’s formulation, analogical correspondence is one feature of typology. Establishing a resemblance, however, is only a starting point within that model, which also includes other characteristics. An interpreter should specify the persons, events, or institutions being compared and the relevant points of resemblance. Theological synthesis becomes more transparent when it also identifies important differences rather than treating the earlier and later realities as interchangeable.15

Beale includes historicity in his account: the proposed correspondence concerns persons, events, institutions, or other realities within a historical framework. A theological proposition that appeals to a biblical pattern should therefore attend to the distinct setting of each text. Naming a pattern is not a substitute for showing how the earlier passage works within its own account or how the later text uses it.16

Beale’s definition also includes a forward-looking element. He notes, however, that scholars differ over whether such anticipation belongs to the earlier author’s conscious perspective, a fuller divine intention, or the later interpreter’s retrospective understanding. These are substantive disagreements, not interchangeable descriptions. A responsible synthesis indicates which claim it is making and does not present a later theological judgment as an uncontested report about an earlier author’s intentions.17

Beale uses “escalation” for a proposed heightening of the later correspondence in relation to the earlier type. That language belongs to his theological model; it is not established merely by observing that two passages have a common word. The interpreter must identify the proposed relation and explain the reasoning behind the claim of development. A different scholar may use typology without accepting every component of Beale’s definition.18

Retrospection names the perspective from which a later writer recognizes an earlier pattern. Beale also argues that some earlier contexts may contain indications that make a forward-looking reading more plausible; both claims need examination in their respective textual settings. When a proposition moves from an earlier passage to a later canonical synthesis, it should identify the direction of this reasoning and the evidence for each stage instead of compressing the entire argument into an unexplained label.19

寫作之前，先用短句記下觀察：誰在行動？情況有何改變？經文提供甚麼理由與結果？哪些子句依附於其他子句？連接詞能提示關係，卻不會自動解決所有語義問題。若某個重建忽略一節經文，或假定同一字詞每次都具有完全相同的意思，就需要重新檢查。

馬太福音九章三十五至三十八節提供一個例子。希臘文資料顯示，耶穌走遍城鄉，教導、宣講並醫治；祂看見群眾，因他們如同沒有牧人的羊而憐憫他們；接著祂向門徒指出莊稼多、工人少，並吩咐他們求莊稼的主差遣工人。這段敘事由耶穌的事工，轉向祂對群眾的回應，再到祂給門徒的指示。第十章隨後的差遣值得一併研究，但不可未經論證，便假定今天每位讀者與十二門徒在敘事中具有完全相同的位置。07

一個候選命題是：「耶穌看見群眾的需要，指示門徒求莊稼的主差遣工人。」它緊扣憐憫與禱告之間的轉折，但也把三十五節的教導、宣講與醫治濃縮在背景中。較完整的版本可以保留耶穌的事工，同時指出經文明確要求門徒禱告。比較不同命題時，要檢查各自能否合理交代整段經文與上下文。

追蹤段落的推進之前，應先問讀者正在閱讀哪一種文本。McLay 區分根據不同見證比較而成的折衷校勘本，以及呈現個別見證的外交式文本。校勘本因而是一項有論據的學術重建，而不是作者手稿的照片。課堂命題應交代所用版本及譯本；如果異文影響思想推進，詮釋者應解釋其影響，不要讓編輯決定隱而不見。08

文本形式不同，並不自動解決為何不同。McLay 討論希伯來文和希臘文見證時，把可觀察到的差別與幾種可能解釋區分開來：譯者的理解、不同的底本，或後來的傳遞。神學摘要應保留這個區分。讀者可以描述現存見證所說的內容，同時把其背後歷史標示為假設；不同假設也可能對文學發展的論證產生不同後果。09

譯者反覆出現的作法，有助評估某一次翻譯。單一少見的對應可能是偶然，也可能反映處境中的決定；一卷書中持續出現的模式，通常能提供較強的比較基礎。McLay 對翻譯技巧的討論鼓勵讀者留意譯者的處理方式，而不是立即重建另一種希伯來文底本。分析時仍須問：所研究的經文是否與比較例子具有相同的文本及翻譯處境。10

明確的引用公式與可能的回響，並非同樣強的證據。McLay 在其入門研究中主要分析明確引用，以便較有把握地檢查文本關係。詮釋者當然也可以研究較細微的回響，但應說明所使用的額外準則，並指出何時從直接引用轉向推定典故。保持這項區分，並不等於否定不同經文之間可能存在的神學關聯。11

McLay 比較阿摩司書的希臘文與希伯來文形式時，展示了把證據與解釋分開的必要。他考慮某個差別究竟較可能由另一底本產生，還是由譯者對希伯來文形式的理解造成。神學詮釋者使用這個方法例子時，應將所提出的解釋歸於 McLay，並在宣稱已獨立確認之前檢查相關見證。某一處文本差異有合理解釋，並不自動解決該引文中的所有差異。12

描述相關文本形式之間的關係之後，才更適合評估引文的神學意義。這個次序是一項保障，而不是宣稱研究可以完全排除神學興趣。詮釋者一開始可以帶着神學問題，但若文本見證不支持所假定的字句，就應願意調整所提出的答案。透明的綜合應指出文本比較確立了甚麼，以及哪些仍屬進一步的詮釋延伸。13

正典層次的命題可能涉及預表解讀，但學者對「預表」的定義並不相同。Beale 描述一場爭論：預表究竟主要是類比，還是也包含預示因素？這些選項對較早文本及後來神學主張有不同含義。寫作者應交代工作定義，不要假定每位讀者都接受同一種關係說明。14

在 Beale 的表述中，類比對應是預表的一項特徵。但在他的模型內，指出相似只是起點，因為模型還包括其他特徵。詮釋者應說明比較的是哪些人物、事件或制度，以及具體相似之處。神學綜合也應指出重要差異，不要把較早與後來的事物視為可以互換；這樣論證才更透明。15

Beale 的說法包含歷史性：所提出的對應關係涉及歷史框架內的人物、事件、制度或其他事物。因此，訴諸聖經模式的神學命題，需要留意每段經文各自的處境。替某種模式命名，不能代替解釋較早段落在自身敘事中的作用，以及較後文本如何使用它。16

Beale 的定義也包括指向將來的因素；然而他指出，學者對這種預示究竟屬於較早作者的自覺視角、較完整的神聖旨意，還是後來詮釋者回顧時的理解，有不同看法。這些是實質分歧，不能互換使用。負責任的綜合應指出自己提出哪一種主張，不要把後來的神學判斷呈現為對較早作者意圖毫無爭議的報告。17

Beale 用「提升」描述後來的對應事物相對於較早預表在某方面被增強。這種語言屬於他的神學模型；單憑兩段經文有共同字詞，不能確立這項主張。詮釋者必須說明所提出的關係，並解釋何以認為其中有發展。其他學者可能使用「預表」一詞，卻不接受 Beale 定義中的每一項要素。18

「回顧」指後來作者辨認較早模式所處的視角。Beale 亦主張，某些較早上下文可能包含線索，使指向將來的閱讀更有可能；這兩項主張都需要在各自的文本處境中檢驗。命題從較早經文走向後來的正典綜合時，應指出推理方向及各階段的證據，不要把整個論證壓縮成沒有說明的標籤。19

Synthesis, theology, and responsible application 神學綜合與應用

A proposition can rise above a paraphrase while retaining textual particulars. Ask whether the proposed theological claim is warranted by this passage, by the larger argument of the book, or by a later canonical comparison. Distinguishing these levels makes an interpretation stronger: a canonical theological judgment can be significant without being silently attributed to every sentence in the earlier passage.

Readers also approach the text with prior commitments and questions. The Oxford New Testament faculty’s graduate guidance stresses the interrelation of historical and theological research and the importance of Jewish and Greco-Roman contexts. This is an explicit research commitment, not proof that every scholar agrees on how the two modes of inquiry relate.20

Application involves another judgment: how does an ancient text address a different community and situation? Describe the original literary situation, identify what carries across, and explain the relevant differences. For Matthew 9:35–38, a contemporary invitation to pray for workers can draw on the explicit command, while its relation to the Twelve's particular commission requires an argument. Avoid substituting a general call to be compassionate for the passage's specific sequence of Jesus' response, disciples' prayer, and the Lord's sending.

a classroom procedure

The following procedure separates observations from interpretive claims without pretending that the reader has no prior assumptions. First, read the unit in a reliable translation and note initial questions. Second, compare the original-language text where competence and resources permit; record a difficulty rather than claiming certainty that a tool has not supplied. Third, mark the unit's principal clauses, participants, and transitions. Fourth, place it between summaries of its preceding and following context. Fifth, formulate a claim about the unit's movement, and finally compare the claim with a rival reading. These stages are not sealed compartments. A new observation can require revision of a boundary chosen earlier.

For a classroom exercise on Matthew 9:35–38, one group may initially identify the contrast between the crowds' condition and the abundance of the harvest as the most important feature. Another may focus on the movement from Jesus' compassion to the disciples' prayer. Each group should indicate which words support its account and what details remain unexplained. The exercise is stronger if students can revise their preferred sentence after hearing a criticism. The aim is not to count the greatest number of individual features, as though every detail had equal rhetorical weight. It is to make a defensible judgment about the relation among details.

The question “what does the author do here?” can be helpful when kept distinct from claims about inaccessible psychology. A text may comfort, warn, praise, narrate, reason, or command. The literary evidence for that assessment includes the genre, grammatical forms, participants and argumentative setting. For instance, Matthew 9:38 includes an imperative directed to the disciples; this observation is narrower than claiming to know every response that the first readers experienced. The account's possible effect on readers deserves discussion, but an interpreter should not present an imagined effect as directly observed history.

A teacher can also ask for a negative control: name one attractive proposition that the passage does not adequately support. For Matthew 9:35–38, “every individual disciple is instructed to appoint the workers personally” would require additional support, because the explicit request is directed to the Lord of the harvest. Another student might propose that the harvest image refers exclusively to judgment or exclusively to evangelization. Either proposal needs wider textual and contextual evidence; a familiar metaphor cannot settle its own referent. The restraint exercised here does not prevent theological application. It makes the basis and extent of that application more transparent.

when the tradition and the text disagree in emphasis

Students often encounter established theological formulations before they interpret particular passages. Prior knowledge can sharpen perception: it can alert a reader to a recurring theme, a scriptural allusion, or a difficult historical question. It can also direct attention away from features that complicate a familiar formulation. A disciplined reader therefore writes down the expected conclusion and then asks what in the passage might challenge it. The exercise is not a demand to abandon theological convictions; it is a way to prevent those convictions from doing the work of textual demonstration.

Suppose a reader expects a passage to supply an immediate individual moral instruction. The reader should still ask whether its dominant form is command, narrative, prayer, praise, argument, or some combination. A narrative of Jesus' action can be formative without turning every action of a character into a direct command. A prayer can disclose a theological vision without being reduced to an instruction about prayer technique. An argument may draw a conclusion for a particular dispute while raising a broader theological question whose transfer requires additional reasoning.

Theological traditions may differ about how the unity of a canon relates to the distinctive voices and historical settings of its books. The responsible writer identifies the tradition or argument guiding a synthesis and distinguishes a claim about one passage from a claim about the whole canon. Theological interpretation can then be debated on identifiable grounds: Is the scriptural relationship plausible? Does the synthesis accommodate the literary context? Does it acknowledge an alternative reading? What assumptions about canon, authorship, or community are doing the work?

One helpful comparison is between a narrow textual proposition and a broader theological thesis. The former might state what a particular section says or does. The latter might relate it to other texts and a doctrinal argument. Neither is automatically superior; they answer different questions. Problems arise when a writer presents a broad doctrinal thesis as though every part were a direct paraphrase of the passage, or when the writer forbids all theological reflection simply because the text does not use later technical vocabulary. Clear transitions between levels allow the reader to evaluate each argument in turn.

The editors of a collection on comparison in New Testament studies present comparison as a problem of purpose as well as method: scholars must ask what they expect comparison to accomplish and how its chosen terms affect the result. A theological proposition likewise rests on choices about what counts as relevant context. Before claiming that a second passage clarifies the first, identify the question that makes the comparison useful. Is the proposed connection lexical, narrative, historical, or theological? Each answer calls for a different sort of argument, and the same pair of texts need not provide equally strong evidence for every one of them.21

Rowe argues that identifying a text as an instance of a tradition already involves a narrative account of that tradition. His point reminds the interpreter to name a classification rather than treat it as a neutral label that explains itself. A reader who compares two uses of “freedom,” for example, should ask which communities, texts, and stories are being brought into view and why. Rowe’s argument is not an instruction to ignore a word’s immediate syntax; it identifies a further question about the frameworks within which comparisons become intelligible.22

Engberg-Pedersen’s discussion of historical difference provides a related caution. He traces an approach that attends to the customs and idioms of earlier audiences rather than treating a modern system as a ready-made key. Historical distance, however, does not mean that ancient texts cannot be understood. It means that a claim about their communication needs reasons that are sensitive to the evidence available for their setting. A responsible synthesis can then distinguish an argument about a text in its ancient context from a further argument about its present theological significance.23

Linebaugh develops the image of comparison as conversation and emphasizes that the interpreter participates in the relation between texts. This model does not require the passages to say the same thing. A conversation can expose a contrast, an unresolved question, or an unexpected convergence. It also asks interpreters to identify their own contribution: why did they choose these partners, and what would be lost by choosing a different pair? A canonical synthesis becomes more accountable when those choices are visible and the particular voice of each text remains audible.24

神學命題可以超越逐字改述，卻仍須保留經文的重要細節。應分辨某個神學判斷是由本段直接支持、由全書論證支持，還是透過後來的正典比較提出。正典層面的判斷可能十分重要，但不應在未說明的情況下，把它直接歸給較早經文中的每一句話。

讀者帶着既有的信念與問題接近文本。牛津大學新約研究團隊的研究生說明強調歷史與神學研究彼此相關，並指出猶太與希羅背景對歷史上負責任地理解新約的重要性。這是一種明確的研究立場，不代表學界對兩者如何關聯已達成完全共識。20

應用也需要論證：先描述經文的文學處境，再解釋哪些意義可以延伸到今天，並指出兩個處境的重要差異。就馬太福音九章而言，邀請信徒為工人禱告可以根據經文明確的吩咐；然而，這與十二門徒所領受的特定差遣有何關係，仍須進一步說明。不要以籠統的「要有憐憫」取代經文中耶穌的回應、門徒的禱告及主的差遣之間的具體關聯。

課堂操練的步驟

以下步驟把觀察與詮釋判斷區分開來，並不假裝讀者沒有先入的問題或信念。首先，以可靠譯本閱讀經文，記下初步問題。其次，在能力與資源容許時比較原文；若工具不能解決某個困難，就應保留疑問，而不是宣稱已經確定。第三，標記主要子句、人物與轉折。第四，分別概括前文與後文，看看本段在兩者之間的作用。第五，提出描述段落推進的命題，再與另一個合理讀法比較。這些步驟不是互相隔絕的抽屜：新的觀察可能要求我們重新修訂原先劃定的界限。

在馬太福音九章三十五至三十八節的課堂練習中，一組學生可能認為群眾的困境與豐盛莊稼之間的對比最重要；另一組可能聚焦於耶穌的憐憫如何引向門徒的禱告。兩組都應指出支持其判斷的經文字詞，也要承認哪些細節尚未解釋。聽取批評後，可以修改原先的句子。練習的目的不是把最多的細節塞進一句話，彷彿所有細節都具有相同的修辭重量；目的在於對細節之間的關係提出有根據、可以討論的判斷。

「作者在這裏做甚麼？」也是有幫助的問題，但須與推測作者無法直接觀察的心理狀態區分。文本可能安慰、警告、讚美、敘事、推理或吩咐。作出判斷時，可以檢查文體、語法形式、人物和論證處境。例如，九章三十八節有一個向門徒發出的祈使語；這項觀察比宣稱我們知道每一位最初讀者的實際反應更有限。讀者可能受到甚麼影響值得討論，但不能把想像中的效果當作已經觀察到的歷史事實。

教師也可以要求學生提出一個「反面對照」：找出一句看似吸引、但本段不足以支持的命題。例如，「每個門徒都被吩咐親自任命所有工人」需要額外論證，因為經文所記載的請求是向莊稼的主發出。又如把莊稼意象限定為單指審判，或限定為單指傳福音，兩種說法都需要更廣的文本與上下文證據。熟悉的比喻本身不能自動解決其所指。這種謹慎不會阻止神學應用，反而使應用的根據和範圍更清楚。

傳統與經文強調不同焦點時

學生通常在解釋個別經文之前，已經接觸過既有的神學表述。先前知識可以幫助讀者留意反覆出現的主題、經文回響或歷史問題；它也可能使讀者忽略那些令熟悉結論變得複雜的細節。因此，可以先寫下自己預期得到的結論，再詢問經文中有甚麼可能對它構成挑戰。這不是要求讀者放棄神學信念，而是避免讓信念取代應有的文本論證。

假設讀者預期每段經文都會立即提供個人的道德吩咐，仍然需要先問這段主要是命令、敘事、禱告、讚美、論證，還是幾種形式的組合。耶穌行動的敘事可以塑造信徒，卻不代表故事中每一個人物的行動都是直接命令。禱告可以呈現神學視野，卻不能只被縮減為禱告技巧。論證可能針對特定爭議得出結論，同時提出較廣的神學問題；要把結論帶入另一處境，仍須交代理由。

不同神學傳統對正典整體的統一性，與各書卷獨特聲音及歷史處境之間的關係，可能有不同理解。負責任的寫作應交代哪一傳統或論證正在引導綜合，也要區分對個別經文的主張與對全本正典的主張。這樣，神學詮釋便可以根據具體問題討論：所提出的經文關聯是否合理？綜合是否照顧文學上下文？是否正視其他讀法？作者對正典、作者身分或詮釋群體作了哪些假設？

另一項有用的比較，是把範圍較窄的經文命題與較廣的神學論題並列。前者可以說明一個段落說了甚麼、做了甚麼；後者可以將它與其他經文及教義論證連結。兩者回答不同問題，因此不能自動說哪一種較優越。若把整個教義論題直接當成某段經文的逐句改述，或只因經文沒有使用後來的技術詞彙就禁止一切神學反思，都會造成問題。清楚交代由一個層次走向另一個層次的理由，讀者才能逐步評估論證。

一本研究新約比較方法的論文集之編者指出，比較不僅涉及方法，也涉及目的：研究者必須問比較預期達到甚麼，以及所選比較對象如何影響結果。經文神學命題同樣依賴對相關上下文的選擇。宣稱另一段經文能照亮本段之前，應指出使比較有意義的問題：所提出的關聯是詞彙、敘事、歷史，還是神學關係？不同答案需要不同論證；同一組文本不一定為每種主張提供同樣力度的證據。21

Rowe 認為，把一篇文本辨認為某個傳統的例子，已經涉及對該傳統的敘事理解。這提醒詮釋者應交代分類方式，而不是把分類當作無須解釋的中性標籤。例如，比較兩處「自由」的用法時，讀者應問：哪些群體、文本和故事被納入視野，理由是甚麼？Rowe 的論點並不是叫讀者忽略字詞在直接上下文中的句法，而是提出比較何以可以理解的另一層問題。22

Engberg-Pedersen 談歷史差異時提出相關提醒：他追溯一種重視古代聽眾習俗與語言的研究方向，避免把現代體系當成現成的解經鑰匙。然而，歷史距離不代表古代文本無法理解；它表示關於文本如何溝通的主張，需要顧及可取得的處境證據。負責任的綜合可以因此區分兩項論證：一項關於文本在古代處境中的意思，另一項關於它對當代神學的意義。23

Linebaugh 發展「比較如同對話」的圖像，強調詮釋者也參與文本之間的關係。這個模型並不要求兩段經文說同一件事。對話可以顯出對比、尚未解決的問題，或令人意外的相近之處；它也要求詮釋者交代自己的貢獻：為何選擇這些對話對象？若換成另一組文本，會失去甚麼？當這些選擇公開可見，並且每篇文本獨特的聲音仍能被聽見時，正典綜合便更容易接受檢驗。24

Compare rival summaries fairly 比較可供選擇的摘要

A good synthesis is accountable to more than the evidence that first attracted the interpreter. Construct at least two plausible summaries and ask what each explains. One might emphasize what the passage says about God's action; another might emphasize the response requested of its audience. A sound comparison asks whether these are genuinely incompatible, whether one is a consequence of the other, or whether the passage deliberately holds both together. Avoid announcing a winner before specifying the relevant evidence.

For Matthew 9:35–38, compare the proposition offered above with: “Jesus sees the crowds' vulnerability and responds with compassion.” The second formulation captures verse 36 closely. Yet as a summary of the whole unit it does not explicitly account for Jesus' teaching, proclamation and healing in verse 35 or for the directive to pray in verse 38. That limitation does not make the sentence false; it means its explanatory reach is narrower than the proposed passage. A third possibility, “Disciples must meet all human needs,” is wider than the explicit instruction and risks turning the Lord's sending into an unqualified demand on the disciples themselves. The exercise illustrates why the boundaries of a claim matter as much as its general attractiveness.25

Evidence may also correct the interpreter's sense of what counts as background. Troels Engberg-Pedersen discusses the historical distance that prompted J. J. Wettstein to urge attention to the language and customs of ancient audiences. His account also traces later tensions between historical description and systematic theology. This historical difference is not a license to declare contemporary questions irrelevant; it is a reason to articulate the steps connecting them to the ancient text. 26

an argumentative test case (James 2:14–18)

An argumentative unit requires a different account of movement from a narrative. James 2:14 opens with a question about benefit: what use is it if someone says that he has faith but does not have works? The wording distinguishes the reported claim to possess faith from the writer's judgment about that claim. The second question asks whether that faith can save. The interpreter should observe the demonstrative force of the article in its setting without assuming that grammar alone settles every larger theological controversy. The question itself gives the first step of the argument; the answer must be inferred from the illustration and conclusion that follow.27

Verses 15–16 introduce a hypothetical brother or sister lacking clothing and daily food. Someone offers a peaceful farewell and wishes that they be warmed and fed, yet does not supply bodily necessities. The repeated question, “what benefit?,” connects the example to the opening. The analogy is not a claim that words are intrinsically worthless: the defect is a response that speaks while withholding what the situation requires. A synthesis that speaks only of private sincerity would neglect the concrete need that the writer places before the audience. Equally, the example does not by itself settle every question about the relation between divine grace, human action, and final judgment across the New Testament.28

The conclusion in verse 17 states that faith without works is dead by itself. Verse 18 then introduces a further exchange about showing faith and works. The exact point at which a hypothetical interlocutor's speech ends is an interpretive question; translations differ in punctuation, which is supplied by editors. A careful proposition should not make its entire argument depend on an unacknowledged punctuation decision. It can instead identify the contrast that remains visible: the demand to show faith apart from works is answered by a claim that faith is shown through works. Further argument continues beyond the chosen illustration, so a summary limited to verses 14–18 must not pretend to have exhausted the entire discussion.29

Compare two candidate propositions. “James asks whether a claim to faith without corresponding action is beneficial and illustrates its inadequacy through a neighbour's unmet need” keeps the question, example, and judgment in view. “Good people are saved because they perform enough deeds” adds a quantitative measure and a comprehensive account of salvation that these verses do not state. Rejecting the second sentence at this point does not decide every theological question about the letter; it identifies the distance between what the selected evidence says and what the proposed sentence asserts. A competing synthesis could emphasize the visibility of faith in verse 18, but should still explain the opening question and the bodily needs in the illustration.

The exercise also shows why transfer to a contemporary setting requires more than substituting a modern noun for an ancient one. A congregation considering how to respond to material need can ask what resources it possesses, who has been overlooked, and whether comforting speech is accompanied by tangible help. That application attends to the logic of the example; it does not license an outsider to infer the entire spiritual condition of any particular person from one observed action. The movement from text to judgment about a real individual introduces evidence and responsibilities that the ancient example alone cannot supply.

A rival proposition may depend on a proposed scriptural echo. Beale’s summary of Hays’s criterion of availability asks whether the earlier text was available to the later writer and could have been recognized. This is a preliminary historical question; it does not by itself establish that a particular phrase is an allusion. If availability is uncertain, state the uncertainty before using the proposed echo as the foundation of a theological synthesis.30

The criterion of volume asks how much verbal or syntactic material the passages share. A striking shared phrase can strengthen a proposal; a single common word is usually less decisive. Theological similarity may make comparison worthwhile, but it must not replace the demonstration of a textual relationship. A synthesis should distinguish an observed overlap from the further claim that an author deliberately invoked another passage.31

Recurrence asks whether related references appear elsewhere in the immediate context or in the same author’s work. A cluster of signals can make a proposed echo more plausible than an isolated resemblance. Yet repeated use of a common theme does not automatically prove dependence on one precise source. A sound proposition explains the force of the recurring material and identifies evidence that would support a different source or a shared convention.32

Thematic coherence considers whether the earlier context fits and illuminates the later argument. This is an interpretive test, not a license to assume a link because two texts can be harmonized. A proposed theological proposition should explain how the earlier passage contributes to the specific movement of the later one. If the alleged precursor adds nothing that matters to that movement, its explanatory value is weaker even when a broad theme is shared.33

Historical plausibility asks whether the writer could have intended the reference and whether an audience could reasonably have understood it. Beale recognizes that even an intended allusion need not be noticed by every reader. The distinction matters when moving from textual interpretation to teaching: one may argue that a connection enriches the text without asserting that all original hearers demonstrably recognized every detail of it.34

The history of interpretation offers a record of readings that may alert us to an overlooked proposal. Beale cautions that this criterion has limits: a long commentary tradition can preserve insights, but repeated dependence on earlier commentators can also narrow the options. A synthesis should identify who has made a claim and why, while testing the argument on its evidence rather than deciding by antiquity or popularity alone.35

Finally, satisfaction asks whether the proposed echo makes sense in the later context and gives a persuasive account of its rhetorical effect. Satisfaction is an invitation to judge explanatory power, not an independent machine for confirming an allusion. A rival that explains the same wording and context with fewer unsupported assumptions should also be considered. The theological proposition can then reflect the relative strength of the proposed connection rather than presenting an attractive possibility as certain.36

好的綜合不能只依靠最先吸引詮釋者的證據。可以提出兩個合理的摘要，逐一檢查各自能夠解釋甚麼。有的摘要強調上帝的行動，有的強調聽眾應有的回應。兩者是否真正互相排斥？其中一項是否為另一項的結果？還是經文同時保留兩者？作出判斷以前，應先說明比較所根據的證據。

以馬太福音九章三十五至三十八節為例，「耶穌看見群眾的困苦，並以憐憫回應」緊扣三十六節，卻沒有明確交代三十五節的教導、宣講、醫治，也沒有提及三十八節求主差遣工人的吩咐。這句話並非錯誤，但它所概括的範圍比整段經文狹窄。若改說「門徒必須滿足所有人的需要」，範圍又可能超過經文明確的吩咐，並將主差遣工人的行動變成未加區分地壓在門徒身上的責任。因此，命題的範圍與它是否動聽同樣重要。25

論證文體的測試案例（雅各書二章十四至十八節）

Troels Engberg-Pedersen 討論歷史距離，指出 Wettstein 鼓勵讀者研究古代聽眾的語言和習俗。他也追蹤歷史描述與系統神學之間後來出現的張力。歷史差異並不令當代問題失去意義，卻要求讀者清楚交代如何從古代文本走到當代問題。26

論證文的推進方式不同於敘事。雅各書二章十四節先問一個關於「益處」的問題：若有人說自己有信心，卻沒有行為，有甚麼益處？這裏必須分辨一個人宣稱自己有信心，與作者對該宣稱作出的判斷。隨後的問題問這信心能否救他。讀者應留意該詞在句中的用法，但不能假定單憑一項語法特徵便解決整場更廣泛的神學爭論。問題構成論證的起點；答案則須從接下來的例子與結論理解。27

十五至十六節設想一位弟兄或姊妹缺乏衣服和日用食物。有人向他們道平安，祝願他們得到溫暖和飽足，卻沒有提供身體所需。重複出現的「有甚麼益處」將例子與開頭的問題連接起來。這個比喻不是說言語本身毫無價值；問題在於回應停留在言語，卻沒有供應眼前所需。若命題只談個人內心是否真誠，就忽略了作者擺在讀者面前的具體缺乏。另一方面，單憑這個例子，也不能自動解決整部新約關於恩典、人的行動與最終審判的每一個問題。28

十七節的結論指出，信心若沒有行為，便是死的。十八節接着展開一段關於顯明信心與行為的對話。假想對話者的話究竟在哪裏結束，是一項詮釋問題；譯本所加的標點可能不同。謹慎的命題不應把整個論證建立在沒有交代的標點選擇上，而可以先指出清楚可見的對比：有人要求在行為以外顯明信心，回應則提出藉着行為顯明信心。論證在所選的十八節之後繼續，因此只概括十四至十八節時，不應假裝已經處理全段討論。29

比較兩個命題：「雅各質疑沒有相應行動的信心宣稱有何益處，並以鄰舍的需要未獲滿足說明其不足。」這句話照顧了問題、例子和判斷。另一句「好人因為做了足夠的善事而得救」則加入經文沒有說明的數量標準及完整救恩模式。此處拒絕第二句，並不表示所有關於本書的神學問題都已解決；它只是指出所選證據與該句主張之間的距離。另一個合理命題可以強調十八節信心的可見性，但仍須解釋開頭的問題和例子中的身體需要。

應用到今天時，也不能只把古代名詞換成現代名詞。關心物質需要的教會可以詢問：我們擁有甚麼資源？誰被忽略？安慰的話是否伴隨具體幫助？這樣的應用尊重例子的論證邏輯，卻不授權旁觀者憑一次所見的行動，推斷某個人的整體屬靈狀況。由文本推論到對真實個人的判斷，涉及古代例子本身不能提供的額外證據與責任。

不同命題有時取決於所提出的經文回響。Beale 摘述 Hays 的「可取得性」準則時，要求研究者詢問較早文本是否為後來作者所能接觸，以及讀者是否可能辨認。這是初步的歷史問題，本身不能證明某一句確實構成典故。若可取得性不確定，在把回響作為神學綜合的基礎前，便應先說明這個限制。30

「聲量」準則詢問兩段文本共享多少詞語或句法形式。顯著的相同語句可能加強提議；單一常見字詞通常較難定論。神學相似可能使比較值得進行，卻不能代替對文本關聯的證明。綜合時應分辨實際觀察到的重疊，以及作者有意引用另一段經文這一步更進一步的主張。31

「重現」準則詢問直接上下文或同一作者其他作品中，是否出現相關的引用。多個線索聚集時，所提出的回響可能比孤立的相似更有說服力；但共同主題反覆出現，並不自動證明依賴某一個特定來源。好的命題需要解釋重現材料的作用，也要指出甚麼證據可能支持另一個來源或共同慣例。32

「主題連貫」考慮較早上下文是否契合並照亮後來文本的論證。這是一項解釋測試，並不授權讀者只因兩段經文可以協調便假定存在關聯。神學命題應說明較早經文如何有助理解後來段落的具體推進；如果所提出的前文並沒有為這個推進帶來重要解釋，即使兩者共享廣泛主題，其解釋價值仍然較弱。33

「歷史可能性」詢問作者是否可能有意引用，以及聽眾是否有合理機會理解。Beale 承認，即使作者有意作出回響，也不代表每位讀者都必定察覺。從經文解釋進入教學時，這個區分很重要：可以論證某個關聯豐富了文本的意思，卻不必宣稱所有最初聽眾都可以被證明認出了每一項細節。34

「詮釋史」保存了不同讀法，可能提醒我們留意曾被忽略的建議。Beale 同時提醒這個準則的限制：長久的註釋傳統能保存洞見，但後來注釋者反覆依賴前人，也可能縮窄選項。綜合時應指出誰提出某個主張及其理由，再根據證據檢驗論證，而不是只按年代久遠或流行程度決定。35

最後，「滿足度」詢問所提出的回響在後來上下文是否合理，能否令人信服地解釋其修辭效果。這個準則是邀請讀者判斷解釋力，並不是能獨立確認典故的機器。若另一個方案以較少未獲支持的假設便能解釋相同字詞與上下文，也應一併考慮。神學命題可以因此反映所提出關聯的證據強弱，而不是把吸引人的可能性當成確定事實。36

Test words within arguments and narratives 在論證與敘事中檢驗字詞

Lexical analysis belongs inside the movement of the passage. A dictionary can list usages; it cannot decide automatically which meaning a word bears in a particular clause. Ask how the term interacts with its grammatical construction, neighbouring words, participants, and literary setting. Repetition can be rhetorically important even when a word's sense shifts. Conversely, a passage can pursue one line of thought using varied vocabulary.

C. Kavin Rowe argues that the intelligibility of weight-bearing words depends on broader narratives and practices, not simply on an abstract comparison of individual terms. That proposal calls attention to questions that word counts alone cannot answer: what story enables the audience to understand a term, and what practices surround its use? It should be presented as a position in a scholarly debate, not as a license to ignore immediate grammar or assume a single, uncontested narrative context. 37

Joel B. Green's review of Peter Doble offers an instructive case of testing a synthesis against alternative evidence. Doble reads the centurion's description of Jesus as *dikaios* in Luke 23:47 through the figure of the righteous sufferer in Wisdom. Green's review raises questions about the selection and handling of other interpretive possibilities. The methodological lesson does not require adjudicating the entire debate here: a theological pattern gains force when it explains the language in context and engages significant competing evidence. A pattern becomes less persuasive if relevant data are set aside without argument. 38

proportion and contrast in a proposed synthesis

Galatians 5:13–15 offers a short exercise in preserving both sides of a contrast. The recipients are said to have been called to freedom. They are cautioned against treating that freedom as an opportunity for the flesh and are instructed instead to serve one another through love. The following verse connects the instruction with the command to love one's neighbour as oneself; verse 15 warns of the consequences of mutual hostility. The repeated reference to one another joins the positive instruction and negative warning. Each observation is visible in the selected text; the interpreter must still investigate the broader argument before explaining how this section relates to the whole letter.39

Consider the proposed sentence, “Freedom means doing whatever one wishes.” It notices the word freedom but omits the explicit restriction and the imperative of mutual service. A second sentence, “The community must serve one another through love because its freedom is not an occasion for the flesh,” represents more of the contrast but still compresses the scriptural command and warning. A third, “The law is abolished and therefore irrelevant,” requires a much wider argument: verse 14 explicitly invokes the whole law and the command to love. The sentence may express a possible conclusion in a separate debate, but it cannot simply be substituted for an account of these three verses. A useful proposition should be judged by what it illuminates and what it leaves for the exposition to explain.

Even an accurate summary is not a miniature commentary. It will not reproduce every grammatical relation or settle the precise force of every word. Instead, it provides a map whose omissions are acknowledged and whose major relationships can be checked. In this passage, a teacher can ask students to mark the call, the prohibition, the alternative command, the supporting appeal, and the warning in separate colours. They can then compare proposed summaries: Does each retain the movement from freedom to service? Does it explain how the warning relates to the command? Does it import claims from elsewhere without identifying them? The exercise rewards attention to proportion rather than the multiplication of abstract theological vocabulary.

The move to application should retain that structure. A community may have legitimate disagreements over how freedom should be exercised, but the text's mutual language presses readers to consider how their actions affect others. Applying this insight to a particular conflict requires listening to the people involved and attending to concrete power and vulnerability. The command to serve through love should not be used as a shortcut for requiring a harmed person to accept further harm; the passage itself must be read alongside the wider duties and responsibilities relevant to the situation. The point here is methodological: an application must explain its grounds and scope, and must not erase the people whose situation it addresses.

A proposition may depend on a key word, but a word is not identical to the object or event it describes. Campbell introduces the distinction among a linguistic symbol, its sense, and its referent. This distinction helps explain why translating a term with the same English gloss in every passage can conceal differences in use. The interpreter should specify the claim about the passage before treating a dictionary equivalent as the theological proposition itself.40

Synonymy is a relation between senses, not a promise that two words are interchangeable in every setting. Campbell notes that words can overlap in some uses while differing in others. For a proposed synthesis, the relevant question is whether a contrast is supported at this occurrence; an attractive contrast drawn from two dictionary entries is not enough. Conversely, the possibility of overlap should not be used to forbid a distinction that the local passage actually develops.41

Context operates at more than one scale. Campbell distinguishes the surrounding literary text from other aspects of context and asks readers to consider sentence, paragraph, and larger composition. A passage-level proposition should therefore be tested in both directions: does it make sense of the clause that supplies the key word, and does it fit the surrounding argument? A broad canonical theme cannot by itself erase a qualification present in the immediate sentence.42

A writer’s lexical choice is meaningful within the options and constraints of a language. Campbell notes both the significance of choosing one expression and the idiomatic restrictions that limit available alternatives. An interpreter can ask what another expression might have conveyed, but must demonstrate that the alternative was actually plausible in the same construction. Imagining an unlimited choice of synonyms can make an ordinary usage appear more deliberate than the evidence warrants.43

A lexical field places related expressions alongside one another for comparison. Campbell describes this as a way to ask how neighbouring terms relate, including similarities and differences. Such a comparison can generate a question for exegesis; it does not establish that every available feature of a field is activated whenever one member appears. A sound proposition identifies the particular relation supported by the passage rather than importing the entire field into one occurrence.44

When a word seems ambiguous, first test whether contextual evidence favours one sense. Campbell distinguishes apparent uncertainty in the interpreter from cases where ambiguity may remain in the utterance. A proposition need not conceal this distinction: it can state the preferred reading, identify a viable alternative, and explain what follows for the larger theological claim. Where the evidence does not decide, a conditional formulation is more precise than a confident but unsupported conclusion.45

字典可以列出字詞的不同用法，卻不能自動決定某個子句中的意思。詮釋者需要同時考慮句法、相鄰詞語、人物及文學處境。重複可能具有修辭意義，但不代表字詞每次出現都必須帶有完全相同的細微意思。反過來說，一段經文也可能使用不同詞彙，繼續發展同一條思想線索。

C. Kavin Rowe 提醒讀者，重要詞語的可理解性涉及較廣闊的敘事與生活實踐；單純計算詞彙不足以處理這些問題。這是學術討論中的一種立場，並不授權我們忽略直接上下文中的語法，也不表示某個敘事背景已經毫無爭議。Joel B. Green 評論 Peter Doble 對路加福音二十三章四十七節的解讀時，也提出另一個有用的問題：某個神學模式是否充分處理相關的替代解釋與證據？不用在此先解決整場爭論，就能看見清楚交代證據取捨的重要性。3738

命題中的比例與對比

加拉太書五章十三至十五節提供一個保存對比兩面的簡短練習。經文說讀者蒙召得自由，提醒他們不要把自由當作放縱肉體的機會，反而要藉着愛彼此服事。下一節把這項指示與愛鄰舍如己的命令連接；十五節警告彼此敵對所帶來的後果。「彼此」一詞在積極命令與消極警告中反覆出現，使兩者連在一起。這些觀察都可在所選經文中看見；但要說明本段如何參與整封書信的論證，仍須研究更廣的上下文。39

試比較幾個命題。「自由就是想做甚麼便做甚麼」留意到自由一詞，卻忽略了明確的限制和彼此服事的命令。「群體應當藉着愛彼此服事，因為自由不是放縱肉體的機會」比較能表現對比，但仍把經文的誡命和警告濃縮了。「律法已經廢除，因此毫不相關」則需要遠較廣泛的論證：十四節明確提到全律法及愛人的命令。這句話可能屬於另一場爭論中的結論，但不能不加說明便取代對這三節經文的描述。好的命題需要按它照亮了甚麼、又留下甚麼要在講解中處理來評估。

即使準確的摘要也不是微型註釋書。它不會重述每一個語法關係，也不能解決每個字詞的確切意思。它比較像一張地圖：主要關係可以核對，而被省略的部分也需要承認。教師可以請學生分別標出蒙召、禁止、相反的命令、支持理由和警告，再比較不同摘要：命題是否保留從自由到服事的推進？是否解釋警告與命令的關係？是否從其他經文帶入主張卻沒有交代？這種練習重視經文各部分的比例，而不是堆砌抽象神學詞彙。

進入應用時也應保留這個結構。群體對如何運用自由可能有正當分歧，但經文中彼此相待的語言，要求讀者思想自己的行動如何影響別人。把這個洞見應用於具體衝突，需要聆聽當事人，並留意實際權力關係和脆弱處境。愛中服事的命令不能被用作捷徑，要求受傷害的人繼續承受傷害；解釋這段經文時也須考慮處境中相關的其他責任。這裏的重點是方法：應用必須說明根據與範圍，也不能抹去受影響的人。

命題可能倚賴某個關鍵字詞，但字詞不等同它所指的對象或事件。Campbell 介紹語言符號、意義與所指對象之間的區分。這有助說明為何在每段經文都用同一個中文對應詞，可能掩蓋實際用法的差異。詮釋者應先說明對經文提出甚麼主張，不要直接把字典中的對應詞當作神學命題。40

同義是意義之間的關係，並不保證兩個詞在每個處境都能互換。Campbell 指出，字詞可能在某些用法重疊，在其他用法則有差異。評估神學綜合時，重要問題是這一次出現的上下文是否支持所提出的對比；兩項字典釋義之間看似吸引的差別並不足夠。反過來，也不能因為存在重疊的可能，就禁止經文本身實際發展的區別。41

上下文有不同層次。Campbell 區分直接相鄰的文學文本與其他處境因素，並要求讀者考慮句子、段落及更大的篇章。經文命題因此需要從兩個方向檢驗：它能否解釋包含關鍵詞的子句，也能否配合周圍的論證？廣泛的正典主題本身不能抹去直接句子中存在的限定。42

作者的選詞在語言可提供的選項與限制之中具有意義。Campbell 同時指出選擇某個表達方式的重要性，以及習語限制如何約束可用選項。詮釋者可以問另一種表達可能帶來甚麼意思，但必須證明該替代形式在相同句法中確實合理。若假定作者可以無限制地選擇同義詞，便可能把普通用法解讀得比證據容許的更有意圖。43

詞彙場把相關表達並列，方便比較。Campbell 說明這種方法可用來探討相鄰詞語的相似和差異。這類比較能產生解經問題，卻不能證明詞彙場中每一項可能特徵都在每次出現時被啟動。可靠的命題應指出本段所支持的具體關係，而不是把整個詞彙場的意思一併帶入某一次用法。44

字詞看似含糊時，首先應測試上下文證據是否支持其中一種意思。Campbell 區分詮釋者暫時尚未解決的不確定性，以及語句本身可能保留的歧義。命題不必掩蓋這個分別：可以說明較可取的讀法、指出仍然可行的替代方案，並解釋兩者對較大神學主張有何影響。若證據不足以定論，條件式表述比充滿信心但欠缺根據的結論更精確。45

Make the inferential steps visible 清楚交代推論步驟

A chain of thought in exegesis is a public sequence of warranted interpretive claims, not a record of every private association a reader experiences. A useful presentation might begin with a textual observation, identify a relation to the neighbouring unit, propose what the relation contributes to the book's argument, and then state the theological synthesis. Each step should have a clear basis. When the evidence permits more than one explanation, label the conclusion provisional and explain what would favour each alternative.

Consider the difference between observation and inference in the Matthew example. The Greek text states that Jesus sees the crowds, is moved with compassion, and tells disciples to ask the harvest's Lord to send workers. It does not explicitly state every reason why Matthew placed this unit at this location. The claim that it prepares for chapter 10 is a literary inference that should be tested by reading the commissioning and its immediate context. To say that a contemporary church should pray for ministers is an application, requiring reflection on continuity and difference. These statements are related, but they are not the same kind of claim. Keeping their levels visible allows disagreement over an inference without requiring disagreement over the words on the page.46

Jonathan Linebaugh's discussion of comparison draws attention to the reader's active role in bringing texts into conversation. Comparison can illuminate similarities and differences, but the selection of comparable texts is itself an interpretive choice. A responsible synthesis explains why a comparison is relevant and where the texts resist being made identical. 47

accountable use of scholarship

An interpreter's reading of a commentary should change the questions asked of the passage, not merely decorate a conclusion already reached. Suppose a commentary identifies a disputed boundary, an unexpected syntactic relation, or an alternative antecedent for a pronoun. The next step is to return to the text and identify which observations distinguish the alternatives. A useful research note records both the scholar's proposed interpretation and the evidence advanced for it. A bibliography that merely lists authorities gives the reader no way to tell whether their arguments were understood or whether their conclusions actually support the sentence carrying the note.48

Nijay K. Gupta describes the research problem of “plundering”: searching a collection of works for convenient fragments while neglecting the shape of their arguments. His observation has a direct consequence for synthesis. If an interpreter quotes one sentence in support of a thesis, the surrounding discussion may reveal qualifications, an alternative position being reported, or a different question altogether. A responsible note identifies the work and the place consulted; the accompanying prose states the position at the level of certainty the source supports. A summary of one chapter is not evidence that the whole work makes the same argument, and a quoted description of another scholar is not necessarily the author's own view.49

Comparison among scholars is most useful when it names the actual point of difference. Two interpreters might agree about the sequence of clauses yet disagree about which relation carries the unit's argumentative weight. Others may differ over whether a phrase evokes an earlier text, or whether a later canonical reading legitimately broadens its significance. A table of names and positions can help, but it must not erase differences in the questions being answered. One scholar's historical reconstruction and another scholar's theological use need not be direct rivals unless they make incompatible claims about the same matter. Conversely, calling positions “complementary” does not resolve a genuine disagreement over evidence.

In a review of an exegetical book, Joel B. Green considers Peter Doble's reading of Luke 23:47 and raises questions about the interpretation's treatment of rival evidence. This example is useful because its disputed word does not float free of the larger reconstruction. The question is how the interpretation of δίκαιος relates to the narrative and to proposed scriptural patterns, and whether the argument fairly engages other possibilities. Green's critique should be distinguished from Doble's own full argument; the review is a witness to the former and a guide to the latter, not a substitute for reading the monograph. A responsible writer who has only the review should say so.50

This discipline applies equally to the interpreter's own proposition. Keep a small evidence register with four columns: textual observation; proposed inference; significant alternative; and what would change the judgment. For the Matthew example, an observation is that Jesus tells the disciples to ask the Lord to send workers. An inference is that the instruction prepares readers for the following commission. A significant alternative might assign greater weight to its function as the close of the preceding account. A decision between them should consider both the repeated summary of ministry and the immediately following narrative, rather than treating either location as self-evident. The register keeps a confident theological claim tethered to the observations that make it plausible.

Supplement: a transparent revision log

An interpretation is more useful to a community of readers when its revisions can be explained. Keep the first formulation, list the strongest objection, and then write a revised formulation next to it. The record need not be long. Its purpose is to show what changed in response to evidence and what remains unresolved. This practice also protects against a common mistake in group work: presenting a negotiated sentence as though everyone had always agreed on both its evidence and its implications.

Imagine that an initial summary of Matthew 9:35–38 reads, “Jesus commissions the disciples to heal every crowd.” A review of the selected verses reveals a mismatch: Jesus is the person described as teaching, proclaiming, and healing; the explicit instruction to the disciples in verse 38 is to ask the Lord to send workers. The following chapter contains a commission, but it must be distinguished from the instruction in the proposed unit. A revised sentence might say, “After ministering to the crowds and seeing their distress, Jesus tells his disciples to ask the Lord to send workers.” This revision has a reason: it changes the subject of the reported actions and restores the passage's explicit instruction.

The revised sentence still makes choices. It places the crowd's distress before the request for workers and may imply a tighter relationship between the shepherd image and the harvest image than an interpreter is prepared to defend. A careful discussion can state that possibility and invite examination of the change in imagery. Another reader may emphasize the contrast between abundant harvest and few workers. These readings need not be forced into a false choice; a fuller exposition can explain both while a short proposition chooses which relation to foreground. The reader should know where the compression has occurred.

Use the same record when evidence is inconclusive. If two plausible segmentations of a letter assign a transitional sentence to different units, note each boundary and the reasons for it. A proposition built on one boundary can be retained provisionally while a reader tests what changes under the other. Similarly, when the historical meaning of a metaphor is disputed, distinguish what the immediate context establishes from what a wider comparison suggests. Label a proposal as uncertain when the evidence is genuinely balanced; the presence of uncertainty does not make the whole passage unintelligible.

Finally, return to the people for whom the synthesis is intended. A sentence prepared for an advanced seminar may identify an exegetical crux in technical terms, while a teaching setting may require a more accessible formulation. Accessibility must not be purchased by erasing the limits of the evidence or presenting a disputed inference as a direct quotation from the text. A teacher can explain a live disagreement in plain language, give reasons for a working judgment, and invite readers to check the passage. The final proposition is therefore a starting point for responsible communication, not a replacement for the interpretation that supports it.

解經中的思想鏈是可以公開檢驗的論證次序，並非讀者每個私人聯想的紀錄。可以從文本觀察開始，指出它與前後單元的關係，提出該關係如何參與全書論證，最後寫出神學綜合。每一步都需要明確根據；若證據容許多種解釋，就應說明結論仍屬暫定，並指出甚麼證據可能支持不同方案。

馬太的例子有助區分觀察與推論。希臘文指出耶穌看見群眾、動了憐憫，並吩咐門徒求莊稼的主差遣工人。經文卻沒有直接列明馬太把這段放在此處的每一個原因。說這段為第十章作準備，是需要透過閱讀差遣敘事及其上下文來檢驗的文學推論。說今天的教會應為工人禱告，則是應用判斷，需要思想兩個處境的延續與差異。這些陳述彼此相關，卻不屬於同一類型的主張。46

Jonathan Linebaugh 討論比較時，注意到讀者主動把文本帶入對話。比較能照亮相似與差異，但選擇哪些文本互相比較本身也是詮釋判斷。負責任的綜合應解釋比較為何相關，也要指出文本在哪些地方不能被視為完全相同。47

負責任地使用學術著作

閱讀註釋書應當改變詮釋者向經文提出的問題，而不只是為既定結論添上權威。假設某位學者指出段落界限有爭議、句法關係可能不同，或代名詞另有可能的指涉，下一步就是返回經文，找出哪些觀察能區分不同方案。有效的研究筆記應同時記錄學者的解釋及其提出的證據。只有作者姓名的書目，不能讓讀者知道寫作者是否理解了論證，也不能證明該書真正支持註腳所在的句子。48

Nijay K. Gupta 描述一種研究問題：寫作者先有結論，再從不同著作中搜集方便使用的片段，卻沒有理解各書原本的論證。這項提醒對神學綜合尤其重要。引文的上下文可能顯示作者加上了限制、正在報告別人的立場，或其實回答另一個問題。負責任的註腳應指出所查閱的著作和位置；正文則按證據所能支持的程度表述結論。一章的摘要不能自動代表整本書，而作者轉述他人的看法，也不一定是作者自己的立場。49

比較學者時，應指出真正的分歧。兩位詮釋者可能同意子句的次序，卻對哪個關係最能承載段落論證有不同判斷；也可能對某一句是否回響較早經文，以及後來的正典閱讀能否擴大其意義有不同意見。列出姓名和立場固然有用，卻不應抹去各人所回答問題的差別。歷史重建與神學運用只有在對同一事項提出互不相容的主張時，才必然是直接競爭的答案。反過來說，若兩種解釋對證據有真正分歧，只稱它們「互補」也不能解決問題。

Joel B. Green 評論 Peter Doble 對路加福音二十三章四十七節的解讀時，提出其如何處理其他可能證據的問題。這個例子提醒我們，δίκαιος 的解釋與整體敘事重建及所提出的經文模式有關；讀者必須詢問論證是否公平面對其他可能性。Green 的評論應與 Doble 本人的完整論證區分：書評能證明評論者提出了甚麼批評，也能引導讀者尋找原書，卻不能取代閱讀專著。若寫作者只讀過書評，就應清楚說明。50

同樣的紀律適用於自己的命題。可以設立四欄證據表：文本觀察、所提出的推論、重要的替代方案，以及甚麼證據可能改變判斷。在馬太的例子中，耶穌吩咐門徒求主差遣工人是觀察；這項吩咐為後面的差遣作準備則是推論。另一個方案可能更重視它作為前一段事工記述結尾的功能。評估兩者時，應同時考慮重複出現的事工摘要與緊接其後的敘事，而不是把任何一個位置的功能當作不證自明。證據表使神學判斷持續連結到令它可信的觀察。

補充：公開記錄命題的修訂

當讀者能夠理解一項解釋為何修訂，它就更有利於群體討論。可以保留最初的命題，列出最有力的反對理由，再並列修訂後的句子。記錄不必很長，目的在於顯示證據如何帶來改變，以及哪些問題仍未解決。這個做法也可避免群體討論中的常見錯誤：把共同修改後的句子呈現得彷彿所有人從一開始便同意其證據與含義。

假設有人先把馬太福音九章三十五至三十八節概括為「耶穌差遣門徒醫治所有群眾」。檢查所選經文便會發現不相符之處：經文描述耶穌教導、宣講和醫治；三十八節對門徒明確的吩咐，是求莊稼的主差遣工人。下一章確有差遣，但必須與所選段落中的吩咐區分。修訂後可以說：「耶穌向群眾施行事工，看見他們的困苦，便吩咐門徒求主差遣工人。」這項修改有清楚理由：它修正各項行動的主詞，並恢復經文明確記載的指示。

修訂後的句子仍然作出了選擇。它先提群眾的困苦，再提求主差遣，可能暗示牧羊意象與莊稼意象之間有比詮釋者願意主張的更緊密關係。謹慎的講解可以指出這種可能，並邀請讀者檢查意象的轉換。另一位讀者可能更重視莊稼多與工人少的對比。兩種讀法不一定非此即彼：較完整的講解可以同時處理，而簡短命題則選擇凸顯其中一個關係。重點是讓讀者知道濃縮發生在甚麼地方。

證據不足以定論時，也可以使用同樣的記錄。若書信中某個過渡句既可能屬於前一單元，也可能屬於後一單元，應列出兩種界限及其理由。根據其中一種界限提出的命題可以暫時保留，同時測試另一種劃分會帶來甚麼改變。又如某個比喻的歷史意思有爭議，就應區分直接上下文可以確立甚麼，以及更廣泛的比較提示甚麼。當證據確實容許不同判斷，應把結論標為暫定；存在不確定性不代表整段經文都無法理解。

最後要回到命題所面向的讀者。研究院研討課可能需要以專門術語指出解經難題；教學場合則可能需要較易理解的句子。但易懂不應以抹去證據限制為代價，也不能把有爭議的推論包裝成經文直接說出的話。教師可以用清楚語言交代不同看法，解釋暫時採取某項判斷的理由，並邀請讀者自行核對。最後的命題是負責任溝通的起點，不能取代支持它的解釋。
