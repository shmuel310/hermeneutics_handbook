01 · COMPARATIVE HORIZONS 01 · 歷史比較視野

Comparison as a disciplined question 比較方法與證據階梯

● COMPARATIVE PRINCIPLE 比較研究原則

Comparison requires both likeness and difference, assessed in historical context. 比較研究須在歷史處境中同時考量相似與差異。

Historical comparison does not begin with an assumption that a biblical author copied a particular ancient document. It begins by identifying what is being compared: a word, an arrangement of clauses, a legal problem, an institution, or a wider concept. Each kind of comparison permits different conclusions. A shared social problem may help illuminate the range of ancient responses without demonstrating direct textual contact. Close verbal sequence may strengthen a hypothesis of literary relationship, but the proposed route and date of transmission still need to be argued. The comparison is most useful when it records differences as carefully as similarities and asks whether the proposed comparandum was historically available to the community under study.01

K. Lawson Younger Jr. proposes assessing possible parallels along linguistic, geographical, chronological, and cultural axes.15 These are not four boxes whose mechanical completion proves literary dependence. They are a way to make the reasoning visible. A closer linguistic relationship can make a comparison more relevant, but translation and multilingualism complicate simple rules; a large chronological gap can be mitigated by evidence of a credible chain of transmission; and geographic closeness does not establish contact between every writer in the same region. Cultural resemblance requires definition too: a broad label such as “ancient Near Eastern” may cover centuries and places with different institutions. The reader should identify what is known on each axis and distinguish missing evidence from evidence against a proposed connection.

Genre supplies a further control. A royal inscription, a court document, and a legal collection may all mention the same ruler while serving different purposes. The inscription may celebrate a public achievement; a court document may preserve an individual dispute; a legal collection may arrange hypothetical cases for purposes that scholars debate. To compare the language of justice across these witnesses is reasonable, but the comparison must account for differences in audience, conventions, and preservation. A royal claim of justice cannot be treated automatically as an impartial statistical account of outcomes in court. Likewise, silence in a surviving court record does not prove that an event never occurred. Sources are selected and preserved unevenly, so the visible record is not a complete census of ancient life.16

Younger illustrates his method with a curse involving bread baked in one oven. He compares several West Semitic inscriptions with Leviticus 26:26 and observes that both the common motif and the different outcomes deserve attention. On his analysis, the texts do not justify identifying one surviving inscription as the direct source of the biblical passage, nor do they by themselves settle its date.17 This is a model of an argument with an explicitly limited conclusion. Students can evaluate whether the evidence supports Younger’s account of a shared stock of imagery without assuming that every similarity must either establish copying or mean nothing. A comparison can illuminate the range of a literary convention while leaving the history of a particular composition open.

歷史比較的起點，並不是先假定聖經作者抄用了某份古代文獻，而是釐清比較對象究竟是詞語、句式次序、法律問題、社會制度，還是更廣泛的觀念。不同類型的相似，只能支持不同程度的結論。共同的社會問題可以幫助我們理解古代社會可能如何回應，卻不能單憑這一點證明文本之間曾有直接接觸。緊密的詞語與次序相似可以增加文學關係假說的說服力，但傳遞路徑與年代仍須另行論證。比較研究也必須如實記錄差異，並追問被比較的材料是否可能為受考察的群體所接觸。01

K. Lawson Younger Jr. 建議從語言、地理、年代與文化四個面向評估可能的平行例子。15 這並不是只要機械地填完四個方格，就能證明文學依賴；它是一種使推理過程可見的方法。語言較接近可能增加比較的相關性，但翻譯與多語環境使簡單規則變得複雜；年代差距很大時，可靠的傳統傳遞鏈可能增加比較的意義；地理位置接近，亦不能證明同一地區的每位作者都曾互相接觸。文化相似也需要清楚界定：籠統的「古代近東」標籤可能涵蓋數百年及制度各異的地方。讀者應說明每個面向已有甚麼資料，並區分「證據缺失」與「有證據反對某種聯繫」。

文類提供另一項控制。同一位君王可能同時出現在王室銘文、法庭文件與法律彙編中，但這些材料的目的各不相同。銘文可能頌揚公開成就；法庭文件可能保存個別糾紛；法律彙編可能依照某種目的排列假設個案，而學者對此目的仍有爭論。比較這些見證中有關公義的語言是合理的，但必須說明受眾、寫作慣例及保存情況的差異。君王自稱施行公義，不能自動當作法庭判決結果的中立統計；同樣，現存法庭紀錄沒有提及某事，也不能證明它從未發生。材料的選擇與保存並不平均，因此可見的紀錄並不是古代生活的完整普查。16

Younger 以多人在一個爐中烤餅的咒詛作為方法示例，將幾篇西閃語銘文與利未記二十六章二十六節比較，指出共同的主題和不同的結果都值得留意。按照他的分析，這些文本不足以證明某一份現存銘文是聖經段落的直接來源，也不能單憑比較確定該段經文的年代。17 這是一個明確限制結論範圍的論證範例。學生可以評估證據是否支持 Younger 所提出的共同意象傳統，而毋須先假定每一個相似處不是證明抄襲就是毫無意義。比較可以照亮某種文學慣例的可能範圍，同時讓個別作品的形成歷史保持開放。

02 · COMPARATIVE HORIZONS 02 · 歷史比較視野

Ancient Near Eastern law and the goring ox 古代近東法律與觸人的牛

● COMPARATIVE PRINCIPLE 比較研究原則

A legal parallel can illuminate form without proving literary dependence. 法律平行例子能照亮形式，但不能單獨證明文學依賴。

Exodus 21:28–32 and cuneiform legal collections both address injury caused by a goring ox. The returned source excerpt identifies this as a particularly fruitful comparison and points to J. J. Finkelstein’s detailed study.02 An interpreter can compare the sequence of conditions, the owner's prior knowledge, and the consequences prescribed in each text. This procedure is more informative than announcing that “ancient law says” one thing: individual cases and collections require separate attention. The close formulations call for an explanation, but the excerpt by itself does not establish which document influenced which, or whether a shared legal tradition lies behind them.

The genre of the collections is itself debated. A retrieved excerpt presents an interpretation in which legal collections express judicial wisdom rather than operating like a modern statutory code.03 That proposal should be attributed to its advocates rather than presented as an uncontested description of every ancient court. Literary form, surviving court records, local custom, and the role of instruction are related questions, but one should not silently substitute evidence for one question as proof of all the others.

The goring-ox example can be made more precise by examining the ancient comparative witness itself. In Martha Roth's translation of the Laws of Eshnunna, section 53 addresses an ox that kills another ox and divides the value of the living animal and the carcass between the owners. Sections 54–55 then specify a situation in which an ox has a known propensity to gore, local authorities have notified its owner, and the owner fails to control it: the stated financial consequences vary depending on whether the person killed is free or enslaved.10 The same collection goes on to discuss a dangerous dog and a collapsing wall. That sequence helps the reader see patterns of notice, control, and consequences across several cases without supposing that the collection addresses every imaginable circumstance.

Place those cases alongside Exodus 21:28–32, but do not rush from formal likeness to a claim that the Hebrew text copied the surviving Eshnunna tablet. At least four questions need separate answers: do the texts describe the same practical problem; how do they organize the conditional cases; what consequences do they specify; and what historical mechanism would make one formulation available to another community? A comparison may answer the first three questions in detail while leaving the fourth open. It should also attend to differences in the valuation of human beings, the status of the animal, and the role of prior warning. A historical judgment that a legal formulation is related to another tradition is stronger when it explains the differences, rather than treating them as inconvenient noise.

Roth introduces the Eshnunna collection as preserved in school-exercise texts and notes its relationship to a broader scribal-legal tradition.11 This matters for historical imagination: preservation and copying in educational settings offer one possible kind of transmission, but do not themselves prove that a particular Israelite writer encountered this copy. Nor does the existence of a legal text automatically tell us how a judge decided a particular lawsuit. The status of the collection, its scribal setting, and evidence from court documents should be investigated together. The methodological gain is not merely the discovery that another ancient society had laws; it is the ability to ask better, more specific questions of each text.

The financial distinctions in Eshnunna §§54–55 must be described without endorsing the hierarchy that they encode. The translation distinguishes a free man's death from the death of an enslaved person and assigns different sums.22 That distinction is evidence about an ancient text's valuation and social categories, not a neutral measure of the persons' human worth. A modern reader should be attentive both to the historical setting and to the ethical difficulty of the formulation. Comparison with biblical law can expose similarities and differences in status and liability; it should not be used either to conceal troubling features of one text or to turn the other society into a caricature. Each ancient formulation deserves accurate reading before a theological or moral judgment is offered.

Notice also how the source's physical history qualifies what can be known. Roth identifies school exercises as the surviving witnesses and explains that no literary prologue or epilogue is preserved for this composition.23 A surviving copy may tell us what a scribe copied, yet its existence does not automatically reveal every stage through which the composition passed. The comparative question can be made more precise by separating the date of the surviving tablet, a proposed date of the composition, the date of a biblical passage's composition or redaction, and the date at which a modern edition reconstructs the text. These are different dates, established by different evidence. Uncertainty about one of them does not make comparison useless, but it does restrict the conclusions that follow.

The biblical interpreter should also avoid the assumption that a comparative case has only one possible explanatory function. A similar case can illuminate legal form, the social significance of a problem, or the range of ancient responses. It may suggest a line of inquiry into transmission, but the latter requires an independent investigation. The distinction can be expressed as a question of burden of proof: “Does another document address an owner warned about a dangerous animal?” is answered by reading the translated Eshnunna sections.24 “Did the author of Exodus use this very witness?” is a much stronger question. It cannot be answered merely by restating the similarity. The difference between those questions makes clear why careful comparison is productive even when a historical dependence remains unproved.

An exercise in comparative legal reading can start with a question narrower than the history of an entire legal system: how does each case treat advance notice? In Roth's translation, the Eshnunna rule about a dangerous ox explicitly mentions notice from local authorities.37 The rule then turns on the owner's failure to control the animal and on a fatal outcome.38 The next section changes the status of the victim and states a different sum.39 The following pair of cases concerns a dog, again combining notice, failure to control the animal, and a fatal bite.40 A further case concerns notice of a dangerous wall and subsequent collapse.41 The recurrence of notice invites comparison, but it should not obscure the different outcomes and social categories stated in the cases. At the same time, the arrangement provides a practical reason for reading the clauses in sequence: the text's way of varying a problem may be as informative as any individual rule.42 When Exodus is brought into the exercise, make a parallel table with columns for warning, agency, injury, social status, and consequence, and mark a blank where a text does not supply the detail. The table is a research tool; it is not a substitute for reading each passage in context, and a blank must not be filled from assumptions about what an ancient society “must have” believed.43

出埃及記二十一章二十八至三十二節和楔形文字法律彙編都處理公牛觸人的傷害。檢索所得的原文摘錄指出，這是值得仔細比較的例子，並引介 J. J. Finkelstein 的專題研究。02 讀者可以逐項比較各文本中的條件次序、主人是否事先知情，以及個案所規定的後果。這種分析比籠統宣稱「古代法律」只有一種看法更有意義：不同法律彙編及個案都需要分開考察。相近的表述需要解釋；然而，這段摘錄本身並不能證明哪份文獻影響了另一份，或它們是否共享某個法律傳統。

法律彙編的文類本身亦有爭議。一段檢索摘錄提出，法律彙編表達的是司法智慧，而其運作並不等同現代成文法典。03 這是應當歸屬於其倡議者的解釋，不宜當成對所有古代法庭皆無爭議的描述。文學形式、留存的訴訟紀錄、地方習俗，以及教導的角色固然互相關聯，但不能不加說明地把某一方面的證據當成其他問題的確證。

公牛觸人的例子若直接閱讀古代比較文本，便能更為具體。按照 Martha Roth 所譯的《以實嫩那法律》第五十三條，公牛觸死另一頭牛時，兩名主人分配活牛的價值與死牛的屍體。第五十四至五十五條接著處理另一種情況：公牛有觸人的習性，地方官員已通知主人，而主人沒有控制牠；若牛觸死人，規定的金錢後果會因死者是自由人或奴隸而有差異。10 同一彙編隨後討論危險的狗與倒塌的牆。這個次序使讀者能夠跨越數個個案觀察事先通知、控制及後果之間的模式，卻不必假設該彙編已涵蓋一切可能情況。

可以把這些個案與出埃及記二十一章二十八至三十二節並置，但不應由形式相似立即跳到「希伯來文文本抄用了現存的以實嫩那泥版」這個結論。至少有四個問題需要分開回答：兩者是否討論同一實際問題？條件個案如何排列？各自規定甚麼後果？又有何歷史機制可能使一個群體接觸到另一種表述？比較可以詳細回答前三個問題，仍然保留第四題未決。研究者亦應留意文本如何評價不同身分的人、如何處置牲畜，以及事先警告有何作用。若某項歷史判斷認為兩種法律表述有關，其論證能同時解釋差異，而不是把差異當作無關雜訊，說服力便更強。

Roth 介紹這份以實嫩那彙編時指出，它保存在學校習作文本之中，並與更廣泛的書吏法律傳統有關。11 這一點有助於歷史想像：教學環境中的保存與抄寫，提供一種可能的傳遞方式；然而，這本身並不證明某位以色列作者接觸過現存的這份抄本。法律文本的存在，也不會自動告訴我們法官如何裁決某一具體訴訟。彙編的性質、書吏環境與法庭文書的證據，需要一同考察。這項方法的收穫，不只是發現另一個古代社會也有法律，更是學會向每一份文本提出更精確、更恰當的問題。

描述《以實嫩那法律》第五十四至五十五條的金錢差別時，不能因此認可條文所呈現的階層秩序。譯文區分自由男子與奴隸的死亡，並列出不同金額。22 這個區分是古代文本如何評價人及劃分社會身分的證據，而不是對人之價值所作的中立衡量。現代讀者既應注意歷史處境，也應正視文字所帶來的倫理難題。與聖經法律比較，可以顯出身分和責任上的相似與差異；但不能藉此掩蓋某一文本令人不安之處，也不能把另一個社會變成漫畫式的反面形象。在提出神學或道德判斷之前，每項古代表述都值得先被準確閱讀。

材料本身的保存歷史也會限制我們所能知道的事。Roth 指出，現存見證是學校習作，這份作品亦沒有保存文學性的序言或結語。23 一份現存抄本可以告訴我們書吏抄寫了甚麼，卻不會自動揭示作品傳遞所經過的每一階段。要使比較問題更精確，應區分現存泥版的年代、作品可能形成的年代、某段聖經經文形成或編修的年代，以及現代版本重建文本的時間。這些是不同的日期，需要不同的證據。對其中一項日期存疑，並不表示比較毫無用處，卻會限制我們可以得出的結論。

解經者也應避免假定，一個比較個案只能有一種解釋功能。相似個案可以照亮法律形式、某個問題在社會上的重要性，或古代社會可能採取的多種回應。它可能提示研究者追問傳遞關係，但後者需要獨立考察。兩者的差別也可用舉證責任來表達：「另一份文件是否討論過主人事先被告知其牲畜危險的情況？」閱讀《以實嫩那法律》的相關譯文就可回答。24 「出埃及記作者是否使用過這份特定見證？」卻是強得多的問題，不能只靠重述相似之處回答。釐清這兩個問題，也就能明白為何即使歷史依賴尚未證實，嚴謹比較仍有生產力。

比較法律閱讀可以從比整套法律制度歷史更狹窄的問題開始：每個個案如何處理事先通知？在 Roth 的譯文中，《以實嫩那法律》關於危險公牛的條文明確提到地方官員通知主人。37 其後的規定取決於主人未能控制牲畜，以及是否發生致命結果。38 下一條改變受害者的身分，並列出不同金額。39 隨後兩條轉而討論狗，同樣結合了通知、未能控制牲畜與致命咬傷。40 另一條則提及危險牆壁的通知及其後倒塌。41 通知的重複出現值得比較，但不應因此忽略各個案所寫明的不同結果與社會身分。同時，這樣的編排提供按次序閱讀條文的實際理由：文本如何改變問題中的條件，可能與任何單獨規定一樣有啟發性。42 把出埃及記帶入練習時，可製作平行表，分欄記錄警告、行動者、傷害、社會身分及後果，並在文本沒有提供細節之處留空。這個表格是研究工具，不能代替在上下文中閱讀各段文字；也不能根據古代社會「必然」持有甚麼想法的假設，填補空格。43

03 · COMPARATIVE HORIZONS 03 · 歷史比較視野

Second Temple texts and the acts of deliverance 第二聖殿文本與拯救作為

● COMPARATIVE PRINCIPLE 比較研究原則

A shared cluster of expectations does not by itself establish direct contact. 共同的期待組合本身不能證明直接接觸。

The discovery of a text in the Judaean desert can establish that an interpretation was possible within a particular ancient Jewish setting. It cannot by itself establish that a New Testament author read that manuscript, belonged to the group that preserved it, or shared all of its beliefs. The *Messianic Apocalypse* (4Q521) offers an instructive exercise in making those distinctions. In fragment 2, column II, the manuscript speaks of heaven and earth listening to God's anointed and then sets out a series of divine acts. The surviving lines associate the Lord with freeing prisoners, giving sight to the blind, healing the wounded, making the dead live, and announcing good news to the meek.06 The physical damage and the brackets in a scholarly translation matter: an interpreter must not silently treat a reconstructed word as if it were preserved without damage. The translation consulted here is evidence for a possible reading of the text, not a substitute for a critical edition of the Hebrew manuscript.

Matthew 11:2–6 has its own literary setting. John, in prison, asks whether Jesus is the coming one or whether another should be expected. Jesus directs John's messengers to report what they hear and see: the blind recover sight, the lame walk, those with skin disease are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor receive good news. The Greek phrases τυφλοὶ ἀναβλέπουσιν, νεκροὶ ἐγείρονται, and πτωχοὶ εὐαγγελίζονται give three points for a precise comparison, rather than a vague claim that both texts concern miracles.07 The Matthean list also concludes with a blessing on the person who does not take offense at Jesus. Its argumentative function is therefore bound to John's question and the difficulty of recognizing Jesus, not merely to the inventory of extraordinary events.

The overlapping cluster is historically significant because it shows that language about healing, life, and good news could be assembled in another Jewish composition in association with divine deliverance and an anointed figure. Yet the overlap does not demonstrate a direct literary route from 4Q521 to Matthew. Both passages may draw upon scriptural traditions also known independently; the fragmentary state of the scroll and the different literary contexts leave open several explanations. The actors must also be distinguished: the scroll's surviving sequence describes what the Lord will do, while Matthew places the reported works in Jesus' answer to John. The theological significance of that juxtaposition is an exegetical question, not permission to flatten the two documents into identical messianic programs.

A seminar exercise should proceed in separate stages. First, mark every item in Matthew's list and every recoverable item in the scroll, recording both correspondences and omissions. Second, distinguish the manuscript's readable text from restorations and from the translator's decisions. Third, ask what each list does within its own document: the addressee, the announced agent, the temporal horizon, and the rhetorical question differ. Fourth, investigate the relevant scriptural passages in their own contexts before proposing a common inheritance. Finally, describe the conclusion at the strength the evidence warrants: the scroll offers a historically relevant analogy for a cluster of hopes; direct dependence and the identity of every intended allusion require additional evidence. A responsible account may be illuminating while still leaving the route of transmission unresolved.

The placement of “his anointed” near the beginning of the scroll fragment is important, but proximity alone cannot settle every question about agency. The translated sequence names the Lord repeatedly, and the acts of freeing, healing, and restoring life appear in a passage that attributes remarkable action to the Lord.20 One should therefore distinguish what a text says about the anointed figure from what it says about the divine acts associated with the setting. It is a further interpretive step to claim that the figure personally performs every listed action. A study of the Hebrew grammar and the available manuscript fragments would be required before deciding such questions more narrowly. The distinction becomes especially useful when students compare translations or read arguments that use the phrase “messianic expectations” as though every ancient Jewish source presented the same figure and program.

The ancient witness also has a material history. A fragment preserves only what survives; gaps do not license the reader to fill an entire historical narrative with confident guesses. The printed translation's editorial brackets make the difference between surviving words and proposed restoration visible. The same edition provides translations of many compositions and manuscript copies, which allows a student to locate the text in a larger collection while preserving its particular designation, 4Q521.21 A page reference to an English edition is useful for finding the discussion, but a research paper making a detailed linguistic claim should also cite the fragment, column, and line and inspect a suitable edition of the original text. Those identifiers are not ornamental: they allow another reader to find precisely the evidence on which the argument depends.

The opening of Matthew's scene reports John's imprisonment and his hearing of the works of the Messiah; his question follows before Jesus names the works.25 That sequence matters because the list answers an explicit question in a narrative, rather than appearing as a free-standing catalogue. The reply instructs the messengers to report what they hear and see.26 Its list includes people who are blind, lame, affected by skin disease, deaf, dead, or poor; these items should be compared as a sequence rather than extracted into an undifferentiated claim about miracles.27 In the scroll translation, the assurance that the Lord observes the devout and calls the just precedes the list of deliverances.28 The report that the Lord acts for prisoners and blind persons appears before the announcement of healing, restoration of the dead, and good news to the meek.29 These distinct arrangements invite questions about each passage's rhetorical movement. The conclusion is a methodological one: both documents are evidence of historically situated religious language, but neither document's internal narrative should be erased in order to make their wording coincide.30

第二聖殿時期的猶太曠野文獻，可以證明某種詮釋在特定古代猶太處境中確曾存在；然而，單靠一份出土手稿，不能證明某位新約作者曾閱讀它、屬於保存它的群體，或接受該群體的所有信念。所謂《彌賽亞啟示錄》（4Q521）正好適合用來練習這些區分。第二殘片第二欄提到天地聆聽上帝的受膏者，隨後列出一系列神聖作為。現存文句將釋放囚犯、使盲人得見、醫治受傷者、使死人復生，以及向謙卑的人報佳音與主相連。06 手稿破損及學術譯本中的方括號不是可以忽略的排版：研究者不應把重建的字詞，悄悄當作完好保存的文字。此處查閱的譯本可用來理解文本的一種讀法，但不能取代希伯來文手稿的批判校訂本。

馬太福音十一章二至六節也有本身的文學處境。被囚的約翰問耶穌是否那位將要來的，還是應當等候另一位。耶穌吩咐使者把所聽見、所看見的報告給約翰：盲人得見，瘸子行走，患皮膚病的人得潔淨，聾人聽見，死人復活，貧窮人聽見福音。希臘文的 τυφλοὶ ἀναβλέπουσιν、νεκροὶ ἐγείρονται 和 πτωχοὶ εὐαγγελίζονται 提供三個可精確比較的項目，而不是籠統說兩段文字都提到神蹟。07 馬太的清單以不因耶穌跌倒之人有福作結；因此，這段話在論證上的作用，與約翰的問題及辨認耶穌身分的困難緊密相連，不能只當成奇事目錄。

這組相同主題具有歷史意義，因為它顯示另一份猶太作品也能把醫治、生命與佳音的語言組合起來，並與神的拯救及受膏者聯繫。然而，這些相似並不能證明馬太直接使用了 4Q521。兩者都可能各自運用共同熟悉的聖經傳統；死海古卷的殘缺狀態以及兩段不同的文學處境，均使多種解釋仍然可能。行動者亦必須加以區分：古卷現存的序列描述主將要作的事，而馬太則把所報告的作為放在耶穌對約翰的回答之中。兩者並列的神學意義有待進一步釋經；它並不容許我們把兩份文獻壓平成完全相同的彌賽亞計畫。

神學院課堂可以分階段進行比較。首先，逐一標記馬太清單的項目和古卷中可辨認的內容，同時記錄相符及缺席之處。其次，區分手稿可讀的字、重建文字與譯者的選擇。再次，考察每份文獻中的清單實際發揮甚麼作用：受眾、被宣告的行動者、時間視野與修辭問題並不完全相同。第四，在提出共同傳統之前，先考查相關聖經段落本身的上下文。最後，按照證據所容許的強度陳述結論：古卷為一組盼望提供歷史上相關的類比；直接依賴關係及每個暗示的確切來源，則需要更多證據。即使傳遞路徑仍未確定，這樣的比較仍可帶來有價值的洞見。

古卷殘片開首附近出現「他的受膏者」，這一位置十分重要；然而，字句接近本身不能解決所有有關行動者的問題。譯文所呈現的序列多次提及主；釋放、醫治及使人復生等作為出現在一段把奇妙行動歸給主的文字之中。20 因此，研究者應區分文本對受膏者所說的內容，與它對相關處境中神聖作為的描述。若要宣稱受膏者親自執行清單上的每一個行動，就已踏出另一個需要論證的詮釋步驟。要更精確地裁定這類問題，必須研究希伯來文語法與現存手稿殘片。當學生比較不同譯本，或讀到有人使用「彌賽亞期待」一詞，彷彿所有古代猶太資料都呈現同一人物及同一計畫時，這個區分便特別有用。

古代見證也有其物質歷史。殘片所保存的只是仍然存在的內容；空缺並不容許讀者以自信的猜測填補一整段歷史敘事。印刷譯本的編輯方括號，使現存字句與所建議的補文之間的差別可見。同一譯本收錄多份作品和手稿抄本，使學生能把此文本放在更大的文獻集合內，同時保留它獨特的編號 4Q521。21 英譯版的頁碼有助於尋找材料；然而，提出詳細語言學主張的研究論文，也應註明殘片、欄及行，並查閱合適的原文校訂本。這些識別標記不是裝飾，而是讓其他讀者能夠準確找到論證所依據的證據。

馬太敘事的開端報告約翰被囚，並聽見彌賽亞的作為；他先提出問題，耶穌才列舉這些作為。25 次序之所以重要，是因為清單回應了敘事中的明確提問，而不是獨立出現的一份目錄。耶穌吩咐使者報告所聽見、所看見的事。26 清單包括盲人、瘸子、患皮膚病的人、聾人、死人及貧窮人；應按照其排列次序比較，而非把各項抽離並混成一個籠統的神蹟概念。27 在古卷譯文中，主看顧虔誠者並呼喚義人的保證，出現在拯救作為的清單之前。28 主為囚犯與盲人行動的記述，又先於醫治、使死人復生和向謙卑者報佳音的宣告。29 這些不同安排促使我們追問每段文字本身的修辭進展。方法上的結論是：兩份文獻都見證有其歷史處境的宗教語言，但不能為了使措辭看似一致，就抹去任何一份文獻內部的敘事脈絡。30

04 · COMPARATIVE HORIZONS 04 · 歷史比較視野

Community rules and rabbinic chronology 團體規章與拉比文獻的年代

● COMPARATIVE PRINCIPLE 比較研究原則

A text’s date and the age of a tradition within it are separate questions. 文本的年代與其中傳統的年代是兩個不同問題。

The opening of the Community Rule supplies a second kind of comparative evidence. In the translation of 1QS I 1–18, those entering the community seek God, pursue justice, commit themselves to revealed ordinances, and bring their knowledge, strength, and resources into the community.08 That evidence is more concrete than the assertion that all ancient Jews understood covenant or purity identically. It represents the rhetoric and stipulations of a particular rule; it does not establish that every person who encountered the document obeyed it perfectly, or that every manuscript from the caves came from one uniform social group. A rule states an ideal and constructs boundaries. Reconstructing actual practice requires other kinds of evidence, and divergent copies should be considered individually.

When comparison turns to rabbinic literature, the date of a written collection and the possible age of an individual tradition must be kept separate. A later compilation may preserve older material, but an attribution to an earlier teacher is a historical claim to investigate, not a guarantee that the wording predates the compilation. Conversely, a later date does not render a tradition useless: it remains direct evidence for the discourse of the work in which it stands, and, under appropriate controls, may raise questions about earlier developments. The volume *The New Testament and Rabbinic Literature* explicitly brings textual criticism, redaction, attribution, and the historical use of rabbinic texts into conversation. Its introduction notes that texts were edited and transmitted over extended periods and warns against assuming that the recovery of a text immediately yields a transparent history of Jewish life.09

The practical question is not simply, “Is there a parallel?” Ask which version is being cited, in which collection the passage occurs, how the unit is framed, whether its attribution differs in another witness, and what independent evidence might support an earlier date. An argument that places a rabbinic passage beside a Gospel without these steps can mistake later reception for first-century background. The opposite error is to treat the rabbinic record as a negligible afterthought and overlook the ways it preserves Jewish interpretation deserving of attention on its own terms. Both errors are avoided by specifying the historical claim and the evidence needed to sustain it. Such care also prevents the use of a single source as a foil for an invented, uniform Judaism.

The opening of 1QS describes seeking God and doing what is good and just as part of the community's stated purpose.31 Its account invokes instruction through Moses and the prophets, then speaks of choosing good and distancing oneself from evil.32 It welcomes volunteers into a covenantal community and describes the sharing of knowledge, energies, and resources.33 Those details show why one should read the document as a particular program of formation and membership, not simply as an abstract list of “Jewish beliefs.” Its terminology of light and darkness also makes boundaries and collective identity central to the text.34 The same published translation offers separate treatments of different Community Rule copies and warns against constructing a composite text without sufficient grounds.35 The historical inference should be proportionate: one can analyze the rhetoric of a surviving copy and compare other copies, while remaining cautious about the exact relationship between a textual norm and the actions of every member.36 This approach gives the scrolls their own voice and prevents them from becoming a convenient mirror in which the interpreter sees only a later controversy.

《團體規章》的開篇提供另一類比較證據。按照 1QS 第一欄一至十八行的譯文，進入團體的人尋求上帝、追求公義、委身於啟示的規條，並將知識、力量及財物帶進團體。08 這比宣稱所有古代猶太人對聖約或潔淨均有相同理解，更具體也更有根據。它反映特定規章的修辭與規定；它並不能證明每一位接觸該文獻的人都完全遵守規條，也不能證明洞穴中所有手稿都來自單一而整齊劃一的社群。規章表達理想並劃定界線；若要重建實際行為，還須其他種類的證據，並且需要逐一考慮不同抄本。

當比較對象轉向拉比文獻時，必須區分書面彙編成書的日期與個別傳統可能形成的年代。較晚的彙編有可能保存早期材料；但是，把傳統歸給某位早期教師，本身是一項需要考察的歷史聲稱，並不保證現存措辭必定早於彙編。反過來說，日期較晚也不代表傳統毫無價值：它仍然直接見證其所屬著作的論述，而且在適當的證據控制之下，可以引發有關早期發展的問題。《新約與拉比文獻》一書明確把文本批判、編修、傳統歸屬及拉比文本的歷史運用放在一起討論。其導論指出，文本經過長時間編輯與傳遞，並提醒讀者不要假定只要恢復某一文本，就能立即透明地重建猶太生活史。09

實際要問的不只是「有沒有平行例子」。研究者還應問：引用的是哪個版本？該段落屬於哪一部彙編？編者如何安排其上下文？另一見證中的歸屬是否相同？有甚麼獨立證據支持較早的年代？若把一段拉比文獻直接放在福音書旁邊，卻跳過這些步驟，就可能把後期接受史誤當成第一世紀的背景。相反的錯誤，是把拉比文獻視為無足輕重的附帶資料，忽略其所保存的猶太詮釋本身值得認真研究。只要明確說明所提出的歷史聲稱，以及支持它所需的證據，就可以避免這兩種錯誤；這種謹慎亦能防止以單一材料建構一個虛假的、整齊劃一的猶太教作為反襯。

1QS 的開篇把尋求上帝、行良善與公義，描述為團體所宣告目的的一部分。31 它提及透過摩西與眾先知所傳的教導，接著談到選擇良善及遠離邪惡。32 它歡迎自願者進入聖約團體，也描述知識、力量和財物的分享。33 這些細節說明，為何我們應把該文獻讀作一套特定的群體塑造與成員身分計畫，而不是抽象的「猶太信念」清單。它使用光明與黑暗的語言，也使界線與群體身分成為文本的核心問題。34 同一譯本分別處理不同的《團體規章》抄本，並提醒讀者不可在根據不足時拼合出一個綜合文本。35 歷史推論應與證據相稱：研究者可以分析現存抄本的修辭並比較其他抄本，同時對文本規範與每名成員實際行為之間的確切關係保持謹慎。36 這種方法讓古卷保有自己的聲音，而不是使它成為一面方便的鏡子，讓詮釋者只看到後期爭論的投影。

05 · COMPARATIVE HORIZONS 05 · 歷史比較視野

Civic benefaction and historical identification 公共捐助與歷史身分辨認

● COMPARATIVE PRINCIPLE 比較研究原則

An inscription and a letter can be compared without assuming their namesakes are identical. 比較銘文與書信時，不應先假設同名人物必為同一人。

An inscription from Corinth offers an example of civic expenditure that can be discussed independently of its possible connection with a biblical person. A surviving pavement inscription records an Erastus and describes paving carried out at his own expense in relation to an aedileship. The object is public and local: the location, surviving letter traces, wording, date proposed for the pavement, and archaeological context each contribute different kinds of evidence. A site guide presents a first-century date and suggests that this Erastus may be the man named in Romans 16:23.12 The difference between the inscription's information and the guide's proposed identification must remain visible in the interpreter's notes.

Romans calls its Erastus ὁ οἰκονόμος τῆς πόλεως, “the city's steward” or an official responsible for civic administration.13 It does not mention a pavement or explicitly call him an aedile. The name, proposed date, geographic setting, and compatibility of offices all matter to the identification; each needs a separate argument. Even if the two figures were the same person, the inscription would not automatically describe the economic circumstances of every member of Paul's communities. The source can help make a hypothesis historically concrete, but it cannot carry all the social conclusions sometimes placed upon it. Nor does a general patronage model establish the motive of this particular donor without evidence from the inscription and its context.

The case shows why social-scientific models are most useful as disciplined questions. What kind of public recognition might a benefaction generate? Which office, inscription, or local practice provides evidence for the answer? Who is visible in the surviving record, and whose labor or perspective is missing? Inscriptions often preserve the voices of people able to finance public display; a lack of comparable inscriptions by poorer residents does not establish that they lacked agency. Bringing literary, material, and social evidence together makes an interpretation richer only when the different evidential limits remain explicit.

The inscription is also a useful reminder that an object has more than one audience. A modern visitor may encounter it because it is associated with Paul, but the ancient paving had a civic location and a public statement of its own. Before discussing a possible connection to Romans, the student should describe the object as material evidence: what survives, how the lettering relates to the surface, where the pavement lies, and what the inscription says about its sponsor.12 These observations can then be placed beside the letter's closing greetings, where Erastus is named together with other individuals.13 The letter's social network and the inscription's civic setting are related questions only after an argument for identification has been made. Such a sequence does not diminish the archaeological find; it helps protect the find from becoming merely an illustration of an interpretation already assumed. It also permits a meaningful conclusion even if the namesake remains unidentified: public expenditure, civic office, and social recognition are themselves subjects for historical inquiry.

哥林多的一段銘文提供了公共支出的例子；即使暫時擱置它是否與聖經人物有關，仍可獨立討論。現存的鋪路銘文記載一位以拉都，並把他自費鋪路與市政官職相連。這件物證具有公共性與地方性：地點、現存字母痕跡、文字、研究者提出的鋪路年代，以及考古上下文，分別提供不同類型的證據。一部遺址指南提出第一世紀的年代，並認為這位以拉都可能與羅馬書十六章二十三節的人物相同。12 解經筆記必須保留銘文實際提供的資訊與指南提出的身分判斷之間的區別。

羅馬書稱其中的以拉都為 ὁ οἰκονόμος τῆς πόλεως，即城內的管事或負責城市行政的官員。13 經文沒有提到鋪路，也沒有明確稱他為市政官。姓名、所提出的年代、地理處境及兩種職務是否相容，都與身分判定有關；每一點都須分開論證。即使這兩人確是同一人，該銘文也不會自動說明保羅各教會每個成員的經濟情況。物證能使歷史假說更加具體，卻不能承擔所有有時被加在它上面的社會結論。同樣，若缺少銘文及其上下文的支持，一般的 patronage（庇護與依附）模式也不能證明這位捐助者的個別動機。

此例顯示，社會科學模型最有用之處，在於它能引導有紀律的提問。捐助可能帶來哪一類公共承認？甚麼官職、銘文或地方慣例能支持答案？現存紀錄讓誰的聲音可見，又遺漏了誰的勞動或觀點？銘文往往保存有能力資助公共展示者的聲音；較貧困居民沒有留下相應銘文，並不證明他們沒有行動能力。只有在清楚交代不同證據的界限時，文學、物質與社會資料的結合，才真正能使詮釋更豐富。

這段銘文也提醒我們，一件物證可能面向不止一類受眾。現代參觀者可能因它與保羅有關而接觸它，但古代的鋪路本身位於城邦空間，並帶有自己的公開陳述。在討論它與羅馬書可能的關係之前，學生應先把它描述為物質證據：現存甚麼、字母與地面表面如何相連、鋪路位於何處，以及銘文如何描述捐助者。12 接著才把這些觀察與書信結尾的問安並列；在那裏，以拉都與其他人物一同被提名。13 只有在提出身分辨認的論證之後，書信的社會網絡與銘文的城邦處境才成為彼此相關的問題。這種次序不會降低考古發現的價值；相反，它有助於防止發現淪為既定詮釋的插圖。即使仍不能確認是否同一人，研究仍可得到有意義的結論：公共支出、市政官職及社會承認本身都是值得探究的歷史課題。

06 · COMPARATIVE HORIZONS 06 · 歷史比較視野

Imperial cult and local historical claims 皇帝崇拜與地方歷史的論證

● COMPARATIVE PRINCIPLE 比較研究原則

A plausible historical setting is not automatically the text’s certain referent. 合理的歷史處境並不自動成為經文確定的指涉。

An account of Roman Asia describes imperial cult as part of a reciprocal network linking provincial communities and imperial power. Cities and emperors could both receive honor through temples, rituals, and official recognition.04 The same account draws on S. R. F. Price to argue that religious practices and political relations need not be reduced to one another. The historian should therefore ask who sponsored a ritual, where it occurred, what participants did, and how the relevant city represented its own status. A model drawn from one province or century is a question to test against local evidence, not a ready-made description of every community.

The phrase “Satan's throne” in Revelation 2:13 illustrates why historical setting does not automatically settle a text's referent. One retrieved account favors Pergamum's great altar of Zeus and considers the healing sanctuary of Asclepius another possibility; another gives imperial cult a prominent place while allowing additional associations.05 These are competing readings of an underdetermined expression. The text itself does not name the monument. Responsible interpretation should present the alternatives and their evidential limits instead of identifying one site as an established fact.

An interpreter of a New Testament letter may be tempted to treat “the imperial cult” as a single institution imposed everywhere in exactly the same form. That phrase is more useful as a starting point for questions than as a complete historical description. A city's festivals, its honors for a ruler, the persons who sponsored buildings, and the inscriptions through which a city advertised its status are particular pieces of evidence. Their dates and settings matter. The existence of a temple at one location does not show that every inhabitant participated in the same way, and a provincial pattern does not eliminate differences between cities. The evidence also concerns more than internal belief: buildings, public ritual, and honorific language formed part of relationships among rulers, local leaders, and communities.14

Revelation 2:13 illustrates both the value and the limits of that historical investigation. The expression “Satan's throne” invites the reader to consider the city of Pergamum and the forces represented by the image, but the wording does not identify a named structure. A proposal associating the phrase with a temple or altar therefore requires an argument that connects the city's material evidence with the rhetoric of the letter. One can compare a proposed monument's prominence, the chronology of its construction or use, and its relevance to the surrounding discussion of faithful witness. One must then ask whether rival explanations make equally good sense of those observations, and whether the expression could evoke more than a single building. An evocative site is not the same thing as a settled referent.

For classroom work, construct an evidence table with four columns: the textual wording, the proposed historical referent, the source supporting the proposal, and the unresolved question. An inscription may prove that a title was used at a place; it does not necessarily prove that the author of Revelation intended to allude to that inscription. A building can demonstrate a city's architectural investment; its presence alone does not establish what a particular congregation experienced there. These distinctions keep historical research from becoming a collection of colorful illustrations. They also allow a reader to acknowledge a serious historical possibility without presenting a disputed identification as the necessary meaning of the text.

The teacher can then ask students to reverse the direction of the exercise. Instead of beginning with a modern theory and searching for illustrations, begin with the features of an ancient document that the theory does not explain. For the Pergamum example, a model focused entirely on imperial institutions needs to account for the city's other religious sites; a model focused on one altar needs to explain why the text should be read so narrowly. Hemer and Thompson give the class two materially different scholarly proposals to test, while neither author's conclusion should be treated as immune from further evidence.18 The point is not that all hypotheses are equally probable. It is that the reasons for ranking them should be available to other readers for examination.

The same discipline applies when a comparison seems especially helpful for theological interpretation. One may ask how a Christian community's language of allegiance or worship differs from the civic practices around it, but those differences should emerge from the documents under discussion. Nor should interpreters assume that Jewish and Christian communities faced identical pressures in every city. Social location, legal standing, economic resources, and local conflicts can vary. A careful argument identifies the group and setting for which evidence exists, states the narrower conclusion, and resists extending it automatically to an entire empire. The historian's willingness to say “this evidence does not decide” is not a failure of interpretation; it protects the reader from mistaking an attractive explanation for a demonstrated fact.

Finally, historical comparison should return the reader to the literary passage. Having examined a monument, a law, or a manuscript, ask what difference the comparison makes to the argument, narrative, or address being interpreted. A comparison that does not change any reading of the passage may still be historically interesting, but it should not be presented as indispensable to that passage's meaning. Conversely, a comparandum can show that a familiar word or image carried possibilities the modern reader had overlooked. In that case, explain both the illumination and the limit: what the outside source makes plausible, and what the biblical context itself supports. This final step joins historical knowledge to textual interpretation without making the ancient evidence serve as a pretext for a predetermined result.19

The disagreement about Pergamum is suitable for this exercise because the authors arrange their evidence differently. Hemer argues that the city's early prominence in imperial cult gives that setting explanatory weight.44 He nevertheless allows that the altar of Zeus and the cult of Asclepius may be incidentally evoked, rather than excluding them from consideration.45 Thompson places the great altar of Zeus first and names the Asclepius cult as another possibility.46 He also cautions that the agent responsible for Antipas's death is not specified in the text.47 These statements should be compared at the level of argument, not reduced to a choice between a scholar who knows the evidence and one who does not. Their different assessments raise questions about how the passage's language relates to the city's combined religious and political life. The interpreter can draw on Thompson's separate discussion of the provincial imperial cult to ask how public honor moved between cities and rulers.48 That discussion is historical context, however, not proof that every individual in Pergamum interpreted a ritual in an identical way. The contrast between the two scholars also demonstrates why the translator or commentator's judgment should be identified by name: a later reader can then check the proposed identification, compare the reasons, and revise the assessment if additional evidence warrants it.49 A responsible conclusion might state that a civic or cultic reference is plausible while leaving the specific referent disputed.50 The degree of confidence is part of the interpretation, and it should be communicated as clearly as the historical details themselves.

一項關於羅馬亞細亞省的研究把皇帝崇拜描述為連繫地方社群與帝國權力的互惠網絡之一。城邦與皇帝都可能透過廟宇、儀式及官方認可得到榮譽。04 同一研究援引 S. R. F. Price，指出宗教實踐與政治關係不必互相化約。因此歷史研究應追問：誰資助儀式？儀式在何處舉行？參與者實際做了甚麼？相關城邦如何描述自己的地位？源於某一省份或某一世紀的模式，應被視為需要用地方證據檢驗的問題，而非每個社群的預設描述。

啟示錄二章十三節的「撒但的座位」顯示，歷史背景並不會自動決定詞句的指涉對象。一份檢索資料傾向別迦摩宙斯大祭壇，並考慮醫神亞斯克勒庇俄斯聖所；另一份則重視皇帝崇拜，同時容許其他聯想。05 這些是對證據未能完全決定的詞句所作的不同解釋。經文本身沒有指明紀念建築；負責任的詮釋應陳述可能方案及其證據界限，而非把某個地點當作已確立的事實。

解讀新約書信時，研究者可能把「皇帝崇拜」當成一種在各地以完全相同方式推行的單一制度。這個詞語更適合作為提問的起點，而不是完整的歷史描述。城邦的節慶、向統治者致敬的方式、資助建築的人，以及城邦用來宣示地位的銘文，都是具體而各異的證據；它們的年代與處境同樣重要。某地存在一座廟宇，並不表示所有居民都以相同方式參與；一個省份的共同模式，也不能抹去城邦之間的差異。證據所涉及的亦不只是個人的內心信念：建築、公共儀式與尊榮用語，都是統治者、地方領袖與社群關係的一部分。14

啟示錄二章十三節同時說明歷史研究的價值與界限。「撒但的座位」這個表達促使讀者考慮別迦摩城及此意象所代表的勢力，但句子本身沒有指定一座具名建築。因此，若要把此語與某個廟宇或祭壇相連，就必須提出論證，說明城邦物質證據如何與書信的修辭互相呼應。研究者可以比較候選建築的顯著程度、建造或使用年代，以及它與上下文忠心見證主題的相關性。接著還要問，其他解釋是否也能同樣合理地說明這些觀察，以及此表達是否可能同時喚起不止一座建築的聯想。引人聯想的遺址，並不等於已確定的指涉對象。

在課堂上，可以製作四欄證據表：經文措辭、提出的歷史指涉、支持該提議的來源，以及仍未解決的問題。銘文可能證明某個稱號曾在某地使用，卻不一定能證明啟示錄作者有意暗指該銘文。建築能見證某城在建設上的投入；其存在本身卻不能確立某個會眾在那裏經歷了甚麼。這些區分使歷史研究不致淪為一組生動但缺乏約束的插圖，也使讀者可以承認某項歷史可能性值得認真對待，卻不把仍有爭議的辨認視為經文唯一可能的意思。

教師接著可以要求學生反轉練習方向：不要先持有一個現代理論，再尋找能說明它的例子；而要從古代文獻中理論尚未解釋的特徵開始。在別迦摩的例子裏，完全聚焦帝國制度的模型需要交代城中其他宗教場所；只聚焦某一座祭壇的模型，則需要說明為何經文應如此狹窄地理解。Hemer 與 Thompson 為課堂提供兩種實質不同、可供檢驗的學術提議，但任何一位作者的結論都不應被視為不受新證據影響。18 這並不是說所有假說都同樣可能，而是說排序假說的理由應公開，讓其他讀者可以檢驗。

當某項比較看似特別有助於神學詮釋時，同樣需要這種紀律。研究者可以問，基督徒群體談論忠誠或敬拜的語言，與周遭城邦實踐有何不同；但差異應從實際討論的文獻中顯示出來。解經者也不應假定猶太群體與基督徒群體在每座城都面對完全相同的壓力。社會位置、法律地位、經濟資源與地方衝突都可能不同。謹慎的論證會指出證據涉及的具體群體與處境，陳述較有限的結論，並避免自動將它擴展到整個帝國。歷史研究者願意說「這些證據尚不能決定」，並不是詮釋失敗，而是防止讀者把吸引人的解釋誤認為已證實的事實。

最後，歷史比較應帶領讀者回到所解讀的文學段落。在考察紀念建築、法律或手稿之後，應問：這項比較對經文的論證、敘事或對受眾的呼籲有何影響？若比較沒有改變我們對段落的任何理解，它仍可能具有歷史趣味，但不應被說成是理解該段不可或缺的鑰匙。相反，某個比較文本可能顯示，現代讀者熟悉的詞語或意象，曾具有容易被忽略的可能涵義。在這種情況下，要同時說明其照亮之處與界限：外部資料使甚麼理解成為合理可能，以及聖經上下文自身支持甚麼結論。這最後一步把歷史知識與文本詮釋連接起來，卻不讓古代證據淪為既定結論的藉口。19

別迦摩之爭適合用於這項練習，因為兩位作者以不同方式安排證據。Hemer 認為，該城在皇帝崇拜方面的早期突出地位，賦予此背景解釋上的分量。44 不過，他也容許宙斯祭壇及亞斯克勒庇俄斯崇拜可能同時被間接喚起，而沒有把它們排除在考慮之外。45 Thompson 則優先考慮宙斯大祭壇，並把亞斯克勒庇俄斯崇拜列為另一可能性。46 他也提醒，經文沒有指明誰應為安提帕的死亡負責。47 這些陳述應在論證層次上比較，而不是簡化成一位學者知道證據、另一位不知道。兩人的不同評估引發一個問題：段落的語言如何與城邦交織在一起的宗教及政治生活相連？解經者可以參考 Thompson 另對省份皇帝崇拜的討論，追問公共尊榮如何在城邦與統治者之間流動。48 但是，這項討論屬於歷史背景，不能證明別迦摩每個人都以相同方式理解某個儀式。兩位學者的對比也顯明，為何應指明譯者或註釋者的判斷出自誰：後來的讀者可以核查所提出的辨認、比較理由，並在新證據足以支持時修正評估。49 負責任的結論可以說，城邦或崇拜方面的指涉有其合理性，而具體指涉對象仍有爭議。50 我們對結論的信心程度也是詮釋的一部分，應與歷史細節同樣清楚地表達。
