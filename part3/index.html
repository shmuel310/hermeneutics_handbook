01 · GENRE COMPETENCE 01 · 文體能力

Genre as an interpretive question 以文體提出詮釋問題

Genre names a recognizable pattern of communication. It draws attention to conventions that writers and readers may share: the opening and closing of a letter, a poem's patterned lines, or a legal formulation's conditions and consequences. It does not replace close reading. The label “narrative,” for example, does not tell us whether a narrator approves every act or whether a given character's speech is reliable. To call a passage a poem does not establish that its claims are unreal. Genre offers questions to ask, not an automatic answer to those questions.01

Begin at two scales. A book may have an overall form while including smaller forms with distinct functions. A gospel tells an extended story yet includes parables, debates, speeches, quotations, and prayers. A letter can incorporate a hymn-like confession or a remembered tradition. An apocalyptic work can address identifiable communities in the form of a letter while reporting a vision. Interpret the smaller form in relation to its immediate setting and then ask what it contributes to the larger work. The existence of a recognizable form does not prove that every example follows a fixed template.02

A useful first reading marks the speaker, audience, situation, repeated features, and movement. What response does the text invite: agreement, lament, praise, recognition, obedience, or a reconsideration of assumptions? A question in an argument may challenge a claim, while a question in dialogue may seek information or expose a character's misunderstanding. The punctuation printed in a modern edition is itself an editorial aid. Where its placement affects the interpretation, compare alternatives and explain the evidence rather than treating the printed mark as an ancient witness by itself.03

Genre categories also have histories. Modern labels can clarify shared features, but familiar names need not correspond exactly to ancient classifications. An interpreter should show the features that warrant a comparison and ask how close the proposed parallels actually are. “Apocalyptic,” for instance, can describe a literary form, a pattern of thought, or a social movement in different arguments; the meanings should not be silently exchanged. The relevant question is not whether a book has received a respectable label, but what its particular form enables it to communicate.04

Consider Mark 4:30–32. Jesus explicitly asks how the kingdom of God may be compared, then presents a mustard seed sown in the ground and a plant in whose shade birds can settle. The comparison highlights movement from a small beginning to a large outcome. The illustrative form invites attention to that movement without requiring every botanical detail to be a separate symbolic equation. At the same time, the text's actual language about the seed, growth, branches, and birds should not be erased by a general slogan about hope.05

Readers may disagree about whether the birds suggest an earlier scriptural image or whether the main emphasis is simply the scale of growth. Any proposed echo needs more evidence than a common image by itself. Read the potential earlier passage in context, compare distinctive wording and themes, and consider the possibility of shared conventional imagery. An interpretation can be confident that Mark uses a comparison while remaining cautious about the precise source of every element. The strength of each claim should match its evidence.06

A practical genre note records both recognition and uncertainty. It might say: “The passage is a comparison within a sequence of teaching; the narrative identifies its speaker and addressees; its details invite, but do not automatically establish, a proposed echo.” Such a note does not settle all questions before the analysis begins. It keeps the investigator from changing standards midway through an argument, treating a metaphor as a diagram at one point and dismissing its details as irrelevant at another.07

Genre competence is therefore an exercise in disciplined flexibility. The interpreter learns to notice conventions, tests them against the actual language, attends to differences among examples, and revises the initial classification if the text resists it. In this handbook genre provides an entry into law and narrative, poetry and prophetic discourse, and epistles and apocalyptic. The following chapters will develop procedures for each; this introduction explains why those procedures must remain responsive to individual texts.08

A teacher can ask students to test a classification against the smallest observable features. Does the passage identify a speaker? Is a comparison introduced explicitly? Does a later verse explain an image? Does the unit ask a question or tell a story? Answers to these questions are more useful than naming a genre without evidence. A student who calls Mark 4 a parable should point to the comparison in verse 30 and the development in verses 31–32, then explain what the surrounding narrative contributes.09

A second exercise compares the passage with a familiar modern form. A contemporary personal letter may help a reader notice sender, recipient, and greeting in an ancient epistle. Yet the comparison becomes misleading if it assumes the same postal system, social relationship, or expectation of privacy. Similarity opens an inquiry; difference refines it. Write down both before making a historical claim. The goal is a historically alert comparison, not a claim that ancient readers used modern textbook terminology.

文體是可辨認的溝通模式，提醒讀者留意作者與讀者可能共同熟悉的慣例，例如書信的開頭與結語、詩歌的行句，或法律條文的條件與後果。文體名稱不能代替細讀。「敘事」不會自動告訴我們敘述者是否認同每個人物的行動；「詩歌」也不表示其中的主張不真實。文體提供需要提出的問題，而不是預先給出答案。01

首先要分別留意大、小兩個層次。一卷書可能有整體形式，同時包含具有不同功能的較小形式。福音書講述長篇故事，也包含比喻、辯論、講論、引文和禱告。書信可能納入頌讚或先前的傳統。啟示性作品既可能向具體群體發信，也記載異象。較小形式既要放在直接上下文中理解，也要問它對整卷作品有甚麼貢獻。辨認出某種形式，並不代表每個例子都完全符合固定模板。02

初讀時可以標記說話者、聽眾、處境、重複的特徵和思想推進。經文邀請甚麼回應：認同、哀哭、讚美、領悟、順服，還是重新思考原有假設？論證中的問題可能用來挑戰一項主張；對話中的問題可能尋求資訊，也可能顯示人物的誤解。現代版本的標點是編者提供的工具；若標點的位置會影響解釋，就應比較其他可能，不能把印出的符號本身當成古代手稿證據。03

文體分類本身也有歷史。現代名稱有助辨認共同特徵，但不一定與古代分類完全對應。詮釋者應交代比較所根據的特徵，並詢問不同作品究竟有多相近。例如「啟示文學」在不同論證中可能指文學形式、思想模式或社會運動；不可不加說明便把這些意思互相替換。重要的不是某卷書有沒有被貼上權威標籤，而是它的具體形式如何傳達意思。04

馬可福音四章三十至三十二節是一個例子。耶穌先問應當以甚麼比擬上帝的國，接着說明芥菜種撒在地裏，長成植物，飛鳥可以在蔭下棲息。這個比較使人注意從微小開始到長成的推進，卻不要求每一項植物細節都變成獨立的象徵公式。同時，也不能以籠統的「要有盼望」取代種子、生長、枝子和飛鳥等實際用語。05

讀者可能對飛鳥是否使人想起較早的經文意象有不同判斷，也可能認為重點主要是生長的幅度。提出經文回響時，不能只依靠常見的圖像；應閱讀較早經文的上下文、比較獨特用語和主題，並考慮共同慣用意象的可能性。讀者可以確定馬可使用了比較，同時對每個細節的確切來源保持謹慎。每項主張的語氣都應與證據相稱。06

實際的文體筆記應同時記下辨認與不確定性。例如：「這是一系列教導中的比較；敘事交代說話者與聽眾；細節可以引人思考某個回響，但本身不能自動證明。」筆記不必在分析開始前解決所有問題。它的作用是避免研究者在同一論證中忽然改變標準，一時把比喻當成精密圖表，一時又把全部細節視為無關。07

文體能力因此需要有紀律的靈活性：辨認慣例，根據實際語言檢驗，留意不同例子的差別，並在經文不符合原來分類時願意修訂。第三部分將依次討論律法與敘事、詩歌與先知書、書信與啟示文學；本章則說明為何各種方法都必須回應個別經文。08

教師可以要求學生根據最小的可觀察特徵檢驗分類：經文是否指出說話者？是否明確引入比較？後文有沒有解釋某個圖像？單元是在發問還是講故事？回答這些問題，比只說出文體名稱更有幫助。以馬可四章為例，應指出三十節的比較及三十一至三十二節的推進，再說明周圍敘事的作用。09

比較現代書信也能幫助讀者留意古代書信的寄件人、收件人和問候，但不能因此假定郵遞制度、社會關係或私隱期待完全相同。相似帶來問題，差異使比較更準確。歷史主張提出以前，兩者都應記錄。

Covenant, law, and narrative 聖約、律法與敘事

Covenant gives many biblical legal passages a social and theological setting. It is not a single interpretive key that makes every legal collection identical. Ask who addresses whom, what relationship the law presupposes, what situation the command imagines, and what reasons or sanctions are stated. A conditional case, a direct prohibition, and a description of ritual practice do not communicate in precisely the same way. A responsible account describes the literary form before deciding how its norm should be applied beyond its original setting.10

The distinction between law and narrative is helpful but not absolute. A narrative may portray obedience or disobedience, interpret the meaning of an earlier command, or expose the consequences of injustice. Legal material may be embedded in a story of deliverance and covenant making. Neither observation licenses the reader to assume that every narrated practice is prescribed. A character may use power, deceive another person, or suffer without the narrator inserting an explicit verdict after every episode. Judgment may emerge from the plot, speeches, consequences, or comparison with other passages; each proposed inference should be argued.11

When a law addresses a case, identify its participants and the problem to which it responds. What condition triggers the rule? What action or remedy follows? Are there distinctions between intent, negligence, accident, or the status of the parties? A reader should resist immediately turning the ancient case into a timeless slogan, but should also avoid treating its difference from modern law as proof that it has no ethical significance. The work lies in explaining the relationship between the historical rule, the values expressed in its context, and the distinct institutions of the contemporary setting.12

A covenantal reading also requires precision about perspective. A passage may describe the obligations of a particular community, speak of divine promises, or narrate the consequences of breach. Its historical and literary context should be considered before it is placed in a broader account of the canon. Later biblical texts may cite, reframe, or debate earlier material; the fact that one text refers to another does not mean the two use every term in exactly the same way. The connection must be shown and its differences preserved.13

Galatians 5:13–15 provides a compact New Testament example of scriptural command within an argument. Paul addresses those called to freedom, cautions against using freedom as an opportunity for the flesh, and urges mutual service through love. Verse 14 cites the command to love one's neighbour; verse 15 warns against mutual destruction. The passage therefore uses a scriptural command to support an exhortation addressed to a community, but a full account of Paul's discussion of law requires the broader letter and a sustained engagement with differing interpretations.14

Compare three summaries. “Freedom permits anything” neglects the prohibition and command. “The readers should serve one another through love rather than treat freedom as an opportunity for the flesh” captures the immediate contrast. “Every question about the law is settled here” overstates what three verses can establish. This comparison trains readers to distinguish a local inference from a theological conclusion that requires additional passages and assumptions. A broad conclusion is not forbidden; it must be argued at the proper scale.15

In application, identify the relevant continuities and discontinuities. A contemporary community can reflect on whether its exercise of freedom contributes to mutual care or conflict. It should also attend to specific circumstances, power, and vulnerability: a command to serve does not itself authorize an abusive demand for submission. An interpreter should state what in the ancient text supports the application and what additional ethical judgment is needed. This makes both the interpretation and the application open to reasoned evaluation.16

A narrator's selection is part of the evidence. A reader can ask why an episode is placed at this point, which characters know what, and whether a pattern recurs. Those questions do not require assuming that the reader can reconstruct every motive behind the composition. A claim that an action is commended should identify a textual basis: a direct evaluation, a favourable outcome, a pattern across the narrative, or an explicit later assessment. These sources of evidence have different strengths and may point in different directions.17

With legal texts, a comparison of two cases can expose details obscured by a broad summary. If the cases distinguish negligence from an unforeseeable event, that distinction matters to an account of responsibility. If they prescribe different outcomes, the difference should not be averaged away. A present-day analogy then requires attention to changed institutions and affected parties. The interpreter should state both the insight carried forward and the reason the analogy has limits.

聖約為許多聖經法律段落提供社會與神學處境，但它不是讓所有法律集變成相同作品的萬能鑰匙。應問誰向誰說話、法律預設甚麼關係、命令想像甚麼處境，以及經文給出甚麼理由或後果。條件式案例、直接禁止和禮儀程序的說明，溝通方式並不完全相同。要討論如何在原始處境以外應用規範，應先描述其文學形式。10

律法與敘事之間的區分有幫助，卻不是絕對界線。敘事可以描寫順服或悖逆、解釋先前命令的意義，也可以揭露不公義的後果。法律材料亦可能置於拯救與立約的故事中。但這些觀察不授權我們假定凡被敘述的行為都是命令。人物可能使用權力、欺騙別人或遭受痛苦，敘述者卻沒有在每一幕後面立即給出評語。判斷可能透過情節、對話、後果或其他經文的比較逐步顯明；每項推論都需要論證。11

法律處理具體案例時，要辨認參與者和所針對的問題。甚麼條件觸發規則？接着要求甚麼行動或補救？經文是否區分故意、疏忽、意外或當事人的身分？讀者不應立刻把古代案例變成永恆口號，也不應因它與現代法律不同便斷言毫無倫理意義。真正的工作是說明歷史規則、其上下文中表達的價值，以及當代制度之間的關係。12

聖約閱讀也需要分清視角。某段可能描述特定群體的責任、宣告上帝的應許，或敘述違約的後果。把它放入更廣的正典神學以前，應先考慮歷史與文學處境。後來的經文可能引用、重新表述或討論較早材料；一處提及另一處，並不表示兩者的每個字詞都完全同義。需要展示連結，也要保留差異。13

加拉太書五章十三至十五節提供新約論證中運用經文命令的簡短例子。保羅向蒙召得自由的人說話，警告他們不要把自由當作放縱肉體的機會，並勸勉他們藉愛彼此服事。十四節引用愛鄰舍的命令，十五節警告彼此吞咬的後果。這裏的經文命令支持向群體發出的勸勉；但要完整描述保羅對律法的討論，仍須閱讀整封書信並認真處理不同學術解釋。14

比較三個摘要。「自由容許人做任何事」忽略了禁止與命令；「讀者不應把自由當作放縱肉體的機會，反而應藉愛彼此服事」能表達直接對比；「關於律法的每個問題都已在此解決」則誇大三節經文能證明的範圍。練習幫助讀者分辨局部推論與需要更多經文及前提的神學結論。較廣結論並非被禁止，而是必須在相應範圍內論證。15

應用時要指出相同與不同之處。當代群體可以反思自己的自由是否促進彼此關懷，還是加深衝突；也要關注具體處境、權力與脆弱性：服事的命令本身不授權任何人提出虐待性的服從要求。詮釋者應說明經文中的哪些內容支持應用，以及另需作出甚麼倫理判斷，使解釋與應用都能接受有理由的檢驗。16

敘述者如何選擇和安排材料，本身也是證據。讀者可以問某事為何置於此處、不同人物各自知道甚麼，以及某個模式是否重複。若主張某行動受到肯定，就應指出根據：直接評語、結果、敘事模式或後來的明確評價。不同證據的力度並不相同，也可能指向不同方向。17

比較法律案例時，應保留疏忽與意外等可能的重要區別；若條文規定不同結果，也不能把差異平均掉。轉到現代類比時，要說明制度與受影響者如何改變，同時交代可延續的洞見及類比的限制。

03 · GENRE COMPETENCE 03 · 文體能力

Poetry, wisdom, and the voice of experience 詩歌、智慧與經驗的聲音

Poetry invites attention to the relation between lines, images, sound, and the movement of a speaker's address. Parallel lines may repeat, intensify, qualify, or contrast ideas. They should not be reduced to an inflexible rule that the second line always says precisely the same thing as the first. A small change of word or participant may carry the development. Ask what the later expression adds to the earlier one and how the sequence affects the reader's understanding.18

Psalm 23:1–3 provides an example of sustained imagery. The Hebrew begins יְהוָה רֹעִי, “the LORD is my shepherd,” and speaks of not lacking. The following lines depict rest in green pastures, guidance beside restful waters, restoration, and leading in paths of righteousness for the divine name. The shepherd image gives coherence to the pictures of provision and guidance, but the lines are not a technical description of animal husbandry. The speaker uses familiar imagery to express a relationship of trust; a claim about how the imagery applies must attend to the psalm as a whole.19

Notice the grammatical progression. The speaker speaks about the LORD in the third person in the opening lines; a reader should then examine the later psalm for any shift in address and ask what that change contributes. Such a shift is an observation in the text, while a claim about a specific historical event in the poet's life would require additional evidence. The title's relationship to authorship and performance is another question that deserves careful treatment rather than an automatic historical reconstruction. Literary attentiveness and historical questions belong together, but one does not erase the other.20

A poetic image may be theologically serious without functioning as a literal physical description. Saying “the LORD is my shepherd” does not turn God into a sheep farmer in a zoological sense. It makes an intelligible claim through an analogy that invites thought about care, guidance, and dependence. Yet an analogy has limits: it does not authorize every assumption about human shepherds to be imported into the psalm. The words of the passage and its wider context, not an unlimited list of possible associations, govern a responsible explanation.21

Wisdom literature poses related but distinct problems. A proverb may state an observed regularity in a memorable, compressed form. Interpreters should ask about genre and setting before treating a general statement as an unconditional prediction for every individual circumstance. This does not mean that proverbs are merely vague optimism. It means that their form asks readers to exercise judgment in particular situations. The wider wisdom corpus also allows questions about suffering, justice, and the limits of human understanding to stand in tension with simple expectations of reward.22

A useful classroom exercise compares two explanatory errors. One reader treats every poetic metaphor as a literal material description; another concludes that because the language is poetic it makes no claim about the world. Both approaches bypass the way figurative language works. The question is how an image makes a claim, what features it brings into focus, and what it leaves unstated. A strong explanation stays with the passage long enough to answer those questions before drawing an application.23

When translating poetic language, a translator must make choices about line division, word order, and the force of idioms. Comparing translations can reveal where interpretation is involved, but counting translations does not decide which option is best. Consult the original-language text and relevant scholarly discussions when possible; otherwise identify the limitation. Readers who do not know Hebrew can still observe changes in speaker, repeated images, and the relation of lines, while recognizing that some decisions require linguistic expertise.24

The image of water in Psalm 23 works within a sequence of rest and guidance, not as a free-floating symbol with one meaning in every passage. Compare the wording of verses 2 and 3: the speaker attributes leading and restoration to the LORD, and the second verse's picture of rest is followed by language of paths. A reader can trace the relationship without claiming that the two images are identical. The larger poem must still be read before a judgment is made about its overall movement.25

The question of translation should remain open to correction. A rendering of נַפְשִׁי may require choices among familiar English equivalents according to the context. A dictionary listing alone does not demonstrate that every possible sense is intended at once. In teaching, show the phrase, compare credible translations, and explain why a proposed reading fits its clause. A reader who lacks Hebrew may consult informed resources, but should accurately identify that dependence.

閱讀詩歌時，要留意行句之間的關係、意象、聲音與說話者的思想推進。平行的行句可能重述、加強、修正或對照不同觀念，不能僵化成「第二句永遠只是第一句的同義重複」。一個詞語或人物的小變化可能承載重要發展。應問後一句比前一句增加了甚麼，以及整個次序如何影響讀者的理解。18

詩篇二十三篇一至三節展示持續發展的意象。希伯來文以「耶和華是我的牧者」開始，接着說自己不致缺乏；後面的行句描繪青草地、安歇的水邊、生命的恢復，以及因上帝的名被引導走義路。牧者意象把供應和引導的畫面連在一起，但這些句子不是畜牧學的技術說明。說話者藉熟悉的圖像表達信靠的關係；要解釋意象如何應用，仍須閱讀整篇詩。19

也要留意語法推進。開頭說話者以第三人稱談論耶和華；讀者應再檢查後文是否轉變稱呼方式，並詢問這種改變有甚麼作用。稱呼改變是文本中可觀察的現象，若要推斷詩人在某一歷史事件中的具體經歷，則需要額外證據。標題與作者身分、演唱之間的關係，也需要謹慎研究，不能自動重建歷史情節。文學細讀與歷史問題彼此相關，但不能互相取代。20

詩歌意象即使不是物質層面的字面描述，仍然可能包含嚴肅的神學主張。「耶和華是我的牧者」不表示上帝在動物學意義上成為牧羊人；它藉類比使讀者思考照顧、引導和倚靠。然而類比有界限，不能把人類牧者的一切特徵都加入詩篇。負責任的解釋要受經文用字及較廣上下文約束，而不是無限制地羅列可能聯想。21

智慧文學提出相關卻不同的問題。箴言可能以容易記憶的短句表達常見規律。把一般陳述當作對每個人、每個處境都無條件成立的預測以前，詮釋者應考慮文體和處境。這並不是說箴言只是空泛樂觀；相反，它的形式要求讀者在具體情況中運用判斷。更廣的智慧文學也容許苦難、公義與人類理解限制等問題，對簡單的報應預期提出張力。22

課堂上可以比較兩種錯誤。一位讀者把每個詩歌隱喻都當成物質事實；另一位因為文字屬於詩歌，就斷言它對世界沒有任何主張。兩者都跳過了比喻語言實際運作的方式。真正的問題是：意象如何提出主張？它凸顯哪些特點？又留下甚麼沒有說？可靠的解釋會在進入應用之前，停留在經文中回答這些問題。23

翻譯詩歌時，譯者需要決定分行、詞序和習語的語氣。比較譯本有助看見哪些地方涉及詮釋，但只計算支持某譯法的譯本數目，不能決定哪個方案最好。有條件時應查考原文與學術討論；若不能，便應交代限制。不懂希伯來文的讀者仍可以觀察說話者的變化、重複意象和行句關係，同時承認某些判斷需要語言專業。24

詩篇二十三篇中的水意象位於安歇與引導的次序中，不是離開上下文、在每一處都有相同意思的符號。比較二、三節可以看見引導與恢復的行動，以及從安歇的畫面轉向道路的語言；讀者既可追蹤關係，也不必宣稱兩個意象完全相同。25

翻譯選擇應容許修訂。某個希伯來詞可能有多種譯法，但字典列出不同意思，不證明所有意思同時存在。教學時可以比較可靠譯本，解釋哪個方案較切合子句；若依靠專業資源，也應如實交代。

04 · GENRE COMPETENCE 04 · 文體能力

Prophecy, vision, and the limits of decoding 先知信息、異象與解碼的界限

Prophetic discourse includes judgment, lament, disputation, promise, symbolic action, and poetry. These forms may occur together within a single book. Ask who is addressed, what conduct is confronted, what grounds are given for the judgment or promise, and how the passage relates to the book's sequence. A warning need not have the same discourse function as a promise, and a symbolic action is not automatically an instruction for every later reader to reproduce it. Differences of form should shape, but not predetermine, the account of the message.26

Prophetic language can address a historical audience and also be read in later canonical settings. Those are related but distinct claims. The interpreter should first identify the textual evidence for a historical addressee, then show how a later writer quotes, echoes, or reuses the passage. A later use may extend or reshape the earlier language. It is inadequate either to erase the earlier setting or to assume without argument that subsequent reception can add nothing to the passage's theological history. The relevant relationship must be demonstrated, not asserted by a label.27

Revelation 1:12–16 offers a New Testament example of visionary description. The seer turns and sees seven golden lampstands and a figure described with a series of comparisons: hair like wool and snow, eyes like a flame of fire, and a voice like many waters. The text itself uses comparison language repeatedly. A description of what is seen in a vision should not be converted without explanation into a photographic report of an ordinary encounter. Nor should it be dismissed as empty decoration. The comparisons direct attention to features of the figure and the narrated experience of the seer.28

The interpreter must read onward: Revelation 1:20 supplies an explicit explanation of the stars and lampstands. Such a textual explanation is stronger evidence for those images in that location than an arbitrary modern code. Other images may draw on earlier scriptural language, yet the degree and function of a proposed allusion still need examination. The presence of one explained symbol does not guarantee that every number, object, colour, or gesture has a single stable meaning everywhere in the book. An interpretation should indicate which explanation the text gives and which connection the reader proposes.29

Consider two competing habits. A decoder assigns a current political figure to each image before establishing the book's literary movement and original audience. A sceptical reader assumes that an image has no referential force because it is symbolic. Neither is warranted simply by genre. Visionary discourse can speak about real communities, judgment, hope, and divine action through mediated images. Responsible interpretation asks how the book itself develops those images and how its historical and canonical contexts bear on them.30

A historical claim also requires historical evidence. The date, setting, and social circumstances of Revelation are subjects of scholarship, not deductions from a single symbol. When a proposed interpretation depends on a particular claim about an emperor, a cult, or a local community, identify the ancient evidence and consider alternatives. The authority of an archaeological image or an inscription must be assessed for date, provenance, and relationship to the text. Similarity is a beginning for comparison, not proof of direct dependence.31

In teaching, invite readers to separate description, interpretation, and application. Description records what the passage says the seer experiences. Interpretation explains how textual signals and context support a proposed meaning. Application asks how a contemporary audience may be addressed by that interpretation, while acknowledging differences of historical location. This sequence is not a machine that eliminates disagreement. It makes disagreement visible and prevents the teacher from presenting a speculative identification as if it were the very words of the passage.32

An interpretive proposal can be tested by asking what would count against it. If someone claims that Revelation assigns an identical numerical meaning to every occurrence of seven, look for the book's own explanations and uses before accepting the claim. Revelation 1:20 explicitly identifies the stars and lampstands in the opening vision; that interpretation is local evidence, not a universal dictionary for every image. The distinction helps a reader take the text's imagery seriously while resisting unwarranted certainty.33

A second control is to read a warning in its immediate address before mapping it onto a later audience. Who is challenged? What response is called for? How does the surrounding sequence present the consequences? The passage may have enduring theological importance without erasing the first addressees. Applying a warning faithfully involves explaining why the analogy holds and where the historical situations differ.

先知性論述可以包含審判、哀歌、爭辯、應許、象徵行動和詩歌；這些形式也可能同時出現在一卷書內。讀者要問說話者向誰發言、指責甚麼行為、審判或應許有甚麼根據，以及本段與全書次序的關係。警告與應許不一定具有相同的語篇功能；象徵行動也不自動構成要求所有後世讀者模仿的命令。形式差異應影響我們對信息的理解，卻不能在閱讀前替我們決定結論。26

先知語言可以面向歷史上的聽眾，也可以在後來的正典處境中被閱讀；這是彼此相關但不同的主張。詮釋者應先指出歷史受眾有哪些文本證據，再展示後來作者如何引用、回響或重新運用原文。後來的使用可能擴展或重塑較早的語言。一方面不能抹去較早處境；另一方面也不能未經論證就假定後來的接受史對神學理解毫無貢獻。具體關係需要展示，不能只靠標籤宣告。27

啟示錄一章十二至十六節提供異象描寫的例子。見異象的人轉身，看見七個金燈臺，以及一位以多個比較描述的人物：頭髮白如羊毛和雪、眼目如火焰、聲音如眾水。經文本身反覆使用比較語言。對異象中所見的描述，不能不加解釋就轉成日常相遇的照片式報告；也不能因其形式特殊，就把它當成空洞裝飾。這些比較引導讀者注意人物的特徵和見異象者所敘述的經驗。28

讀者也必須繼續往下讀：一章二十節明確解釋星和燈臺。對這些意象在該處的意義，經文本身的解釋比任意的現代密碼更有力。其他意象可能運用較早的聖經語言，但回響的程度與功能仍需研究。一個象徵得到解釋，不代表書中每個數字、物件、顏色和動作都在所有地方只有一個固定意思。解釋應指出哪些是經文給出的說明，哪些是讀者提出的連結。29

可以比較兩種習慣。解碼者在確立全書的文學推進與原有受眾以前，先把每個圖像套到當代政治人物。另一位讀者卻因為圖像具有象徵性，就假定它沒有任何所指。單憑文體都不能支持這兩種做法。異象性論述可以藉中介圖像談論真實群體、審判、盼望和上帝的行動。負責任的閱讀應考察全書如何發展意象，以及歷史與正典處境如何影響判斷。30

歷史主張還需要歷史證據。啟示錄的年代、處境與社會環境是學術研究問題，不能單憑一個象徵推論。若某個解釋依賴對皇帝、崇拜或地方群體的特定判斷，就應指出古代證據並考慮其他方案。文物圖像或碑文的證據力需要按年代、出土地點及其與經文的關係評估。相似是比較的起點，並非直接影響的證明。31

教學時，可以邀請讀者區分描述、解釋與應用。描述記錄經文說見異象者經歷了甚麼；解釋說明文本線索和上下文如何支持某個意義；應用則詢問當代聽眾如何受這項解釋提醒，同時承認歷史處境的差異。這個次序不是消除分歧的機器，而是讓分歧可見，防止教師把推測性的對號入座說成經文直接寫出的話。32

可以用反證問題檢驗解釋。若有人宣稱啟示錄每次使用七都具有完全相同的意思，就應先檢查全書實際如何運用及解釋。啟示錄一章二十節明確辨認開頭異象中的星和燈臺；這是該處的證據，不能自動變成全書所有意象的通用字典。33

另一項控制是先閱讀警告所面向的聽眾：誰受到挑戰？經文要求甚麼回應？上下文如何描述後果？警告可以具有持續的神學意義，卻不必抹去原有收信者；應用時要交代類比成立的理由與歷史差異。

05 · GENRE COMPETENCE 05 · 文體能力

Letters, argument, and the shape of persuasion 書信、論證與說服的結構

A New Testament letter addresses a communicative situation. Its opening identifies sender and addressees; thanksgiving, argument, exhortation, personal news, and final greetings may follow in varied orders. The presence of recurring features can orient the reader, but not every letter uses the same sequence or has one easily reconstructed occasion. Begin with what the letter itself says and distinguish a plausible historical reconstruction from evidence directly available in the text.34

An argument unfolds through relationships among clauses and paragraphs. Mark assertions, questions, objections, examples, commands, and reasons. Words translated “for,” “therefore,” or “but” can indicate relations, but their interpretation depends on the clause and context. A diagram is useful when it makes an argument visible; it is misleading if it forces every sentence into a modern essay outline or assigns a single technical meaning to each connector everywhere it occurs. A provisional outline should be revised when it cannot account for a paragraph's movement.35

Galatians 5:13–15 illustrates how a command can be supported and sharpened by neighbouring clauses. The call to freedom is followed by a restriction and a contrasting imperative to serve one another through love. The following appeal to love of neighbour provides a reason, and the warning against mutual destruction shows the stakes of the contrast. A summary that isolates “freedom” from the command to serve or separates the warning from the community's relationships loses part of the argument. Conversely, three verses cannot settle every question concerning Paul's use of law throughout the letter.36

Rhetorical analysis should illuminate the text rather than require the author to have consciously followed one textbook's rules. Ask what a proposed category explains: does it clarify an objection, a transition, an appeal to shared values, or the arrangement of examples? Then ask whether the evidence actually distinguishes that category from a simpler description. Ancient rhetorical handbooks, letters, and inscriptions can offer useful comparisons, but proximity in language or form is not proof that one author copied another. Comparison is strongest when similarities and differences are both described.37

A passage may contain a quotation or an embedded traditional formula. Such material has at least two contexts: its earlier setting and its function where it is now used. The command to love one's neighbour in Galatians 5:14 should be read both in its place in the Torah and in Paul's argument about freedom and service. The interpreter should not simply transfer all the concerns of one context to the other, nor should the earlier context be ignored because the later writer has selected a short phrase. The relationship must be established through careful comparison.38

Readers also encounter instructions directed to a historical community. Responsible application asks what problem the instruction addresses, which reasons accompany it, and what features of the original situation are relevant to a contemporary case. Some commands may have broad implications, while others refer to a specific person, place, or circumstance. A responsible account explains why continuity or difference matters instead of invoking “context” as a word that automatically nullifies or universalizes a command.39

A classroom exercise can make these distinctions observable. Ask one group to produce an outline from verbs and conjunctions; ask another to trace participants and changes in address. Then compare what the outlines capture and omit. One might see a transition that the other missed; both may need to revise a boundary. The purpose is not to crown a single method as infallible but to make the proposed interpretation answerable to the actual text and to the best available evidence about its context.40

Arguments also gain meaning from what they presuppose. A writer's appeal to a shared text, previous instruction, or common practice may carry persuasive force for the recipients. Modern readers should identify the appeal without inventing a detailed social history that the letter does not supply. Where reconstruction is uncertain, compare more than one plausible setting and test whether the proposed interpretation depends on a disputed premise.41

An outline should represent relations, not merely assign headings. For Galatians 5:13–15, a diagram could place the call to freedom, the limitation, the contrasting command, the scriptural support, and the warning in sequence. It should preserve the connection of the mutual-service command to the contrast, not detach it as an unrelated moral maxim. Another outline might emphasize different features, provided it can explain the same textual relationships.

新約書信在具體溝通處境中發言。開頭辨認寄信者與收信者，接着可能按不同次序出現感恩、論證、勸勉、個人消息和結語。反覆出現的特徵有助讀者定位，但不是每封信都採用同一順序，也不是每封信的寫作背景都能輕易重建。應從書信本身所說的開始，區分可行的歷史重建與文本直接提供的證據。34

論證透過子句和段落之間的關係展開。可以標記主張、問題、反對意見、例子、命令和理由。「因為」「所以」「但是」等連接詞可能提示關係，但如何理解仍取決於句子及上下文。圖表若能使論證更清楚便很有用；若硬把每句話放進現代論文格式，或給每個連接詞一個在所有地方都固定不變的技術意思，就會誤導。暫定大綱若不能解釋段落如何推進，就應修訂。35

加拉太書五章十三至十五節顯示一個命令如何受前後子句支持並得到具體方向。蒙召得自由之後，經文提出限制，並以相反的祈使命令讀者藉愛彼此服事。隨後引用愛鄰舍提供理由，彼此吞咬的警告則顯示對比的嚴重性。若摘要只留下「自由」而抽掉服事的命令，或把警告與群體關係分開，就失去部分論證。反過來說，這三節也不能解決整封書信中保羅使用律法的每個問題。36

修辭分析應使文本更清楚，而不是要求作者有意遵循某本教科書的每項規則。應問所提出的類別實際解釋了甚麼：它是否澄清反對意見、轉折、訴諸共同價值，或例子的排列？再問證據是否真能區分這個技術類別與較簡單的描述。古代修辭手冊、書信和碑文可以提供有用比較，但語言或形式相近，不等於證明作者直接抄用。比較同時交代相似與差異，論證才會較強。37

段落可能包含引文或原有的傳統公式。這類材料至少有兩個需要注意的處境：它較早的背景，以及它在現有論證中的功能。加拉太書五章十四節的愛鄰舍命令，應同時放在妥拉和保羅關於自由與服事的論證中閱讀。不能把一個處境的全部問題直接搬到另一處，也不能因後來作者只選用短句就忽略較早上下文；兩者的關係需要細緻比較。38

書信中的指示也面向歷史上的群體。負責任的應用需要問：這項指示處理甚麼問題？它附帶甚麼理由？原始處境的哪些特點與今天的案例相關？有些命令可能具有較廣的含義，另一些則涉及特定人物、地點或情況。可靠的解釋必須說明為何延續或差異重要，而不是只用「上下文」一詞自動取消命令，或把它自動普遍化。39

課堂練習可以使區分更具體。一組依據動詞和連接詞提出大綱，另一組追蹤人物與稱呼方式的變化，再比較各自抓住或忽略了甚麼。一組可能看到另一組漏掉的轉折，兩組也可能都需要修訂界限。目標不是宣布某種方法永遠正確，而是讓解釋持續回應實際經文及最可靠的處境證據。40

論證的力量也來自它所預設的共同背景。作者可能訴諸共同經文、先前教導或慣例；現代讀者應辨認這種訴求，卻不可編造書信沒有提供的詳細社會歷史。若背景重建不確定，可以比較多個合理處境，檢查解釋是否依賴有爭議的前提。41

大綱應呈現關係，而不只是加上標題。加拉太書五章十三至十五節可以依次標出自由的呼召、限制、相反命令、經文支持和警告；彼此服事的命令不應與原來的對比切斷，變成孤立的道德格言。

06 · GENRE COMPETENCE 06 · 文體能力

A repeatable workflow for reading across genres 跨文體閱讀的可重複步驟

A disciplined reading can be organized as a series of questions rather than as a rigid sequence that guarantees the correct answer. First identify the unit and its neighbours. Mark changes of speaker, place, time, topic, repeated words, and formal signals. Then state a tentative boundary and test what happens if the preceding or following material is included. A modern paragraph heading is a useful starting point, not a final decision. If the unit serves as a transition, a good account should explain how it looks backward and forward.42

Second describe what kind of communication the text presents. Is it an instruction, story, prayer, argument, comparison, judgment speech, or visionary report? More than one answer may be appropriate. Identify the evidence for each label and say what the label helps the reader notice. A narrative containing an instruction requires attention both to the instruction's immediate hearers and to the role of that instruction in the plot. A poetic oracle can address a real community while using imagery that should not be flattened into a literal inventory.43

Third trace the movement of the passage. In narrative, follow actors, events, conflict, speech, and resolution. In an argument, identify a claim, its support, objections, and consequences. In a poem, compare line relations, shifts of address, repeated images, and movement between lament and trust. These questions are not mutually exclusive; a psalm can argue, a letter can tell a story, and a narrative can include poetry. The purpose is to notice what the selected passage does rather than to apply a checklist without judgment.44

Fourth record original-language and textual questions in proportion to their importance and the reader's competence. Distinguish a manuscript reading from an editorial decision, and a lexical possibility from a demonstrated contextual meaning. If access to a primary source is unavailable, identify the source actually consulted and do not imply direct verification. A tool can provide a text or morphology, but it does not decide the discourse function of a clause by itself. Interpretive responsibility remains with the reader who states the claim.45

Fifth consider relevant historical and intertextual evidence. Ask whether a proposed parallel is sufficiently close in date, language, genre, and function to illuminate the passage. Similarity does not automatically establish borrowing. When an explicit quotation appears, compare its earlier context with the argument in which it is now placed. When a proposed echo is less certain, describe the evidence and plausible alternatives. The aim is neither to prohibit comparison nor to multiply parallels without controls.46

Sixth formulate a summary that captures the unit's principal movement and compare it with a rival. A sentence that can accommodate any passage is probably too general; a sentence that reproduces every detail may not show which relationships matter. State what the proposed summary explains, what it compresses, and which claims remain disputed. Ask whether the summary accounts for the passage's genre: a command, lament, narrative, and vision will not all make their central point in the same form.47

Finally, explain the route from interpretation to theological reflection and application. Identify what is asserted by the passage, what is inferred from its place in a book, what follows from a wider canonical comparison, and what requires a judgment about the present situation. An honest application names both continuity and difference. It also acknowledges who is affected by the teaching and what additional information is needed to act responsibly. A well-supported application is not a detachable slogan; it grows from the text's distinctive form and argument.48

The workflow is iterative. A new observation may require a revised boundary; a credible alternative may change the proposed synthesis; a historical discovery may make one reconstruction less likely. Record these revisions so another reader can follow the evidence. The goal is not to eliminate theological conviction or personal engagement, but to make interpretation open to correction by the passage, by evidence, and by a community of careful readers.49

A research note can distinguish three types of source. A primary text is the material being interpreted; a critical edition or translation presents it through editorial decisions; a secondary study proposes an interpretation or provides historical analysis. These categories depend on the question being asked. A commentary becomes a primary source for a history of interpretation, even while it is a secondary source for the ancient passage. Identifying the role of a source makes it easier for another reader to check the inference.50

At each stage make a modest claim before making a larger one. Record the wording, describe the relationship that seems present, compare a rival account, and specify which additional evidence would change the judgment. Such restraint does not require abandoning a theological reading. It helps readers see which parts of that reading rest on the immediate unit and which require a wider argument. The resulting interpretation can be both substantive and revisable.

The same procedure applies when an interpreter changes a position after consulting a source. Keep the earlier hypothesis, record the evidence that challenged it, and explain the revised conclusion. For instance, a student may initially assume that a story commends every character's actions. After comparing the narrator's presentation with later consequences, the student might formulate a more qualified judgment. The new account should identify the observations that caused the change; it should not claim access to the author's private thoughts. When disagreement persists, readers can then see whether they differ about the text, the force of the evidence, or the assumptions that connect the text with a contemporary case.

Communicating uncertainty also requires proportional language. “The passage states,” “the passage suggests,” and “one possible comparison is” express different levels of confidence. Choose among them according to the available evidence. A statement of uncertainty need not be a substitute for analysis: it should identify the particular unresolved question and explain why it remains open. In teaching or preaching, this practice can model thoughtful conviction without disguising the limits of a source or a contested inference.

有紀律的閱讀可以安排成一系列問題，而不是保證正確答案的僵硬程序。首先辨認單元及其前後文，標記說話者、地點、時間、主題、重複詞語和形式訊號的改變；然後暫定界限，測試若把前文或後文納入，解釋會有甚麼變化。現代分段標題是有用的起點，卻不是最後決定。如果段落具有過渡功能，好的解釋應交代它如何連接前後文。42

第二，描述經文正在進行甚麼溝通：指示、故事、禱告、論證、比較、審判宣告，還是異象報告？一段文字可能同時有多種形式。應指出支持各種標籤的證據，以及該標籤幫助讀者注意甚麼。敘事中包含命令，就既要關心命令當時的聽眾，也要關心它在情節中的功能。詩歌形式的先知信息可以面向真實群體，同時使用不宜壓平成字面清單的意象。43

第三，追蹤段落的推進。敘事中要留意人物、事件、衝突、對話和結果；論證中要辨認主張、支持理由、反對意見及後果；詩歌中要比較行句關係、稱呼變化、重複意象，以及哀歌與信靠之間的移動。這些問題並不互斥：詩篇可以論證，書信可以講故事，敘事也可以包含詩歌。目的在於看見本段經文實際做甚麼，而不是毫無判斷地完成檢查表。44

第四，按問題的重要性與讀者的能力記錄原文及文本問題。要區分抄本讀法與編者決定，也要區分字典列出的可能意思與上下文已經證明的意義。若不能取得原始資料，應交代實際查閱的是甚麼，不可暗示已經直接核對。工具可以提供文本或形態資料，卻不能自動決定子句的語篇功能；提出主張的讀者仍要承擔詮釋責任。45

第五，考慮相關歷史與文本互涉證據。提出的平行材料在年代、語言、文體和功能上是否足夠接近，能夠照亮經文？相似本身不自動證明借用。遇到明確引文時，要比較較早的上下文與引文在新論證中的功能。若回響不那麼確定，應描述證據與合理的替代方案。目的既不是禁止比較，也不是毫無控制地增加平行例子。46

第六，提出能概括單元主要推進的摘要，再與其他合理方案比較。可以套用到任何段落的句子可能過於籠統；塞進所有細節的句子則可能看不出哪些關係最重要。應說明摘要解釋了甚麼、濃縮了甚麼，以及哪些主張仍有爭議。也要問摘要是否尊重經文的文體：命令、哀歌、敘事和異象不會全以同一形式提出核心意思。47

最後，交代由解釋走向神學反思和應用的途徑。分辨經文直接主張甚麼、從它在全書的位置推論甚麼、較廣正典比較支持甚麼，以及哪些結論還需要對當代處境作判斷。誠實的應用會指出延續與差異，也會關心誰受教導影響，以及要負責任地行動還需要甚麼資訊。有根據的應用不是可隨意拆出的口號，而是從經文獨特的形式與論證長出來。48

整個程序可以反覆修訂。新的觀察可能要求改變界限，可信的替代解釋可能改變神學綜合，歷史發現也可能使某種重建變得較不可能。記錄修訂，讓其他讀者能追蹤證據。目標不是消除神學信念或個人參與，而是讓解釋能夠受經文、證據與認真閱讀的群體修正。49

研究筆記可以區分三類資料：作為解釋對象的主要文本、帶有編輯選擇的校勘本或譯本，以及提出解釋或歷史分析的次要研究。但分類取決於問題：研究解釋史時，註釋書本身也可能成為主要資料。交代資料的角色，能幫助讀者核對推論。50

每個階段都應先提出範圍適當的主張：記錄用語、描述可能關係、比較替代解釋，再指出哪些額外證據會改變判斷。謹慎不是放棄神學閱讀，而是讓讀者看見哪些結論直接依據本段，哪些需要更廣的論證。

在諮詢資料後改變立場時，可以保存原本假設，記錄使它受到挑戰的證據，再說明修訂後的結論。這樣即使分歧持續，讀者也能看見究竟是在文本、證據力度，還是從經文連接當代處境的前提上意見不同。表達不確定性時，也要指出具體尚未解決的問題，而不只說一句「有爭議」。
