01 · EPISTLES & APOCALYPSE 01 · 書信與啟示文學

Letters as situated communication 書信是處境中的溝通

A New Testament letter is a literary work addressed to particular people, though its surviving form also invites readers beyond its first audience. Its opening commonly identifies sender and addressee and offers a greeting; a thanksgiving or blessing may follow before the body; greetings and a closing benediction may conclude it. These observations describe frequent features, not a universal template. Hebrews, for example, begins without the opening sender–addressee formula familiar from many Pauline letters, while its ending includes greetings. The interpreter should first observe what a particular document does instead of declaring a feature absent merely because a later template expects it.01

The epistolary setting makes it tempting to reconstruct the missing side of a conversation. A command, an objection, or a change in tone may indicate a problem among recipients. Yet the letter normally gives only one voice, even where it quotes a question or reports a messenger. Readers should distinguish what the text expressly reports, what its language makes probable, and what is merely a possible reconstruction of an ancient dispute. The safest reconstruction explains several features without requiring imagined documents, motives, or factions for which no evidence survives. It remains open to revision when another reconstruction accounts for the same details with fewer assumptions.02

Philippians begins by naming Paul and Timothy, the holy ones in Christ Jesus at Philippi, and overseers and deacons. The opening greeting and thanksgiving establish a relationship before the first lengthy report and appeal. The mention of imprisonment and the progress of the gospel belongs to the letter's narrated situation; assigning the exact location and chronology involves historical arguments beyond the bare fact of imprisonment. The interpreter should not treat a scholarly reconstruction as an autobiographical statement of the text. The passage itself can be read attentively before a decision is made between plausible historical proposals.03

Letters are also public and theological communication. A request directed to a community may draw on shared memory, Scripture, prayer, biography, and teaching. A text may be read aloud, copied, or circulated; these possibilities require evidence in each case, rather than being presumed from genre alone. The command to read the letter among the Thessalonians and the instruction concerning the letter from Laodicea demonstrate that early Christian letters could move beyond a private exchange. They do not prove an identical circulation history for every document. Genre supplies questions to investigate, not ready-made answers to the history of reception.04

A letter's opening establishes relationships and creates expectations that the body may confirm or qualify. In Philippians 1:1–2 the senders, recipients, and greeting appear in a compact sequence. Paul and Timothy are described as servants of Christ Jesus; the recipients are addressed as saints in Christ Jesus in Philippi, together with overseers and deacons. The grace-and-peace greeting identifies God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. A reader can observe these features directly without first deciding exactly when and where the letter was written.05

In verses 3–8 thanksgiving and affection are linked with the recipients' participation in the gospel. Verses 9–11 then report a prayer that love may abound with knowledge and discernment, so that the recipients may approve what matters and be sincere and blameless for the day of Christ, filled with the fruit of righteousness through Jesus Christ. The movement from thanksgiving to prayer is not an arbitrary sequence of religious formulas. It establishes themes of relationship, participation, discernment, and hope that a reader can trace in the larger letter. Nevertheless, an opening may introduce more than one theme; an interpreter must demonstrate recurrence rather than assume that each term is a coded preview of a fixed outline.06

A useful classroom question is whether thanksgiving proves that the relationship contains no disagreement. It does not. Affirmation and later exhortation can coexist in the same letter, and neither should be erased by a reconstruction that requires the community to be either entirely harmonious or entirely divided. Another question is whether the mention of named offices establishes that all early Christian communities had exactly the same organization. The salutation concerns this group; comparison with other texts is needed before proposing a broader historical claim. In both cases, the question disciplines the relation between close reading and historical generalization.07

One can also distinguish the recipients as portrayed in the letter from everything that may have been true of them. The text provides a perspective on persons within a particular communicative act. It does not give a complete demographic survey or direct access to each recipient's response. When interpreting ancient communities, modern readers often possess unequal evidence about elites, enslaved persons, women, and other groups. An argument from silence should therefore be carefully qualified. This restraint does not prevent the use of historical research; it helps scholars make clear when a claim rests on the letter, an external source, or a reconstruction joining the two.08

新約書信是寫給特定人物或群體的文學作品，但其保存下來的形式也邀請最初聽眾以外的人閱讀。許多書信在開首表明寄件人和收件人，隨後有問安，接着可能出現感恩或頌讚；正文之後則有問候與祝福。這些是常見特徵，並非放諸四海皆準的模板。希伯來書的開頭沒有許多保羅書信熟悉的寄件人與收件人格式，結尾卻包含問安。讀者應先觀察每一份文獻實際如何展開，而不是因為後來的模型預期某個元素存在，就斷言作者刻意省略了它。01

書信的處境容易使人嘗試重建沒有保存下來的另一方對話。命令、反對意見或語氣轉變可能暗示收信人的問題；但即使作者引用問題或報告使者消息，我們通常仍只聽見一個聲音。讀者需要分辨經文明確報告了甚麼、哪些推論較可能，以及哪些只是對古代爭議的一種可能重建。較穩健的重建應能解釋多項文本特徵，而不需要大量沒有證據的想像文件、動機或派系。若另一個方案能以較少假設解釋相同資料，我們便應願意修訂原先的推測。02

腓立比書開頭提到保羅和提摩太、在腓立比基督耶穌裏的眾聖徒，以及監督和執事。問安與感恩先建立彼此關係，然後才進入較長的報告和勸勉。書信提及捆鎖與福音進展，這屬於書信所敘述的處境；要確定監禁的確切地點和年代，則需要超越「作者被囚」這個基本事實的歷史論證。詮釋者不應把學者重建的情境當作文本中的自傳式陳述。即使尚未在不同歷史方案之間作決定，讀者仍可以仔細閱讀現存經文。03

書信也是面向群體的神學溝通。對群體的要求可能訴諸共同記憶、聖經、禱告、個人經歷與教導。文本可能被公開誦讀、抄寫或傳閱，但每一種情況都需要具體證據，不能單憑文體推定。帖撒羅尼迦書信中公開誦讀的吩咐，以及有關從老底嘉來信的指示，說明早期基督徒書信有可能超出私人交換；這卻不能證明每封信都有完全相同的傳播歷史。文體為研究提供問題，而不是現成的接受史答案。04

書信的開頭建立關係，並形成可能在正文中得到確認或修正的閱讀預期。腓立比書一章一至二節以簡潔次序列出寄件人、收件人與問安。保羅和提摩太稱為基督耶穌的僕人；收件人包括在腓立比、在基督耶穌裏的眾聖徒，以及監督和執事。恩典與平安的問安提及父上帝與主耶穌基督。讀者無須先確定書信的精確寫作時間和地點，也能直接觀察這些特徵。05

三至八節把感恩與情感連結於收信人在福音上的共同參與；九至十一節接着報告禱告，求他們的愛心在知識和辨別力上增長，使他們辨明重要的事，並在基督的日子純潔無過，藉着耶穌基督結滿公義的果子。從感恩走向禱告，不只是任意排列宗教套語。它建立關係、參與、辨別和盼望等主題，讀者可以追蹤它們如何在全書發展。然而，開頭可能引出多個主題；必須實際證明後文的重複與發展，不能假定每個詞都是固定大綱的暗號。06

課堂上可以問：感恩是否證明雙方關係完全沒有分歧？答案是否定的。同一封信可以既有肯定也有稍後的勸勉；不應因為某種重建要求群體必須完全和諧或完全分裂，就抹去其中一面。又如，提到職分是否證明所有早期基督徒群體都有完全相同的組織？問安涉及這個群體；提出更廣的歷史結論之前，需要比較其他文本。兩個問題都能訓練讀者謹慎連接細讀與歷史概括。07

讀者亦可分辨書信如何描繪收件人，以及他們實際生活中可能存在的其他情況。文本提供某次溝通中對人物的視角，卻不是完整的人口調查，也不能使我們直接知道每位收信人的反應。研究古代群體時，我們對精英、受奴役者、婦女及其他群體所掌握的證據往往並不對等。因此，從沉默推論的結論需要特別保留。這種謹慎並不妨礙歷史研究，而是幫助學者說明哪項主張根據書信、外部資料，或結合兩者的重建。08

From sentence to argument 從句子走向論證

An epistle's argument is not identical with a list of its topics. Trace what assertions, questions, imperatives, examples, and scriptural appeals do in relation to one another. Connectives such as γάρ, οὖν, δέ, and ἵνα can signal grounds, inference, contrast, continuation, or purpose, but their force depends on construction and context. A dictionary gloss does not mechanically decide whether a clause gives a reason for the immediately preceding statement or for a larger unit. Mark the alternatives and ask which makes sense of the surrounding movement.09

In Philippians 2:1–4, an appeal to shared life in Christ leads to an appeal for a common disposition expressed in humility and attention to others. The repeated language of thinking and the contrast between selfish ambition and concern for others give the reader more than a general theme of “unity.” The subsequent call to adopt a disposition related to Christ Jesus introduces the passage in 2:6–11. A responsible summary should explain how the exhortation and the depiction of Christ relate, rather than detach the latter as a free-standing theological ornament.10

The movement from Christ's humiliation to God's exaltation has a clear textual hinge at διό in verse 9. The participles and main verbs require further grammatical analysis, but the direction of the narrative is visible: the text describes Christ's self-emptying and obedience, then God's exaltation and the confession of Jesus Christ as Lord. The meaning of ἁρπαγμός in verse 6 is disputed; it should not be resolved by asserting that one English gloss is the root meaning. The question is how the phrase functions within its Greek construction and the larger contrast introduced by ἀλλά. Alternative proposals must be compared with linguistic and contextual evidence.11

The rhetorical relation between the Christ passage and the preceding appeal also requires care. It is possible to say that the passage has an ethical function in its immediate setting without reducing Christ to a merely human moral example. Conversely, confessing its theological significance does not require ignoring the exhortation that introduces it. An interpretive proposition should state what evidence warrants a given relation and where a competing account differs. If a passage is thought to incorporate an earlier hymn or confession, distinguish the observable elevated style from a historical reconstruction of its earlier form and use.12

Philippians 2:1 uses a series of conditional expressions before the appeal in verse 2. The reader should notice the relationship between shared consolations and the request that follows, without assuming that an English conditional automatically expresses doubt. Verse 2 relates a common disposition to shared love, unity, and common purpose. Verse 3 contrasts selfish ambition and empty conceit with humility; verse 4 turns attention from one's own concerns to those of others. These clauses do not constitute a detailed institutional blueprint. They offer a moral and communal orientation that subsequent interpretation must apply to particular situations with care.13

Verse 5 presents another grammatical question: how should the relation between the readers' disposition and Christ Jesus be represented in translation? Some renderings supply words not explicit in Greek to make the comparison readable. Comparing translations can expose the choice; it cannot by itself decide it. A student should mark what is expressed in the Greek, what is supplied in translation, and why the supplied words are thought to fit. If the question has several defensible answers, the theological exposition should not claim a level of grammatical certainty greater than the evidence permits.14

Verses 6–8 describe a sequence in which Christ is spoken of in relation to the form of God, does not regard equality with God in the way described by the debated ἁρπαγμός construction, empties himself, takes the form of a servant, and humbles himself in obedience to death on a cross. Verse 9 changes the grammatical subject: God highly exalts him and grants the name above every name. Verses 10–11 describe the intended universal response, culminating in confession of Jesus Christ as Lord to the glory of God the Father. The theological significance is extensive, but a short summary should preserve the distinction between Christ's actions, God's action, and the response of the worshipping creation.15

For teaching, ask participants to sketch the passage's movement without adding explanations in the first pass. Then add interpretive questions beside the verbs and images: what is meant by equality, taking, self-emptying, obedience, and exaltation? Which questions can be addressed by immediate syntax, and which require comparisons with other texts or historical sources? A second group might test whether the passage moves from status to service and from obedience to exaltation; the group should show the expressions on which that summary depends. This procedure helps a class learn the difference between a textual observation and a larger theological inference without pretending that one can read free of prior convictions.16

書信的論證不等同於主題清單。讀者需要追蹤陳述、問題、命令、例子和經文引用彼此如何發揮作用。γάρ、οὖν、δέ、ἵνα 等連接詞可提示理由、推論、對比、延續或目的，但其作用取決於句法和上下文。字典給出的一個對譯不能機械地決定某子句是前一句的理由，還是整個較大段落的根據。應先標記可能方案，再詢問哪個方案最能解釋前後思想的推進。09

腓立比書二章一至四節從在基督裏共同生活的基礎出發，勸勉讀者有共同心志，並以謙卑和關注別人的需要表達這心志。反覆出現的思想語言，以及私心與關心他人的對比，使本段超過一個籠統的「合一」主題。接着，作者呼籲讀者具有與基督耶穌相關的心志，並引入二章六至十一節。負責任的摘要必須解釋勸勉與基督描繪之間的關係，而不是把後者抽出來當作獨立的神學裝飾。10

第九節的 διό 是基督降卑與上帝升高之間清楚可見的轉折。分詞和主要動詞仍需要進一步句法分析，但敘述的方向可以看見：經文先描述基督虛己與順服，再描寫上帝將他升高，以及眾口承認耶穌基督為主。第六節 ἁρπαγμός 的意思存有爭議，不能宣稱某個英文對譯就是詞根本義，藉此解決問題。真正要問的是該詞在希臘文結構及 ἀλλά 引出的整體對比中如何發揮作用；不同解釋須以語言與上下文證據比較。11

基督段落與前文勸勉的修辭關係也需要謹慎處理。承認這段在直接上下文中有倫理作用，並不要求把基督縮減成單純的人類道德榜樣；反過來，肯定其神學意義，也不需要忽略引入它的勸勉。詮釋命題應交代哪項證據支持所提出的關係，以及競爭方案在哪一點上不同。若認為本段引用較早的詩歌或信仰告白，也應分辨可觀察的莊重文體，與對其較早形式和使用方式的歷史重建。12

腓立比書二章一節以一系列條件表達引出第二節的呼籲。讀者應留意共同經歷的安慰與隨後要求之間的關係，卻不能假定中文或英文的「若」必然表達懷疑。第二節把共同心志與同一的愛、合一和相同的心意連接起來。第三節以謙卑對照自私野心與虛榮；第四節把目光從自己的事轉向別人的事。這些子句不是詳細的制度藍圖，而是群體生活的道德方向；要應用到具體處境，仍須謹慎判斷。13

第五節提出另一個語法問題：讀者的心志與基督耶穌之間的關係應如何翻譯？有些譯文為使比較清楚而補上希臘文沒有明寫的詞。比較譯本能揭示這項選擇，卻不能單靠譯本數量解決它。學生應標記希臘文明說甚麼、譯文補充甚麼，以及為何補充的詞被認為符合上下文。若有幾種可以辯護的答案，神學講解便不應宣稱超過證據所容許的語法確定性。14

六至八節描述基督與上帝形像的關係，以及他如何看待與上帝同等；其中 ἁρπαγμός 結構仍有爭議。經文接着說他虛己、取了僕人形像，並謙卑順服至死，甚至死在十字架上。第九節的語法主詞改變：上帝將他升為至高，並賜給他超乎萬名之上的名。十至十一節描述所期待的普世回應，以眾口承認耶穌基督為主、歸榮耀給父上帝作結。其神學意義十分豐富，但短摘要仍應保留基督的行動、上帝的行動和受造界回應之間的區別。15

教學時，可先要求學生畫出段落推進，暫時不要加入解釋；第二步再在動詞和意象旁列出問題：同等、取形像、虛己、順服和升高各指甚麼？哪些問題可以從直接句法入手，哪些需要比較其他文本或歷史來源？另一組可以測試本段是否從地位走向服事，再從順服走向升高，並指出摘要所依據的表達。這個步驟幫助學生區分文本觀察與更大的神學推論，同時不假裝讀者能完全擺脫先前信念。16

Ancient rhetoric as a comparative resource 古代修辭學作為比較資源

Ancient rhetorical handbooks offer vocabulary for analyzing persuasion, including invention, arrangement, style, memory, and delivery. They also describe conventional parts of speeches and forms of argument. Such sources help an interpreter ask how a writer establishes credibility, addresses opponents, anticipates objections, or moves an audience toward action. Yet an ancient handbook is a comparative witness, not a secret table of contents automatically governing every letter. The overlap between oral speech and written correspondence is an empirical question, and ancient rhetorical practice was itself diverse.17

Before assigning a section to an exordium, narratio, proof, or peroration, identify what the passage actually does and compare the proposed arrangement with alternatives. Different scholars may locate the same boundary differently, or question whether a formal division is useful at all. A paragraph can have more than one function: a personal report may also strengthen a relationship, and a prayer may introduce themes developed later. The more elaborate the proposed rhetorical architecture, the more important it is to show the verbal and structural evidence for each division. The mere availability of a category in a handbook does not show that an author consciously followed it.18

Rhetorical analysis can illuminate questions without claiming knowledge of a speaker's inward intention. A writer may use a question to challenge an inference, introduce an imagined objection, or solicit agreement; these functions should be assessed in context. Direct address may identify a change of audience, but it can also be a rhetorical device within a continuing argument. Repetition can foreground a theme, although frequency by itself does not establish its meaning. These are hypotheses for reading the form of the text, not universal rules that remove the need for judgment.19

Ethical use of comparative material requires attention to chronological and social difference. Elite rhetorical treatises, school exercises, documentary letters, and inscriptions reflect different settings. A parallel may demonstrate that a construction or practice existed without proving that a biblical author borrowed it directly. Conversely, absence from a limited surviving archive cannot prove that a practice was unknown. The interpreter should record the date, location, genre, and relevant similarities and differences, then explain the specific interpretive claim for which the parallel is offered. A modest comparison can be illuminating precisely because it does not claim more than its evidence permits.20

Consider a paragraph that begins with a question and continues with a rebuttal. One hypothesis treats the question as a real quotation from an opponent; another sees a question composed by the writer for pedagogical effect. A third allows that a reported view has been reformulated for the argument. The text may contain clues, such as explicit attribution, pronoun changes, repeated wording, or a response directed to an imagined interlocutor. None of these clues should be interpreted in isolation, and the mere fact that an ancient handbook recommends a technique cannot determine which historical situation occurred.21

A rhetorical outline can be compared with a simpler discourse outline. The former might describe how the writer introduces, supports, and concludes an appeal; the latter might trace participants, conjunctions, and shifts in topic. If both lead to the same division, that convergence is useful, though not conclusive. If they produce different divisions, the disagreement reveals a decision that needs explanation. In either case, the aim is not to make a letter fit a predetermined set of boxes, but to describe what its features contribute to the communicative act.22

In a seminar, ask each student to identify a proposed rhetorical category and state what evidence would count against it. For example, if a supposed conclusion is followed by a substantial new argument, the interpreter should explain why it belongs to a peroration or consider a different boundary. If a proposed proof section includes extended praise and personal news, perhaps the category is being used too rigidly. A well-supported model should make the text more intelligible and should remain capable of correction. Naming a technical term is the beginning of an analysis, not its completion.23

Comparison with ancient handbooks also has a social dimension. The writers of elite rhetorical manuals addressed particular educational settings and could describe ideals that were not followed uniformly in ordinary practice. Surviving documentary letters may exhibit formulaic openings or closings without reproducing a formal courtroom speech. A careful comparison therefore identifies the kind of document involved and does not treat every writer in the Greek-speaking world as equally trained in the same curriculum. The historical claim that an author had specific schooling requires evidence beyond the presence of a common persuasive device.24

古代修辭手冊提供分析說服的詞彙，包括材料構思、安排、風格、記憶和演說，也描述演講常見部分及論證形式。這些資料有助詮釋者詢問：作者如何建立可信度、回應對手、預先處理反對意見，或引導聽眾採取行動？然而，古代手冊是比較見證，不是自動支配每封書信的秘密目錄。口頭演講與書面通信有多少重疊，需要實際證據；古代修辭實踐本身也具有多樣性。17

把段落標為開場、敘述、證明或結語之前，應先指出該段實際在做甚麼，再把所提出的安排與其他方案比較。學者可能對同一界限的位置有不同判斷，也可能質疑正式分段是否有幫助。一段文字可能同時有多種功能：個人報告也能鞏固關係，禱告也可能引入稍後發展的主題。提出的修辭架構越複雜，越需要展示支持各個分段的字詞和結構證據。手冊中有某個類別，本身並不能證明作者有意照着它寫作。18

修辭分析可以幫助理解文本，而不必聲稱知道作者內心每一個意圖。作者提出問題，可能為了挑戰推論、引入假想反對意見，或促使聽眾同意；需要放回上下文評估。直接稱呼可能表示聽眾改變，也可能是持續論證中的修辭方式。重複可以凸顯主題，但單憑出現次數不能確定詞語的意思。這些都是閱讀文本形式時需要檢驗的假設，而不是免除判斷的萬用法則。19

負責任地運用比較材料，必須注意年代和社會差異。精英修辭著作、學校練習、文書書信與碑文屬於不同場合。某個平行例子能證明一種結構或做法曾經存在，卻不一定證明聖經作者直接借用；反過來，在有限的現存資料中找不到，也不能證明古代人完全不認識它。詮釋者應記錄年代、地點、文體、相關異同，再交代引入平行例子具體支持甚麼主張。適度的比較之所以有啟發性，正是因為它沒有要求證據承擔超過其能力的結論。20

假設一個段落以問題開頭，接着提出反駁。一種假設認為問題直接引用真實對手；另一種認為作者為教學效果而自行提出；第三種則容許作者為論證而重新表述曾經聽過的看法。文本可能提供線索，例如明確歸屬、代名詞轉變、重複措辭或對假想對話者的回應。這些線索不能孤立處理；古代手冊曾建議某種技巧，也不能單獨決定實際發生了甚麼歷史情況。21

可以把修辭大綱與較簡單的語篇大綱比較。前者可能描述作者如何引入、支持和結束勸勉；後者則追蹤人物、連接詞和主題轉換。若兩種方法得到相同分段，這種會合有幫助，卻不構成絕對證明；若分段不同，分歧便揭示需要說明的判斷。無論哪一種情況，目標都不是把書信塞進預定格子，而是描述其特徵對溝通行動有甚麼貢獻。22

在研討課中，可請每位學生提出一個修辭類別，並說明甚麼證據會對它構成挑戰。例如，如果所謂結語之後仍有一段新的重要論證，詮釋者應說明為何它仍屬結語，或考慮另一個界限。若所謂證明段包括大量讚美與個人消息，可能表示所用類別過於僵硬。有證據支持的模型應使文本更容易理解，也應能夠接受修正。說出一個術語只是分析的開始，不是完成。23

比較古代手冊也涉及社會差異。精英修辭作者面向特定教育場合，描述的可能是理想，而非所有普通實踐都一致遵從的規則。現存文書書信可能有固定的開頭或結尾，卻沒有重現正式法庭演講。謹慎比較必須辨認文件類型，不能假定希臘語世界的每位作者都接受完全相同的訓練。要主張某位作者受過特定教育，單憑一種常見說服方式仍不足夠。24

Apocalypse, prophecy, and letter 啟示、預言與書信

Revelation identifies itself in several overlapping ways. Its opening calls the work an ἀποκάλυψις, speaks of the words of prophecy, and pronounces blessing on the reader and hearers who keep what is written. Shortly afterward, John addresses seven churches in Asia with a greeting, and the book closes with testimony, warning, and grace. These features mean that a genre label should invite attention to several literary dimensions rather than force a choice between a visionary work and a letter. The book's own descriptions need to be read together with comparison to other ancient writings.25

Apocalyptic visions present revealed realities through symbolic scenes, mediated communication, and journeys or visionary perspectives. This broad description should not be turned into a claim that every image functions in the same way. Some features are interpreted within the text; others develop through allusions, repeated images, or contrasts across scenes. Revelation 1:20 identifies the seven stars and the seven lampstands in relation to angels and churches. The presence of an interpretation for these images is evidence about those images, not a license to assign a fixed one-to-one value to every number, colour, creature, or action in the book.26

Prophetic address and epistolary address both involve audiences and response. The messages to the seven churches contain commendations, rebukes, commands, and promises, yet their differing situations should not be collapsed into one undifferentiated description. The instruction to hear what the Spirit says to the churches invites a wider audience to listen without removing the distinct features of each message. Readers should attend to the specific wording and sequence of each address before using the seven messages as a general chart of later history.27

The opening's blessing links reading, hearing, and keeping with the declaration that the time is near. The phrases deserve careful grammatical and theological attention, including the relation between an ancient audience's horizon and later readers. Neither a claim that every detail was exhausted immediately nor a claim that the text was irrelevant to its first hearers should be assumed before the argument is made. Responsible interpretation distinguishes what the language says, what it may imply about temporal expectation, and what a wider theological framework proposes about fulfilment. Alternative judgments must be represented fairly and tested against the text.28

Revelation's opening is especially useful for testing genre categories because it includes both a mediated disclosure and an address to churches. The communication moves from God to Jesus Christ, through an angel, to John and the servants for whom the revelation is intended. The prologue then speaks of a reader and hearers who keep the written words. One need not reconstruct every detail of a worship service to notice that the scene anticipates communication to a listening community. A later liturgical setting may be plausible, but it requires its own argument.29

The seven messages in chapters 2–3 display repeated elements alongside differences. Each begins with an address to the angel of a church and words attributed to the risen Christ, while the content of commendation, correction, or warning varies. Some include explicit calls to repentance; others emphasize endurance. Reading only a list of repeated features can conceal those differences. Reading only each local problem in isolation can miss the recurring invitation to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. Both scales matter for interpretation.30

The sevenfold arrangement may encourage the whole audience to listen to each address, but the interpreter should state the evidence for claims about the number's symbolism. Revelation uses sevens in many settings, and repetition can contribute to a sense of completeness; it does not follow that every set of seven encodes seven chronological periods. That latter proposal has to be argued from the book and its contexts, not assumed as an automatic consequence of the number. A historical reader also asks how such a proposal accounts for the concrete places named in the opening and the conditions represented in each message.31

Genre expectations are especially powerful when contemporary readers approach a difficult text. Someone who expects a coded timetable may focus on calculation, while someone expecting an anti-imperial manifesto may emphasize political resistance. Each approach may illuminate selected details, but each can also screen out material that complicates it. A disciplined reader writes down an initial expectation and asks what the text does not fit. The prologue's concern with hearing and keeping its words, for example, should be considered alongside questions of future expectation and historical setting. Such a practice makes prior commitments visible and opens them to correction.32

啟示錄以幾種互相重疊的方式介紹自己。開首稱這作品為 ἀποκάλυψις，提到預言的話，並祝福誦讀、聆聽和遵守所記內容的人。稍後，約翰向亞細亞七個教會問安；全書結尾又有見證、警告和恩典。這些特徵顯示，文體名稱應促使我們留意多個文學層面，而不是迫使讀者在異象作品與書信之間作二選一。書卷自己的描述需要與其他古代文獻的比較一同考察。25

啟示異象常透過象徵場景、中介的溝通及異象視角呈現被揭示的實況。但這種概括不能變成「每個意象都以同一方式運作」的斷言。有些特徵在文本內得到解釋；另一些則透過經文回響、重複意象或場景對比逐漸發展。啟示錄一章二十節把七星和七燈臺與使者及教會聯繫起來。這是關於這些意象的證據，卻不授權讀者為全書每一個數字、顏色、活物或動作指定固定的一對一代碼。26

預言性的宣告與書信式的稱呼都涉及聽眾及其回應。給七教會的信息包括稱讚、責備、命令和應許；但各教會不同的處境不應被壓縮成一個沒有差異的描述。「應當聽聖靈向眾教會所說的話」邀請更廣的聽眾聆聽，同時沒有抹去各則信息的獨特內容。讀者應先留意每一封信息的具體措辭和次序，才討論其對其他讀者的意義，不應直接把七封信當成後世歷史的固定圖表。27

開首的祝福把誦讀、聆聽和遵守連在一起，並宣告日期近了。這些語句需要仔細的語法與神學討論，包括古代聽眾的時間視野與後來讀者之間的關係。不能在論證之前，便假定所有細節立即完全應驗；也不能假定文本對最初聽眾毫無關係。負責任的詮釋要分辨字句明說甚麼、可能暗示甚麼時間期待，以及較廣的神學架構如何理解應驗。不同判斷都應得到公平陳述，並接受經文檢驗。28

啟示錄開頭同時有經過中介的啟示及對眾教會的稱呼，因此特別適合檢驗文體類別。溝通由上帝到耶穌基督，經由使者到約翰及啟示所面向的眾僕人；序言接着提到誦讀的人、聆聽並遵守所記之言的人。即使不重建一次崇拜的每個細節，也能留意文本預期信息會傳到聆聽的群體。後來的禮儀處境可能合理，卻仍需要獨立論證。29

二至三章給七教會的信息既有重複元素，也有差異。每則信息都稱呼一個教會的使者，並呈現出自復活基督的話；但稱讚、糾正或警告的內容各有不同。有的信息明確要求悔改，另一些則強調忍耐。若只列出共同格式，便可能忽略差異；若只把每個地方問題孤立閱讀，又會錯過反覆出現的邀請：聽聖靈向眾教會所說的話。兩個閱讀尺度都重要。30

七重結構可能鼓勵整個聽眾群閱讀每則信息，但主張數字有何象徵意義時，應交代證據。啟示錄在許多場合使用七，重複可能有助傳達完整性；然而，這不代表每一組七都自動編碼七個按時間排列的時期。後一主張必須根據書卷及其處境論證，不能只由數字推出。歷史閱讀也要詢問：該方案如何處理開首所列具體地名及每則信息所呈現的處境？31

當代讀者面對困難文本時，文體預期尤其有影響力。期待密碼式時間表的人可能專注計算；期待反帝國宣言的人可能強調政治抵抗。兩種視角都可能照亮部分細節，卻也可能遮蔽令其複雜化的材料。有紀律的讀者會寫下初始預期，再詢問文本有哪些部分不符合它。例如，序言關注聆聽和遵守，就應與未來期待和歷史處境的問題一同考慮。這種做法讓先入承諾顯明，也使其能接受修正。32

Vision, scriptural allusion, and historical judgment 異象、經文回響與歷史判斷

The inaugural vision in Revelation 1:9–20 places John among his fellow participants in affliction, kingdom, and endurance; he hears a voice and turns to see seven lampstands and a figure described through a series of images. The text moves from hearing to seeing and from fear to reassurance: the figure lays a right hand on John and says not to fear. It then identifies itself as the first and the last and the living one who died and lives. A reading that lists possible symbolic equivalents without tracing this movement risks losing the scene's address to its audience.33

Many features of the vision invite comparison with earlier biblical texts, including Daniel's vision of a human-like figure and descriptions of an ancient heavenly figure. Comparison begins by setting the passages alongside one another and identifying actual verbal or thematic correspondences. Then the reader asks which texts were available, how distinctive the shared elements are, and what the earlier context contributes to the new scene. Several proposed sources may overlap; a composite image need not be explained by one antecedent alone. The interpreter must also allow for common imagery and differences in transmission. An allusion can be persuasive without establishing which manuscript lay before the author.34

The claim that imagery evokes imperial contexts requires the same discipline. Cities in Roman Asia contained institutions and practices connected with imperial power, and Revelation itself names a throne of Satan in the message to Pergamum. Whether a particular phrase refers specifically to a provincial temple, a local cult, persecution, or another aspect of civic life is an interpretive question. Ancient inscriptions, archaeology, and literary evidence may illuminate possibilities, but their dates and contexts matter. An attractive parallel should not be treated as decisive merely because it resembles a modern political concern.35

Symbols can be ethically difficult. Images of judgment and violence must not be converted into instructions for present readers to harm opponents. The text depicts divine judgment and addresses communities called to endurance and testimony; the relationship between symbolic representation and contemporary conduct needs explicit discussion. Readers should consider the voices affected by their interpretation, particularly when language about enemies has been used to justify coercion. Historical interpretation and theological application are connected but distinct judgments. A faithful discussion names the tension rather than treating it as solved by a quick allegorical equation.36

A disciplined comparison of Revelation 1 and Daniel 7 begins by distinguishing the figures in each scene. Daniel 7 describes an Ancient of Days and one like a son of man coming with the clouds. Revelation describes one like a son of man among lampstands, with features that invite comparison with more than one biblical scene. The interpreter should list the shared features and the differences before drawing theological conclusions. Similarity in one feature does not erase differences in setting, action, or the relationship between figures.37

Next consider the literary work done by the image in Revelation itself. The figure speaks, reassures John, identifies himself as the living one who died and is alive, and commands writing. These actions shape the interpretation of the visual description. If a proposed allusion explains a colour or a garment but cannot account for the speaker's words or the messages that follow, it is at best a partial interpretation. Conversely, a summary of the speaker's identity should not ignore the visual details that the narrative takes care to describe.38

An allusion hypothesis becomes stronger when a cluster of distinctive elements appears in a coherent relationship and when the proposed earlier text could reasonably have been available. Yet allusions do not all operate in the same manner. A later text may echo language, adapt an image, reverse a pattern, or combine materials from several earlier contexts. A reader must show the particular relationship being proposed. The fact that two passages share an image is not by itself proof that one gives the complete interpretive key to the other.39

G. K. Beale's study of John's use of earlier Scripture treats allusion, symbolism, and the interpretation of Revelation as connected questions. His argument is one scholarly account and should be evaluated alongside other readings rather than used as a list of automatic identifications. A responsible footnote points to the specific discussion actually consulted; if a scholar proposes a connection on different grounds from the present interpreter, the difference should be made clear. The student's own comparison remains an argument for which the student is responsible.40

啟示錄一章九至二十節的開場異象，先把約翰放在與讀者共同經歷患難、國度及忍耐的位置；他聽見聲音，轉身看見七燈臺和一位以多重意象描述的人物。文本由聽轉向看，也由恐懼轉向安慰：那位人物以右手按着約翰，吩咐他不要懼怕，隨後自稱首先的、末後的及曾死而今活着的一位。若只列出象徵可能對應甚麼，而不追蹤這個推進，便可能失去整個場景如何向聽眾發出信息。33

異象中許多特徵邀請讀者與較早的聖經文本比較，包括但以理書中像人子的異象及對古老天上人物的描述。比較應先把經文並列，指出實際存在的字詞或主題對應，再詢問較早文本是否可供作者使用、共同特徵有多獨特，以及原本上下文對新場景有何貢獻。多個可能來源可以互相重疊；複合意象不一定只能用一處先行經文解釋。詮釋者也必須考慮共同意象與文本傳遞的差異。有說服力的回響並不自動證明作者眼前放的是哪一份抄本。34

認為意象回應帝國處境，也需要相同的紀律。羅馬亞細亞的城市有與帝國權力相關的制度及實踐；啟示錄給別迦摩的信息本身提到撒但的座位。但某句話是否具體指向省級廟宇、地方崇拜、迫害或城市生活的其他方面，仍是詮釋問題。古代碑文、考古資料及文獻能照亮可能性，但它們的年代與處境非常重要。不能只因某個平行例子令人聯想到現代政治關切，就視它為決定性證據。35

象徵也可能帶來倫理困難。審判與暴力圖像不能直接變成今天傷害對手的命令。文本描繪上帝的審判，並向被呼召忍耐和作見證的群體說話；象徵表達與當代行動之間的關係需要明確討論。敵我語言曾被用來合理化強迫，因此讀者應考慮詮釋會影響哪些人。歷史解釋與神學應用彼此相關，卻是不同的判斷；負責任的討論應指出張力，而不是用一個迅速的寓意對照宣告問題已經解決。36

有紀律地比較啟示錄一章與但以理書七章，第一步是分辨各場景中的人物。但以理書七章描述亙古常在者，又描述一位像人子的駕着天雲而來；啟示錄則描述一位像人子的在燈臺中間，其特徵使人想起不止一處聖經場景。作出神學結論之前，應先列出共同特徵與差異。一項相似不能抹去場景、行動或人物關係的不同。37

接着要問，這個意象在啟示錄自身的文學作用是甚麼。那位人物說話、安慰約翰、自稱曾死而如今活着，並吩咐他寫下來。這些行動會影響我們對視覺描寫的理解。若所提出的回響只能解釋顏色或衣服，卻無法處理說話內容和隨後的信息，最多只能算部分解釋；反過來，概括說話者身分時，也不應忽略敘事特別描繪的視覺細節。38

當一組較獨特的共同元素以連貫方式出現，而且較早文本合理地可供作者使用，回響假設便更有力量。但所有回響並非以同一方式運作。後來文本可能回應字句、改造意象、反轉模式，或結合多處較早處境的材料；讀者必須展示具體提出的是哪一種關係。兩段文字共享一個意象，本身並不能證明其中一段是理解另一段所有細節的完整鑰匙。39

G. K. Beale 對約翰使用較早聖經的研究，把回響、象徵與啟示錄詮釋看作相互關聯的問題。他的論證是學術討論中的一個方案，應與其他讀法一同評估，而不是當成自動鑑定清單。負責任的註腳指出實際查閱的具體討論；如果學者與學生提出相似連結的理由不同，也應讓差異顯明。學生自己的比較仍是一項需要自行負責的論證。40

06 · EPISTLES & APOCALYPSE 06 · 書信與啟示文學

A repeatable workshop and responsible application 可重複的研讀與負責任的應用

Begin a letter study by reading the entire document aloud or in a continuous sitting. Identify sender, addressees, opening and closing, then mark recurring participants, arguments, questions, and commands. Write a provisional outline with alternatives at uncertain boundaries. In a second pass, select a manageable paragraph, identify its main clauses, and ask how it advances the larger argument. Only then consult historical reconstructions and rhetorical handbooks. Record what each source contributes and where it makes assumptions that the primary text does not independently establish.41

For Philippians 2, a workshop can compare three proposed summaries: a call to unity, a portrayal of Christ's self-humbling and exaltation, and an argument relating communal humility to Christ. Each captures something observable, but they have different scopes. Test each against 2:1–4, the transition in verse 5, the movement in verses 6–11, and the subsequent appeal in verses 12–13. Ask whether a summary leaves out the agent of exaltation, turns the passage into a bare moral example, or ignores its ethical setting. Disagreement can then be located in an explicit interpretive choice rather than hidden behind a fluent slogan.42

For Revelation 1, first describe what is heard, seen, interpreted, and commanded, noting changes in speaker and addressee. Compare selected phrases with Daniel and other relevant texts only after the internal scene is understood. Separate explicit interpretations in the chapter from proposed allusions and modern historical identifications. Then ask what the passage calls its first audiences to hear or do. The resulting record should identify evidence for each claim, a significant alternative, and the reason for a provisional judgment. When the evidence does not decide, make the limitation visible.43

Application requires attention both to continuity and difference. Philippians' concern for the interests of others can inform a congregation's decision-making, but specific decisions require knowledge of persons, needs, and power relations that the ancient letter does not supply. Revelation's call to patient witness can encourage communities under pressure, but a contemporary group must not assume it is automatically identical to every ancient church or equate an opponent with a monstrous symbol. Interpretations should be accountable to the text and to their foreseeable consequences. A classroom should permit questions, protect those harmed by coercive applications, and make room to revise a reading in response to evidence.44

The conclusion of a compendium is not the end of interpretation. Return repeatedly to primary texts, language, literary structure, historical sources, competing scholarly explanations, and theological judgment. Explain the path from observation to inference, and distinguish the claim supported by one passage from a broader synthesis across the canon. Invite others to test the argument and acknowledge limitations in the evidence. These habits enable readers to receive both the situated character of ancient writings and their continuing challenge without forcing one to cancel the other.45

An application is more than a sentence beginning with “therefore we should.” It identifies a textual observation, explains the relation between the ancient situation and the present one, and names the action or attitude being proposed. Each step may be disputed. A group can agree that Philippians encourages attention to others while disagreeing about how to allocate limited resources. The disagreement then requires practical knowledge and ethical deliberation in addition to exegesis. A claim that the text settles every detail would conceal decisions the present community must still make.46

A responsible application also specifies its audience. An instruction to those with authority to attend to others is not necessarily equivalent to an instruction that a person subjected to coercion must remain silent. The original letter's communal exhortation calls for careful moral judgment rather than a simple transfer that ignores unequal power. Teachers should invite those affected to speak and should distinguish a proposed application from a direct quotation or universal rule. Theological interpretation becomes more accountable when its practical reasoning is open to examination.47

Readers of Revelation similarly need to distinguish the book's symbolic portrayal from their own identification of contemporary actors. Its warnings about compromise and its calls to endurance may be relevant to modern communities, but the move from ancient imagery to naming a present enemy is an additional interpretive step. That step can have serious consequences and must not be smuggled into a paraphrase as if it were explicitly stated in the text. Competing applications should be evaluated by their fidelity to the passage, their attention to context, and their treatment of affected people.48

A final peer-review exercise can test the entire chain. Ask a reader unfamiliar with the writer's conclusion to locate the passage, reproduce the observations, and identify each inference. Can the reader tell which scholarly work was actually consulted? Are alternative interpretations described fairly? Are a biblical quotation and a scholarly reconstruction clearly distinguished? Does the proposed application explain continuity and difference rather than merely asserting them? Where the answer is no, revise the argument or state its limits. The purpose is not to eliminate disagreement but to make the evidence and its consequences available for responsible deliberation.49

When interpreters disagree, begin by locating the disagreement. Is it over the reading of the Greek text, the boundary of a paragraph, the sense of a word, the identification of a historical practice, or the theological implications of a claim? These questions require different evidence. A new inscription might affect a historical reconstruction without resolving a grammatical dispute; a grammatical argument might clarify a clause without settling how the passage should guide a contemporary institution. Listing the steps separately helps participants discover whether they disagree at every point or share substantial common ground.50

A revision log can record a first judgment, the strongest competing explanation, and the evidence that led to a change. The writer need not pretend to have reached certainty: a tentative conclusion can be the best available judgment. What matters is that the reader can see how the conclusion depends on the evidence and what would make it vulnerable to revision. This posture applies to classroom discussion as well as scholarly publication. It encourages careful argument, makes room for correction, and protects the distinction between conviction and proof.51

研讀書信時，可先一口氣讀完全書，或在群體中誦讀。指出寄件人、收件人、開頭與結尾，再標記反覆出現的人物、論證、問題及命令。先寫出暫定大綱，並在不確定的界限旁列出替代方案。第二遍選擇一段可處理的文字，找出主要子句，詢問它如何推進整封書信的論證。然後才查閱歷史重建與修辭手冊，記錄各來源提供甚麼，以及哪些假設不能單憑原文獨立確立。41

研讀腓立比書二章時，可以比較三個摘要：呼籲合一、描繪基督降卑與升高，以及把群體謙卑與基督連在一起的論證。每個摘要都捕捉到可以觀察的內容，但範圍不同。應把它們放在二章一至四節、第五節的過渡、六至十一節的推進，以及十二至十三節接續的勸勉中檢驗。某個摘要是否遺漏使基督升高的主體？是否把本段變成單純的道德榜樣？是否忽略其倫理處境？這樣便能把分歧定位為明確的詮釋選擇，而不是藏在流暢的口號後面。42

研讀啟示錄一章時，先描述聽見、看見、得到解釋和接受吩咐的內容，並留意說話者及聽眾如何轉換。理解內部場景後，才把所選語句與但以理書及其他相關文本比較。應分辨本章明確給出的解釋、所提出的經文回響，以及現代歷史身分鑑定。再詢問這段呼籲最初聽眾聽見甚麼或作甚麼。研究記錄應指出每項主張的證據、一個重要的替代解釋，以及暫定判斷的理由。若證據不能作出決定，就要讓限制顯明。43

應用時需要兼顧延續與差異。腓立比書對他人利益的關注能夠引導教會決策，但具體決定仍需要認識古代書信沒有提供的個人、需要和權力關係。啟示錄對忍耐見證的呼召可以鼓勵承受壓力的群體，但當代群體不可自動假定自己與每個古代教會完全相同，也不可把對手等同於可怕的象徵。詮釋既要對經文負責，也要考慮可以預見的後果。課堂應容許提問、保護受強迫性應用傷害的人，並容許讀者因證據而修訂看法。44

一本手冊的結束並非詮釋工作的結束。讀者仍須反覆回到原始文本、語言、文學結構、歷史來源、不同學者的解釋及神學判斷。應清楚說明從觀察走向推論的道路，並區分一段經文所支持的主張，與跨越整本正典的較廣綜合。邀請他人檢驗論證，也承認證據的限制。這些習慣使讀者能同時正視古代著作的特定處境與其持續挑戰，而無須讓其中一項取消另一項。45

應用不只是用「所以我們應當」開頭的句子。它需要指出文本觀察，解釋古代處境與今天處境之間的關係，並說明所建議的行動或態度。每一步都可能受到討論。群體可能同意腓立比書鼓勵關心他人，卻對有限資源應如何分配有不同意見。這種分歧除了需要解經，也需要實際知識與倫理商議。若聲稱經文已決定所有細節，便會掩蓋當代群體仍須作出的判斷。46

負責任的應用也要指明對象。向有權力的人提出關心他人的要求，不一定等同要求受強迫的人保持沉默。古代書信對群體的勸勉需要審慎的道德判斷，不能以忽略權力不對等的方式直接套用。教師應邀請受影響的人發聲，並區分所提出的應用、經文直接引用和普遍規則。當神學詮釋的實踐推理可以接受檢驗時，它便更能向人負責。47

啟示錄讀者同樣需要區分書卷的象徵描繪，與自己對當代人物或制度的鑑定。文本對妥協的警告與對忍耐的呼籲可能與今天的群體相關，但由古代意象走向指認現代敵人，仍是額外的詮釋步驟。這一步可能造成嚴重後果，不能悄悄放進經文改述中，好像文本已經明說。比較不同應用時，應評估它們是否忠於段落、是否留意處境，以及如何對待受影響的人。48

最後，可以透過同儕審閱檢驗整條論證鏈。請一位不知道作者預設結論的讀者找出經文、重現觀察，並指出每一項推論。讀者能否知道實際查閱了哪部學術著作？替代解釋是否得到公平描述？聖經引文與學者重建是否清楚區分？所提出的應用是否解釋延續和差異，而不是單單宣稱兩者存在？若答案是否定的，就應修訂論證或說明限制。目的不是消除所有分歧，而是讓證據與其後果能夠進入負責任的討論。49

當詮釋者有分歧，先找出分歧所在：究竟是希臘文本的讀法、段落界限、字詞意義、歷史實踐的鑑定，還是某個主張的神學含義？不同問題需要不同證據。一塊新碑文可能改變歷史重建，卻不能自動解決語法爭議；語法論證可能使子句更清楚，卻不能單獨決定一段經文如何指引當代機構。逐步列出這些問題，有助參與者發現彼此是否在每一點都不同意，或其實有相當多共同基礎。50

修訂記錄可以保留最初判斷、最有力的競爭解釋，以及促使作者改變的證據。寫作者不必假裝已獲得確定性：暫定結論也可能是現有證據下最好的判斷。重要的是讀者能看見結論如何依賴證據，以及甚麼情況會使它需要修訂。這種態度同時適用於課堂討論和學術出版；它鼓勵細緻論證，為糾正留下空間，也維持信念與證明之間的區別。51
