Reading Hebrew Poetry 閱讀希伯來詩歌

Hebrew poetry is not identified simply by short lines on a printed page. Editors make decisions about lineation, and ancient manuscripts do not always present the visual layout familiar from modern translations. Readers should therefore begin with syntax, repeated forms, sound, imagery, and the relation between adjacent clauses. The point is not to deny poetic form but to locate the evidence for it in the language and literary context. A single formal feature cannot settle whether a passage is poetry, song, prayer, or a mixture of forms.01

The paired lines of Hebrew poetry frequently resemble each other, yet their relationship is not always mere repetition. A second clause may specify, intensify, qualify, oppose, or complete the first. James Kugel describes the second member as frequently carrying the first further. This is an interpretive proposal about a common literary movement, not a rule that every second line must introduce an entirely new idea. Read the pair aloud, observe what changes, and ask how the second member affects the reader’s understanding of the first.02

Psalm 1:1 offers an instructive pattern. Three negated actions move through walking, standing, and sitting; their associated phrases name counsel, way, and company. The pattern creates a cumulative description of association with wrongdoing. It is possible to hear a progression from movement to settled presence, but that rhetorical reading should be distinguished from a claim that every Hebrew reader must infer precisely the same sequence. The paired and repeated elements invite comparison without requiring a mechanical code.03

Psalm 1:2 sets delight in divine instruction against the previous negative descriptions. The repeated reference to instruction and the expression “day and night” portray sustained engagement, not necessarily a timetable specifying every activity. Verse 3 then introduces the planted tree: fruit appears in its season and the leaves do not wither. A simile asks the reader to consider selected points of comparison, such as nourishment and fruitfulness, without assuming that every detail of a tree corresponds to a separate theological proposition.04

The contrast with chaff in Psalm 1:4 is both visual and argumentative: the wind drives away what the tree image has presented as rooted and sustained. Verse 5 moves from imagery to judgment and assembly; verse 6 concludes with two ways and their different outcomes. A responsible reading tracks these transitions rather than treating the images as independent decorative pictures. The psalm’s stark contrasts also invite comparison with other biblical texts that describe the apparent prosperity of the wicked, without suppressing the particular voice of either passage.05

A translation is itself an interpretation of Hebrew grammar, vocabulary, and poetic lineation. Compare two translations when a term or clause matters to an argument, then consult the Hebrew where competence permits. If they differ, state the nature of the difference: word choice, the division of a clause, or a more substantial textual issue. Do not treat different English wording as proof of different Hebrew manuscripts. Likewise, an apparent verbal echo in translation needs checking in the original language before it is used as an argument for dependence.06

The psalm’s final claim that the Lord knows the way of the righteous is not reducible to a detached definition of the verb “know.” Its object is the way, and the closing contrast is the perishing way of the wicked. The reader should ask how the clause functions in the whole poem before importing every possible lexical meaning. The verse answers the preceding contrast by placing the two ways before divine judgment; it does not supply a complete philosophical account of human flourishing.07

A practical exercise is to map one poetic unit in three columns: the words and clauses observed; the relationship proposed between them; and an alternative that remains possible. For Psalm 1, a reader might observe repeated negations, propose cumulative movement, and note that the degree of progression is interpretive. The table makes a useful distinction between what another reader can see immediately and the judgment advanced about its effect. Such discipline is compatible with theological reading because it clarifies the route by which the theological claim is reached.08

希伯來詩歌不能只靠現代譯本一行較短的排版來辨認。編者必須決定如何分行，而古代抄本不一定採用今天熟悉的視覺格式。因此，讀者應先注意句法、重複形式、聲音、意象，以及相鄰子句之間的關係。這並非否認詩體的存在，而是要在語言和文學處境中尋找證據。單一形式特徵不能自動判定一段究竟是詩、歌、禱告，還是多種形式的結合。01

希伯來詩歌的兩個詩句經常相似，但兩者的關係不一定只是重複。第二個子句可能具體說明、加強、限制、對比或完成第一句。James Kugel 認為第二部分經常把第一部分向前推進。這是對常見文學運作提出的解釋，不是說第二句每次都必須加入全新的意思。讀者可以朗讀兩句，觀察哪些字詞改變，再問第二句如何影響第一句的理解。02

詩篇一篇一節提供可觀察的例子。三個否定行動依次出現：行走、站立和坐下；相關語句提到計謀、道路與座位。這種形式逐步累積對與惡相連的描述。讀者可以聽出由行走走向停留的推進，但這種修辭理解應與「每個希伯來文讀者都必然讀出完全相同次序」的主張區分。重複與並列邀請比較，卻不要求機械式解碼。03

詩篇一篇二節以喜愛上主教導的描述，與前面的否定形式形成對照。對教導的重複提及及「晝夜」一語呈現持續的投入，並不必然規定生活中每項活動的時間表。三節接着引入栽種的樹：它按時結果，葉子不枯乾。比喻邀請讀者思考滋養和結果等相關對應，但不要求把樹木的每個細節都變成獨立神學命題。04

詩篇一篇四節以糠秕形成既可見又具有論證功能的對照：風把糠秕吹散，與前面樹木扎根並得到滋養的畫面不同。五節由意象轉到審判與義人的群體；六節以兩條道路及不同結局結束。負責任的閱讀應追蹤這些轉換，而非把意象當成互不相連的裝飾。詩篇鮮明的對比也邀請讀者與其他描寫惡人似乎亨通的經文比較，但不應抹去任何一段的獨特聲音。05

譯文本身已涉及對希伯來語法、詞彙及詩句分行的判斷。若某個字詞或子句對論證重要，應比較兩種譯本，並在能力容許時查考希伯來文。兩者若不同，要指出差異究竟來自選詞、子句劃分，還是較深層的文本問題。不能只因英文不同，便斷定底本不同；同樣，譯文中看似相似的詞語，在用來支持文獻依賴關係之前，也須核對原文。06

詩篇結尾說上主知道義人的道路，不能被縮減為「知道」一詞脫離上下文的字典定義。動詞的受詞是道路，而結尾的對照是惡人的道路滅亡。讀者在搬入詞典中的各種可能意思以前，應先問這句話在全詩中有何功能。這一節把兩條道路置於上主的判斷之下，回應前面的對比；它沒有提供一套關於人類昌盛的完整哲學體系。07

實際練習時，可以用三欄整理一段詩：實際觀察到的字詞和子句、所提出的關係，以及仍然可能的其他解釋。以詩篇一篇為例，讀者可先記下重複的否定，再提出累積推進的理解，同時承認推進程度是一項詮釋判斷。表格有助區分他人可以直接看見的文本與讀者對其效果所作的推論。這種紀律與神學閱讀並不衝突，因為它讓神學主張的推論途徑更清楚。08

Lament, Address and Metaphor 哀歌、呼求與意象

A lament is an act of address as well as a report of distress. It may name an absent or threatening situation, call upon God, request intervention, and voice trust or praise. These elements do not always occur in one fixed order, and not every lament ends with a declaration that suffering has ceased. Describing a psalm’s parts can help readers follow its movement, but a rigid checklist may conceal the way a particular prayer refuses or modifies an expected pattern.09

The Hebrew numbering of Psalm 13 counts the superscription as verse 1; many English editions number the first question as verse 1 instead of verse 2. This distinction matters whenever a note directs readers to a specific clause. In the Hebrew text, four questions beginning “how long” occupy verses 2–3. Their repeated opening gives the speaker’s waiting an audible form. The questions address divine hiddenness, personal anguish, and the enemy’s apparent advantage without requiring the interpreter to reconstruct a precise historical crisis.10

The imperatives in Psalm 13:4 ask God to look, answer, and give light to the speaker’s eyes. The following clauses give reasons for urgency: the fear of death and the enemy’s boast. Reading the prayer as a sequence makes it difficult to replace the petition with an abstract statement that faithful people never feel abandoned. The poem preserves the speaker’s experience as speech directed to God. Its imagery of sleeping in death is a poetic expression within a plea, not a complete map of the state of the dead.11

The final Hebrew verse, Psalm 13:6, speaks of trust in divine steadfast love, rejoicing in deliverance, and singing because of divine beneficence. It changes the tone but does not explain the exact circumstances under which the change occurred. Some readers understand a movement toward renewed confidence; others ask whether a change of tense reflects a remembered act, anticipated rescue, or liturgical convention. The important first observation is more modest: the poem itself moves from repeated questions and urgent requests to an affirmation of trust and song.12

Psalm 23 combines images without reducing them to a single visual scene. The opening presents the Lord as shepherd, with pasture, water, and guidance. In verse 4 the speaker addresses God directly while walking through a dark valley; in verse 5 a prepared table and abundant cup introduce hospitality imagery. Interpreters may discuss whether these images belong to one extended field or move from one scene to another. The observed change of address and images should be described before selecting a preferred account.13

The shepherd image should be read with the poem’s own verbs: leading, restoring, guiding, and comforting. It is inappropriate to infer every detail of ancient pastoral practice solely from the presence of the noun “shepherd.” Historical study can enrich the picture when it provides dated evidence, but even a well-attested custom does not establish that every feature is active in this poem. The movement from imagery to theological claim must be argued, especially when a contemporary reader applies an ancient image to a situation very different from the speaker’s.14

A second practical exercise distinguishes the speaker, addressee, and audience at each turn. In Psalm 23:1–3 the Lord is spoken of in the third person; in verse 4 the speech turns to “you.” The change does not by itself prove a change in historical authorship or a later editorial seam. It does, however, give the reader evidence for the prayer’s rhetorical intimacy. Asking what the shift does in the poem is different from claiming to know the private emotional history of the person who first composed it.15

In teaching lament, attend to the people hearing the text. A psalm that gives words to anger or fear should not be used to force a grieving person to move immediately to a prescribed emotional outcome. The ending of Psalm 13 can be read in relation to its questions without treating the intervening verses as a schedule for recovery. Reading in community allows praise and protest to be heard together while respecting the distinct histories and vulnerabilities of actual readers.16

哀歌既是向對象發出的言語，也是對困境的描述。它可以說出缺席或威脅的處境、呼求上主、要求介入，並表達信靠或讚美。這些元素不一定按照固定次序出現，也不是每首哀歌最後都宣告痛苦已經停止。分析詩篇的部分有助追蹤其推進；但若把清單當成僵硬規則，就可能忽略某篇禱告如何拒絕或修改預期形式。09

詩篇十三篇的希伯來文編號把標題算作第一節；不少英文譯本則把第一個問題編為第一節，而不是第二節。註腳若指向具體子句，就必須注意這個差異。在希伯來文本中，二至三節有四次以「直到何時」開始的問題。反覆的開頭使說話者的等待成為可以聽見的形式。問題涉及上主隱藏、個人的痛苦與仇敵似乎佔優，卻不要求詮釋者重建某個確定的歷史危機。10

詩篇十三篇四節的祈使語求上主看顧、回應，並使說話者眼目明亮。接下來的子句說明迫切的原因：對死亡的恐懼及仇敵可能的誇口。把禱告讀成一個推進中的整體，就難以用「有信心的人從不感到被離棄」這種抽象句子取代祈求。詩篇保留說話者向上主表達的經驗。「睡了死覺」是懇求中的詩性語言，不是對死後狀態的完整地圖。11

希伯來文十三篇末節提到信靠上主的慈愛、因拯救歡樂，並因上主的恩待而歌唱。語氣確實改變，但經文沒有解釋這種改變發生時的確切處境。有讀者看見重拾信心的推進；也有人詢問動詞形式究竟指向過去記憶、期待中的拯救，或禮儀慣例。最基本的觀察應較為謹慎：詩歌本身由重複問題與迫切祈求，轉向信靠與歌唱的表達。12

詩篇二十三篇結合多個意象，不能簡化成一幅完全一致的圖畫。開頭把上主描繪為牧者，並提及草場、水與引導；四節的說話者走過幽暗山谷時直接向上主說話；五節預備的筵席和滿溢的杯則帶出款待意象。詮釋者可以討論這些圖像是否屬於一個延伸意象，或由一個場景轉到另一場景；但在選擇解釋之前，應先描述稱呼和意象實際出現的變化。13

牧者意象應與詩中動詞一起閱讀：引導、使生命復甦、帶領及安慰。不能只因出現「牧者」一詞，便推論古代牧羊生活的每一個細節。歷史研究若能提供有年代根據的證據，可以豐富畫面；然而，即使某種習俗確實存在，也不代表它的所有細節都在本詩中發揮作用。由意象走向神學主張需要論證，尤其當現代讀者把古代圖像應用於與詩人十分不同的處境時。14

第二個實際練習是逐一區分說話者、受話者及聽眾。詩篇二十三篇一至三節以第三人稱談及上主，四節則轉向「你」。這個改變本身不能證明作者更換或後來編輯的接縫；然而，它確實提供證據，讓讀者討論禱告中的直接親近。詢問稱呼轉換在詩中產生甚麼效果，與宣稱知道最初作者私人的情緒歷史，是兩種不同的主張。15

教導哀歌時，應留意實際聆聽經文的人。一首為憤怒或恐懼提供語言的詩，不應被用來迫使悲傷的人立即達到預設情緒結果。詩篇十三篇的結尾可以與前面的問題一起閱讀，卻不能把其間的經節變成康復時間表。群體閱讀容許讚美與抗議同時被聽見，也尊重實際讀者各自的歷史和脆弱處境。16

Wisdom and the Proverb 智慧與箴言

A proverb is a concise, memorable form of instruction. Its brevity makes it portable: a saying can be recalled in conversation, deliberation, or correction. Brevity also requires judgment, because a sentence cannot include every condition relevant to its use. Readers should consider the literary collection, the relationship between speaker and addressee, the actions commended or criticized, and the consequences envisaged. Treating every saying as a mathematical formula can obscure the discernment that wisdom itself seeks to cultivate.17

Proverbs 1:2–4 introduces learning, discipline, discernment, justice, and prudence as aims of instruction. The passage addresses the inexperienced and the young, yet verse 5 also invites the wise to listen and increase in learning. Wisdom is therefore not merely a possession divided between two permanently fixed classes. The opening portrays a process of listening and acquiring understanding. Verse 7 places the fear of the Lord at the beginning of knowledge while contrasting those who despise wisdom and discipline.18

Proverbs 26:4–5 places two commands next to each other: do not answer a fool according to folly; answer a fool according to folly. Each has its own warning introduced by the Hebrew particle פֶּן, “lest.” The first warns that the respondent might become like the fool; the second warns that the fool might remain wise in his own eyes. The juxtaposition gives readers two dangers to weigh. It would be premature to remove the tension by declaring one verse universally valid and the other irrelevant.19

The difference between the two sayings can be expressed as a practical question: when will participation reproduce foolishness, and when will silence leave a harmful claim unchallenged? The text itself does not provide a decision tree for every situation. It trains attention to consequences rather than authorizing a reader to label a disagreeing person a fool without argument. A wise response considers the audience, vulnerability, public effects, and the actual form of the claim. The sayings are concise prompts for judgment, not substitutes for it.20

The opening of Proverbs also warns against turning the contrast between wisdom and folly into a claim that the teacher has nothing to learn. The wise person in 1:5 listens and gains instruction. When a contemporary community uses a proverb to correct another person, it can also ask whether its own conduct exemplifies the discernment, justice, and equity commended in the introduction. A proverb applied only outward can become a tool for avoiding self-examination.21

Wisdom texts include poems, speeches, questions, observations, and extended debate, not only short aphorisms. A reader moving from an individual proverb to Job’s long dialogues or Qoheleth’s reflections should attend to changes in speaker and literary situation. The introduction of a view into a dialogue does not automatically identify it as the final verdict of the book. First ask who says it, to whom, under what pressure, and how the surrounding speech responds.22

Comparing wisdom books does not require flattening Proverbs into naive optimism or Job into a simple denial of all moral order. The books select different forms, voices, and situations. Their placement in a shared canon enables readers to ask how instruction, observation, protest, and divine speech relate, but the historical question of direct literary dependence requires additional evidence. A canonical conversation can be fruitful even when the direction or date of influence cannot be established.23

A classroom comparison should make its claims visible. Ask one group to paraphrase Proverbs 26:4 and another to paraphrase 26:5; then ask each group to identify the particular danger named by its verse. Only after both dangers are on the table should the class consider contemporary analogies. This order prevents a preferred application from erasing half the textual evidence. It also encourages the charitable recognition that opposing responses may address different risks rather than merely express inconsistent rules.24

箴言是一種簡潔、便於記憶的教導形式。由於篇幅短，語句可以在交談、商議或勸戒時被記起；但簡短也要求判斷，因為一句話不能列出使用時所有相關條件。讀者應留意它所屬的文集、說話者與受話者的關係、受到稱許或批評的行動，以及經文設想的後果。若把每句話都當成數學公式，反而可能遮蔽智慧本身要培養的分辨能力。17

箴言一章二至四節以智慧、教訓、分辨、公義和精明等詞語說明學習的目的。經文提到經驗不足的人及年輕人，但五節也邀請智慧人聆聽並增長學問。因此，智慧不只是兩個永遠固定群體之間分配好的財產。這個開頭描寫聆聽和學習理解的過程；七節把敬畏上主放在知識的起點，並與藐視智慧及教訓的人形成對比。18

箴言二十六章四至五節把兩個命令並置：不要按愚妄回答愚昧人；要按愚妄回答愚昧人。每節都有希伯來文 פֶּן 引出的警告，可譯為「免得」。前者擔心回答的人會變得像愚昧人；後者擔心愚昧人自以為有智慧。兩句並置要求讀者衡量兩種危險。若宣布其中一句無條件適用、另一句無關緊要，就過早消除了文本保留的張力。19

兩句話的差異可以整理成實際問題：甚麼時候參與爭論會複製愚妄？甚麼時候沉默會讓有害的主張得不到挑戰？經文沒有為每個情況提供決策樹。它訓練讀者注意後果，卻不授權人不經論證便把持不同意見者稱為愚昧。智慧的回答需要考慮聽眾、脆弱處境、公共影響及主張本身的形式。箴言是促進判斷的簡潔提示，不能取代判斷。20

箴言開頭也提醒我們，不能把智慧與愚妄的對比變成「教師自己不必學習」的主張。一章五節的智慧人會聆聽並增長學問。當今天的群體用箴言糾正他人時，也可以反問自己：我們的行動是否呈現序言所稱許的分辨、公義和正直？若一句箴言只被用來指向外人，就可能成為逃避自我檢驗的工具。21

智慧文學除了短小格言，也包含詩歌、演說、問題、觀察與長篇辯論。讀者由單句箴言轉到約伯記的長篇對話，或傳道者的反思時，應留意說話者與文學處境的變化。在對話中出現的一種看法，不會自動等同全書最後判決。首先要問：誰說了這句話？向誰說？在甚麼壓力下說？周圍的言論又如何回應？22

比較智慧書並不要求把箴言簡化成天真的樂觀主義，也不必把約伯記當成對一切道德秩序的否定。各書選用不同形式、聲音和處境。它們同在正典中，使讀者能詢問教導、觀察、抗議及上主說話之間的關係；但若要主張歷史上存在直接文獻依賴，仍需要更多證據。即使無法確定影響方向或年代，正典之間的對話仍然可以有意義。23

課堂比較應讓主張清楚可見。可以請一組學生改寫箴言二十六章四節，另一組改寫五節，再請兩組指出自己一節所說的特定危險。只有在兩項危險都被提出之後，才討論當代類比。這個次序避免偏好的應用抹去一半經文證據，也鼓勵讀者公允地承認：看似相反的回應，可能針對不同風險，而不只是互相矛盾的規則。24

Job, Qoheleth and Limits 約伯、傳道者與人的界限

The Book of Job requires readers to distinguish its narrative frame from the speeches within it. A voice in a dispute can make a serious claim without the book requiring readers to accept every part of that claim. Observe who speaks, how another speaker responds, and what information the audience receives from the larger literary setting. This is especially important when claims about suffering are addressed to a person already in pain: the truth or inadequacy of a statement cannot be assessed without considering how it is used.25

Job 38:1 introduces divine speech from the storm. The questions that follow ask where Job was at the founding of the earth and who fixed its measurements. They shift the horizon from the immediate dispute to the scale and complexity of creation. The passage should not be paraphrased as though it gives a simple causal explanation for Job’s suffering: the selected verses ask questions about knowledge and the created world. Nor does the appearance of divine speech erase the difficult earlier conversations that make it necessary to read the book as a whole.26

The imagery of foundations, a measuring line, bases, and a cornerstone in Job 38:4–6 draws on recognizable construction language. Verse 7 adds singing morning stars and divine beings shouting for joy. These expressions invite close attention to the literary representation of creation. They should not be treated as a literal engineering diagram, nor should a reader invent a complete ancient cosmology from one image. The images work in a sequence of questions directed to Job and must be interpreted within that rhetorical movement.27

Ecclesiastes 3:1–8 organizes a sequence around the repeated word for “time.” Contrasting activities appear in pairs: birth and death, planting and uprooting, weeping and laughing, silence and speech, war and peace. Repetition makes the poem memorable while the pairs place sharply different experiences within a shared temporal frame. Not every pair is equally easy to interpret. A responsible reader can identify the pattern without claiming to know the precise practical occasion in which each action is commanded.28

Ecclesiastes 3:9 immediately asks what advantage the worker gains from toil. The question guards against isolating the famous catalogue from the inquiry that follows. C. L. Seow argues that the poem in its present context concerns divine activity and the human response rather than simply the human power to choose the appropriate moment. That is a substantive interpretation of the sequence; it is useful to compare it with readings that emphasize prudent timing while asking how each explains the question in verse 9 and the subsequent reference to God’s work.29

The list’s opposites should not be converted automatically into a blanket moral approval of every action at some unspecified time. The poem names killing and healing, war and peace, within a pattern; determining a particular action’s ethical status requires attention to its context and to broader argument. Conversely, a reader should not suppress the disturbing members of the list to make the poem uniformly comforting. Its form brings difficult contrasts into the open and asks the interpreter to explain their relation to the following reflection.30

One way to compare Job and Qoheleth is to trace their questions rather than assign each book a slogan. Job 38:4 asks where the human addressee stood when the earth was founded; Ecclesiastes 3:9 asks what advantage the worker has from toil. Their grammatical forms, speakers, and immediate subjects differ. Reading them side by side can deepen a discussion of human knowledge and limits, but similarity of theme alone does not demonstrate direct quotation or literary dependence.31

Pastoral application requires particular care with texts about suffering. A reader who quotes a speech from Job to another person should first identify the speaker and the situation in the narrative. One should also distinguish an invitation to contemplate the vastness of creation from a claim that a particular sufferer’s loss has been explained. The literary dialogue gives space for protest, disagreement, and silence; a contemporary use that silences a grieving person can contradict the very careful listening that the book demands.32

約伯記要求讀者區分敘事框架與其中的各種言論。辯論中的聲音可以提出嚴肅主張，但全書不一定要求讀者接受其中每一部分。應觀察誰在說話、另一位說話者如何回應，以及讀者從較廣的文學處境得到甚麼資訊。當關於苦難的主張向一位正在受痛的人說出時，這點尤其重要：若不考慮其使用方式，便難以評估一句話是否充分。25

約伯記三十八章一節引入上主從旋風中說話。接下來的問題問約伯在大地立基時身在何處，以及誰決定其尺寸。這些問題把視野從眼前爭辯轉向受造世界的廣闊與複雜。不能把所選經文改寫為對約伯苦難的簡單因果解釋；這幾節實際提出的是關於知識與世界的問題。上主開始說話，也不會抹去先前艱難的對話，因此仍須把整卷書一起閱讀。26

約伯記三十八章四至六節以根基、測量繩、底座和房角石等可辨認的建造語言描寫大地。七節又加入晨星一同歌唱、神的眾子歡呼的圖畫。這些表達邀請讀者仔細觀察創造如何被文學性呈現；它們不應被當成字面的工程圖，也不能憑單一意象創造一套完整古代宇宙論。這些圖像出現在一系列向約伯提出的問題中，必須放在該修辭推進裏理解。27

傳道書三章一至八節圍繞「時候」一詞的重複安排一連串相對的活動：出生與死亡、栽種與拔出、哭與笑、沉默與說話、戰爭與和平。重複使詩容易記憶，成對的詞語則把差異很大的經驗放進共同的時間框架。並非每一組都同樣容易解釋。負責任的讀者可以辨認形式，而不必宣稱自己知道每一個行動在甚麼精確處境下被命令。28

傳道書三章九節緊接着問：工作的人從勞碌中得到甚麼益處？這個問題提醒讀者，不要把著名的時間列表與後面展開的探問分開。C. L. Seow 主張，這首詩在現有上下文中關注上主的行動及人的回應，而不只是人能否選擇適當時機。這是對整段推進所作的具體解釋。讀者可以把它與強調審慎時機的讀法比較，並問各自如何解釋九節的問題與隨後對上主作為的描述。29

列表中的相對詞語不能自動變成「每個行動總有某個時候在道德上正當」的全面認可。詩在形式中列出殺戮與醫治、戰爭與和平；要判斷具體行動的倫理地位，仍須考慮其處境與更廣的論證。反過來，也不能為了讓詩完全令人安慰，就刪去令人不安的項目。形式把艱難的對比呈現出來，要求詮釋者說明它們與後續反思的關係。30

比較約伯與傳道者的一個方法，是追蹤各自的問題，而不是先替每卷書貼上口號。約伯記三十八章四節問受話的人在大地立基時身在何處；傳道書三章九節問工作的人從勞碌中得到甚麼益處。兩句的語法形式、說話者和直接主題都不同。並讀可以深化對人類知識及界限的討論，但主題相近本身不能證明直接引用或文獻依賴。31

把苦難經文用於牧養時需要特別謹慎。若讀者要向別人引用約伯記中的一段話，應先辨認說話者及敘事處境；也應區分「邀請人思想創造的廣闊」與「宣稱已經解釋某人所受的損失」。文學對話為抗議、分歧與沉默留出空間；當代運用若令悲傷的人無法說話，就可能違背本書所要求的細心聆聽。32

Prophetic Speech and Rhetoric 先知言語與修辭

Prophetic books bring together speeches, visions, symbolic actions, narratives, and editorial arrangements. A prophetic oracle should therefore be read both as a unit with its own speaker and audience and as part of the book in which it now stands. A messenger formula, an accusation, a warning, a call to hear, or a promise can help identify a speech’s rhetorical form, but labels are tools rather than proof of a single fixed template. A text may combine forms or resist a neat classification.33

Amos 5:21–23 presents a series of divine rejections: festivals, assemblies, offerings, and musical sound are addressed in forceful language. The speaker’s rejection is not merely reported by an observer; the oracle presents divine speech in the first person. The accumulation of verbs and ritual practices creates rhetorical pressure before the positive image of justice and righteousness in verse 24. Read the rejection and the demand together rather than choosing only the language that supports a prior preference.34

The Hebrew of Amos 5:24 places מִשְׁפָּט and צְדָקָה in parallel with water and a perennial stream. The imagery suggests movement and persistence, while the nouns invite an inquiry into justice and righteousness in the wider book. The metaphor does not itself provide a detailed institutional programme; nor does its poetic form make the demand merely symbolic. The interpreter must connect the image with the surrounding accusations and with the historical and social evidence available for the book.35

John J. Collins reads Amos’s criticism as a challenge to worship that is separated from justice, rather than an invitation to infer that every form of worship is inherently wrong. That is an interpretation of the prophetic rhetoric and its larger social context, not a sentence explicitly stated in these four verses. It should be tested against the book’s other accusations and against alternative accounts of the relation between ritual and ethics. Distinguishing the text’s words from a scholar’s inference makes the discussion more precise.36

Isaiah 1:10 begins with a call to hear the divine word and instruction, addressing leaders and people through the names Sodom and Gomorrah. The language evokes a comparison; it does not require readers to ignore the actual audience named by the surrounding book. Verses 11–15 challenge sacrifices, assemblies, and prayer, culminating in the accusation of blood on the hearers’ hands. The speech’s terms must be read as rhetoric directed to a community, not as permission to identify any present-day group with the ancient addressees without argument.37

Isaiah 1:16–17 changes to a sequence of imperatives: wash, remove evil, cease doing harm, learn good, seek justice, and attend to the vulnerable. The change from accusation to instruction matters. A summary that speaks only about rejected offerings misses the positive direction of the unit, while a summary that lists social duties without the preceding speech overlooks the rhetorical force of the divine challenge. A good proposition explains how the two movements relate without reducing either to an incidental illustration.38

Historical setting matters, but it must be argued from evidence rather than inserted as a colourful background. Ask which people and places the book names, which historical claims are explicit, and what is inferred from archaeology or other ancient texts. A plausible eighth-century setting for an oracle does not establish the date of every editorial layer in a prophetic book. Distinguish a historical reconstruction of a speech from the literary arrangement of the book as received, and identify which question the present argument addresses.39

A prophecy can move between judgment and restoration, but the appearance of a hopeful promise elsewhere does not authorize a reader to erase a particular warning. Conversely, a warning should not be made the last word of an entire book without reading its literary conclusion. Attend to shifts in addressee, time reference, and speaking voice. When interpreters disagree about whether a passage reflects one historical setting or several stages of composition, describe their evidence rather than presenting a disputed reconstruction as a fact in the biblical text.40

先知書匯集言論、異象、象徵行動、敘事和編輯安排。因此，閱讀先知神諭時，既要留意單元本身的說話者與聽眾，也要考慮它在現存書卷中的位置。使者公式、指控、警告、呼籲聆聽或應許，都可幫助辨認言論的修辭形式；但標籤只是工具，不能證明所有文本遵循一個固定模板。一段經文可能結合多種形式，也可能不適合被整齊歸類。33

阿摩司書五章二十一至二十三節連續呈現上主的拒絕：節期、聚會、祭物與音樂都受到強烈言語對待。這不只是旁觀者報告某個拒絕；神諭以第一人稱呈現上主說話。多個動詞和敬拜行動的累積形成修辭壓力，然後二十四節才提出公平與公義的積極意象。閱讀時應同時考慮拒絕與要求，而不是只挑選符合既有偏好的語句。34

阿摩司書五章二十四節的希伯來文把 מִשְׁפָּט 與 צְדָקָה 和水及長流不息的河流並列。意象提示流動與持續，而這兩個名詞也邀請讀者在全書中研究公平與公義。比喻本身沒有提供詳細的制度方案；但它是詩，也不代表要求只是象徵。詮釋者必須把意象連接到周圍的指控，以及研究本書時可以取得的歷史和社會證據。35

John J. Collins 把阿摩司的批判理解為對敬拜與公義分離的挑戰，而不是邀請讀者推論一切敬拜形式本身都是錯誤。這是對先知修辭及其較廣社會處境的解釋，並不是這四節直接說出的一句話。該理解應與全書其他指控及禮儀與倫理關係的其他讀法一起檢驗。分辨經文原話和學者推論，可以使討論更精確。36

以賽亞書一章十節呼籲人聆聽上主的話語和教導，以所多瑪及蛾摩拉的名字稱呼領袖與百姓。這種語言喚起比較，卻不要求讀者忽略全書處境中的實際聽眾。十一至十五節質疑祭物、聚會和禱告，最後指出聽眾的手滿有血。應把這些字詞讀作向特定群體發出的修辭，而不能不經論證便把今天任何群體直接等同古代受話者。37

以賽亞書一章十六至十七節轉向一連串命令：洗淨、除掉惡行、停止作惡、學習行善、尋求公平，以及關顧脆弱的人。由指控轉到教導的變化十分重要。若摘要只說祭物被拒，就忽略了段落的積極方向；若只列出社會責任而略去前面的言論，也會忽略上主挑戰所帶來的修辭力量。好的命題應說明兩個推進如何相連，而不把任何一方縮減成附帶例子。38

歷史處境重要，但必須由證據論證，而不是當成生動背景直接放進文本。應問本書提及哪些人物與地點、哪些歷史主張是明說的，以及哪些是根據考古或其他古文獻推論。某段神諭合理地與主前八世紀相關，並不確立先知書每一層編輯的年代。要區分對言論的歷史重建與現存書卷的文學安排，也要交代當前論證究竟在回答哪一類問題。39

先知宣講可以在審判與復興之間轉換，但別處出現盼望的應許，並不授權讀者抹去某個具體警告。反過來，也不能不讀全書結尾，就把某項警告當成整卷書最後的話。應留意受話者、時間指涉與說話聲音的變化。若詮釋者對某段反映單一歷史處境還是多個形成階段有分歧，就要說明各自的證據，而不是把仍有爭議的重建當成經文直接陳述的事實。40

From Form to Responsible Application 由形式走向負責任的應用

A complete reading begins by defining a question proportionate to the passage. “What does this poem communicate through its movement and imagery?” is more manageable than “What does the Bible teach about every form of suffering?” A second question is legitimate, but it requires a wider corpus and an explicit account of how distinct texts are brought together. The scale of a theological conclusion should match the scale of its evidence. A genre label can guide the inquiry, but it cannot carry the argument by itself.41

First read the unit in its immediate literary setting and identify participants. In Psalm 13, the speaker addresses God and speaks about an enemy; in Job 38, the narrator introduces the divine reply to Job; in Isaiah 1, the oracle addresses leaders and people. These are different communicative situations. A later reader may learn from each, but should not silently exchange the roles of speakers and addressees. The distinction makes both historical interpretation and contemporary application more responsible.42

Next mark the text’s movement with ordinary verbs. Psalm 13 asks, petitions, and affirms trust; Proverbs 26 places two warnings beside opposite instructions; Amos 5 rejects practices and introduces the image of justice flowing. These verbs are working descriptions rather than authoritative labels. They should be adjusted when a clause does more than one thing or when readers disagree about the boundary of a unit. The test is whether the map illuminates the actual sequence rather than forcing every passage into the same outline.43

Then compare the parts of a poetic line. Ask what the second clause repeats and what it changes: an image, a participant, an action, a consequence, or a perspective. Kugel’s discussion of the second member’s contribution encourages readers to see relation and development rather than treating parallelism as two independent sentences that happen to say the same thing. His proposal should be tested on individual examples; the analyst must not assume that all pairs behave identically.44

Fourth, make a witness and translation note. Identify the Hebrew text actually consulted, record verse numbering, and state when an ancient version or manuscript has not been independently checked. The Hebrew versification of Psalm 13 counts the superscription as verse 1, so its first question appears in verse 2. An English translation may place that question in verse 1. This difference of numbering should not be confused with a textual variant, and a claim about a Greek or Dead Sea Scroll witness requires that witness to be consulted.45

Fifth, compare interpretations at the point where they differ. In Ecclesiastes 3, a reader who hears the poem as practical counsel about choosing the right moment should explain the question about the worker’s gain in verse 9. Seow’s contextual reading emphasizes divine activity in the larger unit. A classroom discussion can place the proposals beside the text and ask what each explains well and what it leaves unresolved. A fair account states the rival interpretation in a form its advocate could recognize.46

Sixth, formulate a proposition that retains the form’s contribution. “The psalmist is honest” captures one feature of Psalm 13 but leaves out the movement from questions to petition and trust. A fuller proposal might say that the speaker brings prolonged distress before God, urgently asks for an answer, and nevertheless voices trust in divine steadfast love. This is a reading to be tested against the details, not another verse added to Scripture. It should be revised if a better account explains the same evidence more faithfully.47

Seventh, distinguish a theological inference from the words of the passage. Psalm 23’s pastoral imagery can support reflection on divine care, but a claim that believers will experience no danger would have to confront the dark valley and the presence of enemies within the poem. Similarly, Amos’s image of flowing justice can inform reflection on public life, but specific institutional proposals need additional reasoning and evidence. Faithful application names both continuity with the text and the differences in circumstance.48

Finally, test the proposed application with affected readers. When addressing grief, do not turn Psalm 13’s ending into a deadline for emotional recovery. When discussing justice, do not use prophetic rhetoric to make accusations without examining facts, responsibilities, and power. When applying a proverb, identify the situation to which it plausibly speaks and the limitations imposed by other relevant evidence. Careful application is not a retreat from conviction; it is the work of connecting a conviction to people and circumstances truthfully.49

The aim of genre competence is not mastery of a vocabulary list but better attention. Poetry asks readers to hear repetition and movement; wisdom calls for judgment about voices, situations, and consequences; prophetic speech directs attention to speaker, audience, accusation, demand, and promise. These categories overlap: a prophetic oracle may be poetic, and a psalm may contain wisdom instruction. The reader’s task is to let the particular passage determine how the categories work together.50

A classroom demonstration can begin with the questions of Psalm 13. Invite students to identify the one addressed, the person experiencing distress, and the anticipated effect of an enemy's boast. Then ask what evidence would be needed to identify the event behind the poem. A particular reconstruction may be possible in some psalms, but the repeated questions here do not supply a date or name the adversary. This distinction does not make the poem abstract. It allows its literary language to be heard without presenting a conjectural biography as though the text had supplied it. A reader can discuss the force of the questions, the urgency of the petition, and the turn to trust while leaving the precise occasion open.51

A similar exercise with Proverbs 26:4–5 can compare decisions rather than people. Present a hypothetical public claim that threatens to mislead others, and ask how a reply might correct it while avoiding imitation of its abuse. Then consider a private exchange in which further argument would reward provocation without helping anyone. These scenarios do not prove the original setting of either proverb. They test whether readers have heard both consequences named in the text. A careful teacher must also guard against equating disagreement, lack of education, or social disadvantage with folly. The proverbs do not authorize dismissal of people with less power to speak. Their practical use requires attention to the claim and to the effects of a response.52

In prophetic study, a useful comparison begins by reading Amos 5:21–24 and Isaiah 1:10–17 separately before listing similarities. Both challenge forms of worship, but Isaiah's selected passage explicitly includes commands to seek justice and defend vulnerable people, whereas Amos uses the image of water and a lasting stream. The difference matters as much as the resemblance: it prevents the interpreter from replacing both speeches with a generic slogan. After the comparison, ask what historical information is actually present in the texts and which additional claims depend on external sources. Shared concern does not establish that one passage directly copied the other. Comparison should clarify each voice rather than invent a story of literary dependence.53

When a congregation prays Psalm 23, familiar metaphors can appear self-evident. Slow reading restores their sequence: pasture and water, guidance, the valley, a table before adversaries, and an affirmation concerning goodness and faithful love. The psalm expresses trust in the presence of danger; it does not make danger disappear from its own imagery. A pastoral paraphrase that promises an immediate end to every threat should be evaluated against that sequence. A more textually accountable application might offer language for trust amid uncertainty while leaving room for help, protection, grief, and practical action. The point of careful reading is to preserve the contours through which the poem communicates its comfort.54

A research notebook can preserve the limits of a synthesis alongside its positive conclusion. Record the Hebrew form or translation actually consulted, the immediate literary setting, a credible alternative reading, and any witness not independently examined. For example, the commands in Proverbs 26:4–5 are directly observable in the Hebrew text; the circumstances of their first use are not supplied by those two verses. Likewise, the movement of Ecclesiastes 3:1–9 is observable, while its bearing on a particular modern decision requires argument. Writing such distinctions makes later revision easier and allows another reader to evaluate the claim. It also prevents the unavoidable selectivity of a short lesson from becoming an illusion of complete coverage.55

完整閱讀首先要提出與經文範圍相稱的問題。「這首詩如何透過推進與意象傳達意思？」比「聖經對一切形式的苦難有甚麼教導？」更容易在單篇文章中處理。後一問題同樣正當，卻需要更廣的經文範圍，並清楚交代如何把不同文本聯繫起來。神學結論的規模應與證據相稱。文體標籤可以引導探問，但不能獨自承擔整個論證。41

首先在直接文學上下文中閱讀單元，並辨認參與者。在詩篇十三篇，說話者向上主呼求，也提及仇敵；在約伯記三十八章，敘述者引入上主對約伯的回應；在以賽亞書一章，神諭向領袖與百姓說話。這是不同的溝通處境。後來的讀者可以從各段學習，卻不應在沒有說明時交換說話者和受話者的角色。這項區分使歷史解釋和當代應用都更負責任。42

接着用一般動詞標記文本推進。詩篇十三篇發問、懇求並表達信靠；箴言二十六章在相反命令旁各放一個警告；阿摩司書五章拒絕某些行動，並引入公義流動的意象。這些動詞只是工作性的描述，不是不可更改的權威標籤。若一個子句同時完成多項功能，或讀者對段落界限有分歧，就應修正圖表。檢驗的關鍵是圖表能否照亮實際次序，而不是強迫每段經文套用同一大綱。43

然後比較詩句的各個部分。應問第二個子句重複了甚麼，又改變了甚麼：意象、人物、行動、後果，還是觀看角度？Kugel 對第二部分如何作出貢獻的討論，鼓勵讀者看見關係與推進，而不是把平行句當作兩個恰好說同一意思的獨立句子。他的提議應在具體例子上檢驗；分析者不能假定所有句對都以相同方式運作。44

第四，記下文本見證與譯本。指出實際查閱的希伯來文本，記錄節數；若沒有獨立查核古譯本或抄本，也應明說。詩篇十三篇的希伯來文本把標題算作第一節，因此第一個問題在第二節；英文譯本可能把同一問題編為第一節。這種節數差異不能與文本異文混為一談；若要主張希臘譯本或死海古卷有某種讀法，就必須實際查考該見證。45

第五，在不同解釋真正分歧的地方進行比較。若讀者把傳道書三章的詩聽成選擇適當時機的實際忠告，就應解釋九節關於工作者得益的問題。Seow 對上下文的閱讀則強調較大單元中的上主行動。課堂討論可以把不同提議並列在經文旁，詢問每個讀法解釋了甚麼、又留下甚麼未解決。公平的比較應以支持者能夠辨認的方式陳述另一種解釋。46

第六，提出保留文體貢獻的命題。「詩人很誠實」抓住詩篇十三篇的一項特徵，卻沒有交代由問題到祈求及信靠的推進。較完整的提議可以說：說話者把長久困苦帶到上主面前，迫切求上主回應，卻仍表達對上主慈愛的信靠。這句話是一項要接受文本細節檢驗的解釋，並非加在聖經後面的另一節。若更好的說法能更忠實地解釋同一證據，就應修訂。47

第七，區分神學推論與經文本身的字句。詩篇二十三篇的牧者意象可以支持對上主照顧的反思，但若主張信徒不會經歷危險，就必須面對詩中幽暗山谷與仇敵的存在。同樣，阿摩司書中公義流動的圖像可以引導對公共生活的反思，但具體制度提議仍需要額外理由與證據。忠實應用應同時交代與文本的延續及處境的差異。48

最後，與受應用影響的人一起檢驗所提出的做法。面對悲傷時，不要把詩篇十三篇結尾變成情緒恢復的期限；討論公義時，不要在未查考事實、責任與權力關係以前，就借用先知修辭指控他人；應用箴言時，則要指出它合理適用的處境，以及其他相關證據帶來的限制。謹慎應用不是退縮，而是誠實地把信念與具體人物和處境連接起來。49

文體能力的目的，不是熟記術語清單，而是更細心地閱讀。詩歌要求讀者聆聽重複與推進；智慧文學要求判斷聲音、處境與後果；先知言語使人注意說話者、聽眾、指控、要求與應許。這些類別彼此重疊：先知神諭可以是詩，詩篇也可以包含智慧教導。讀者的工作是讓個別經文決定這些類別如何共同發揮作用。50

課堂示範可以從詩篇十三篇反覆的問題開始。教師可請學生指出說話者向誰發問、誰正在經歷困苦，以及仇敵誇口可能造成甚麼影響。接着可以問：若要確定詩篇背後的事件，還需要哪些證據？有些詩篇或許容許較具體的歷史重建，但這裏的問題沒有提供日期，也沒有說出仇敵名字。這種區分讓讀者聆聽詩歌，而不把猜測的傳記包裝成經文本身提供的資料。讀者可以討論問題的力量、懇求的迫切和信靠的轉折，同時保留對確切處境的疑問。51

箴言二十六章四至五節的類似練習可以比較決定，而不是替人物貼標籤。先設想一個可能誤導別人的公開主張，詢問如何回應才能糾正錯誤，同時避免模仿其中的辱罵；再設想一場私人爭論，繼續回應只會獎勵挑釁，卻不能幫助任何人。這些例子不能證明兩句箴言的原始處境，而是檢驗讀者是否聽見文本所指出的兩種後果。謹慎的教師也要避免把不同意見、教育機會不足或社會弱勢等同愚妄。應用時仍須注意具體言論及回應的影響。52

研究先知書時，可以先分別閱讀阿摩司書五章二十一至二十四節及以賽亞書一章十至十七節，然後才列出相似處。兩段都挑戰某些敬拜形式，但所選的以賽亞經文包含尋求公平及為脆弱者伸冤的一系列命令；阿摩司則使用水與長流河流的意象。差異與相似同樣重要，因為它阻止詮釋者用一個籠統口號取代兩段言論。完成比較後，應問文本實際提供哪些歷史資訊，哪些額外主張依賴外部資料。共同關切不能證明一段直接抄用另一段。比較的成果應是更清楚聽見各自的聲音。53

群體禱告詩篇二十三篇時，熟悉的意象可能顯得不證自明。慢讀可以重新看見其次序：草場與水、引導、山谷、在敵人面前預備的筵席，以及關於恩惠和慈愛的肯定。詩篇在危險存在時表達信靠，並沒有從自己的意象中刪去危險。若牧養性改寫保證每個威脅立即消失，便應根據這個次序重新檢驗。較忠於經文的應用可以為不確定中的信靠提供語言，同時容許人尋求幫助、保護、哀傷和實際行動。細讀的目的，是保留詩篇傳達安慰的具體輪廓。54

研究筆記可以把綜合結論的限制與積極主張一起保留下來。記下實際查閱的希伯來文或譯文、直接文學處境、可信的其他解釋，以及沒有獨立檢驗的文本見證。箴言二十六章四至五節中的命令可以直接在希伯來文本觀察，但那兩節沒有說明最初使用的具體場合。同樣，傳道書三章一至九節的推進可以觀察，其與某個現代決定的關係則需要論證。把這些區分寫下來，有助日後修訂，也讓其他讀者評估主張；這能避免短篇教學不可避免的選擇性，變成已經涵蓋一切的錯覺。55
