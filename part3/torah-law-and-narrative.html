01 · LAW & NARRATIVE 01 · 律法與敘事

Form, covenant and the problem of genre 律法不是單一形式

The Torah contains commands, case rulings, instruction, genealogy, poetry and narrative. These forms do not all communicate in the same way. A case law often describes an initial circumstance, adds qualifying facts and gives a consequence; a prohibition addresses an action directly. These labels are useful descriptions, not two sealed boxes that determine the origin of each sentence. A legal paragraph can contain both kinds of language, and narrative can present a law within the story of a people and its covenant. Begin by asking what work this particular form does in its present setting.01

A reader should locate a ruling in its immediate sequence before turning it into a universal proposition. Who acts? Who suffers harm? Which condition activates the ruling? What outcome is stated? Does the text distinguish an anticipated case from one involving knowledge, warning or prior action? If a modern summary deletes a condition, it may make the command broader or narrower than the passage itself. A careful outline makes the condition, actor and consequence visible, then tests whether adjacent cases qualify the first impression.02

The placement of law in covenant narrative is significant, but it does not mean that every provision has exactly the same audience or function. Exodus recounts deliverance and covenant formation before presenting a sequence of judgments. That sequence can be read as part of a larger literary movement without assuming that literary arrangement by itself settles all questions of composition or historical enforcement. The interpreter should distinguish the text as received, proposals about its formation, and evidence for actual judicial practice. They are connected questions, yet none supplies an automatic answer to the others.03

Comparative evidence also requires attention to form. An ancient legal collection and a biblical case may concern the same kind of injury while differing in its stated remedy, the status of those affected or the situation that activates the rule. These differences are evidence, not inconvenient details to erase. A comparison gains precision when it specifies date, language, provenance and genre for each witness, and when it asks whether a similar formulation could arise from shared practical problems rather than direct literary borrowing.04

Consider the risk of treating the presence of a stated penalty as evidence that it was imposed in every historical instance. A legal text can articulate norms, ideals, limits or examples; how a court acted in a particular case requires evidence beyond the formulation itself. Conversely, a lack of surviving court records does not prove that a rule had no effect. The historian's conclusion must be proportionate to the surviving evidence. A theological reader can ask how the literary presentation frames obligations while acknowledging that historical application remains a separate question.05

The relationship between a law's ancient addressees and modern communities must be argued rather than assumed. A reader may consider the passage's place within Israel's covenant, its moral vision, later biblical reception and the differences between ancient and contemporary institutions. None of those steps gives permission to disregard those whose vulnerability the passage names. Responsible application asks both what protection a ruling provides within its setting and which hierarchies or exclusions challenge a modern reader. The interpreter should make continuity and discontinuity explicit instead of allowing a familiar slogan to settle them silently.06

Genre awareness is therefore a discipline of listening. It prevents the interpreter from reading a narrative description as a direct rule or a legal example as though it contained every conceivable case. It also resists the opposite error of treating genre as a reason to avoid theological or ethical questions. First describe the passage's form; then ask what claims its structure supports, what assumptions it leaves implicit, and what additional evidence a larger judgment would require.07

妥拉包含命令、案例規定、教導、家譜、詩歌與敘事；不同形式並非以完全相同的方法傳達意思。案例律法常先描述情況，再加入限定條件並說明結果；直接禁令則針對某項行動發話。這些名稱有助描述，卻不是能自動決定每句來源的兩個密封箱。一段法律文字可能兼有兩種形式，敘事也可能把法律放在群體及盟約故事之中。首先要問：這個具體形式在現有上下文裏發揮甚麼作用？01

概括任何規定之前，先把它放回相鄰案例中。誰行動？誰受害？甚麼條件啟動規定？結果是甚麼？經文有否區分一般情況與當事人已經知情、受過警告或曾採取行動的情況？現代摘要若刪去限定條件，可能令規定比原文更寬或更窄。仔細列出條件、行動者和後果，再檢查前後案例，才能測試最初印象是否可靠。02

律法放在盟約敘事裏具有意義，但不代表每項規定都有完全相同的對象與功能。出埃及記先記述拯救與立約，再呈現一連串判例。讀者可以把這個次序視為整體文學推進的一部分，卻不能以文學排列自動解決成書過程或實際執法的所有歷史問題。必須分辨現存文本、對其形成的重建，以及個別法庭如何運作的證據；三者彼此相關，卻不能互相取代。03

比較古代法律時也要留意形式。兩份文獻可能討論相似傷害，卻對補償、受害者身分或啟動規則的條件作出不同安排。差異是證據，不能因為想證明相似而刪去。比較時應說明每份見證的年代、語言、出土地及文類，再詢問相似公式是否可能來自共同的實際問題，而不是直接抄用。04

法條列出某項刑罰，不足以證明歷史中每次都照此判決。法律文本可能表達規範、理想、限制或例子；要知道某一案件如何處理，必須有額外證據。反過來說，沒有保存法庭紀錄，也不能證明規定從未產生影響。歷史結論應與保存下來的證據相稱。神學閱讀可以問文學呈現如何塑造責任，卻應承認歷史上的具體運用是另一個問題。05

古代受眾與今日群體之間的關係需要論證，不能直接假設。讀者可以考慮經文在以色列盟約中的位置、其道德視野、後來聖經的接受方式，以及古今制度的差異。任何步驟都不能授權讀者忽視經文所提及的弱勢者。負責任的應用既要問某項規定在其處境中提供甚麼保護，也要正視其中挑戰現代讀者的等級與排除。延續和差異都應被明確說出。06

文體意識因此是一種聆聽的紀律。它避免把敘事中所描述的事情直接當成命令，也避免以為一個法律例子涵蓋所有可能情況。同時，它不容許讀者以文體為藉口逃避神學或倫理問題。先描述形式，再詢問結構支持哪些主張、留下哪些假設，以及較大的結論需要甚麼額外證據。07

A worked legal comparison: a dangerous ox 案例研究：會傷人的牛

Exodus 21:28–32 offers a compact sequence in which details change a legal outcome. Verse 28 describes an ox that gores a man or woman to death; the ox is stoned, its flesh is not eaten, and the owner is described as not liable. Verse 29 changes the condition: the ox had previously been prone to gore, its owner had been warned, and the owner failed to guard it. The statement then specifies consequences for both the ox and its owner. These differences make the owner's prior knowledge and conduct central to any account of the passage.08

The Hebrew wording in verse 29 connects a history of goring with warning to the owner and failure to keep the animal under control. It is important to keep these elements together rather than attributing the outcome to ownership alone. Verse 30 adds a condition concerning an imposed ransom. Its relation to the preceding death sentence is itself a question for interpretation: the reader must ask who imposes the payment, under what conditions, and what the passage explicitly says or leaves unstated. A concise lesson should not pretend the legal procedure is fully described where the text does not provide it.09

Verse 31 applies a specified rule when a son or daughter is gored. Verse 32 treats the death of an enslaved man or woman differently: payment is made to the enslaved person's master and the ox is stoned. The distinction is morally significant. A modern reader should neither silently assimilate the social statuses nor use the presence of a penalty to claim that the passage erases slavery. The law recognizes an injury while operating within a social hierarchy that requires honest discussion. The words “protection” and “equality” cannot be used interchangeably without argument.10

For a controlled comparison, the Laws of Eshnunna include provisions about a goring ox and distinguish a prior warning from other circumstances. Martha Roth's translation identifies relevant provisions in paragraphs 53–58. The comparison is illuminating precisely because the details do not simply match. Ask what each text says about the animal, the owner, prior warning and the person harmed, and identify where the consequences differ. Comparison does not establish that the author of Exodus copied Eshnunna; similarity may have more than one explanation, and the surviving witnesses require their own historical assessment.11

A useful classroom table has separate columns for the biblical text, the comparative text and the interpreter's inference. In the first two columns list only what the respective texts state. In the third identify a possible connection, the evidence that favours it and an alternative account. This separation prevents an attractive analogy from becoming a claim of direct dependence. It also makes omissions visible: a surviving legal collection may be selective, and a modern reader should not assume that silence about one case proves that no other ancient ruling existed.12

The comparison invites but does not finish theological reflection. One might consider accountability when foreseeable harm is left unguarded, but moving from an ox to a modern institution requires attention to differences in knowledge, power, causation and remedy. A congregation discussing safety must not turn an ancient passage into an unqualified substitute for applicable law or for careful attention to people harmed. The interpretive move should name its analogy and its limits. Where a modern case differs materially, those differences must be part of the argument rather than a footnote to it.13

A final check asks what each proposed summary leaves out. “Owners are always punished for their animals” overlooks the contrast between verses 28 and 29. “Only the animal matters” ignores the owner's warning and responsibility. “All victims are treated identically” erases the distinction in verse 32. Each summary picks up a feature but fails to account for the progression of the cases. The passage's legal reasoning becomes clearer when the conditions and outcomes are read together.14

出埃及記二十一章二十八至三十二節呈現一組細節改變法律結果的案例。二十八節描述牛觸死男人或女人：牛要被石頭打死，不可吃其肉，牛主則被視為無責。二十九節改變了條件：牛先前慣於傷人，主人曾受警告，卻沒有看管；隨後經文對牛及主人都列出後果。要說明此段，必須正視主人原先所知和所作的事。08

二十九節的希伯來文把牛過去觸人的情況、對主人的警告及沒有看管連在一起。不可只憑「擁有牛」就解釋結果，而把其他條件刪去。三十節再提出可能施加贖價；其與前面死刑規定的關係本身需要解釋。誰有權施加？在甚麼條件下施加？文本明說了甚麼，又留下甚麼沒有說明？若經文沒有列出完整程序，簡短教材也不應假裝一切都已清楚。09

三十一節把指定的規則應用於兒子或女兒被觸；三十二節則以不同方式處理奴僕或婢女的死亡：銀錢交給其主人，牛也要被石頭打死。這個區別有重要的倫理意義。現代讀者既不應悄悄把不同社會身分視為相同，也不能因有刑罰就宣稱此處消除了奴隸制度。經文承認傷害，卻仍在一個需要誠實討論的社會等級中運作。「保護」和「平等」不能不加論證地互換。10

作有控制的比較時，以實嫩拿法律也包括傷人之牛的規定，並區分先前警告等情況。Martha Roth 的譯本把有關條款列於第五十三至五十八條。比較之所以有啟發，是因細節不完全相同。應逐項問兩份文本如何描述動物、主人、先前警告及受害者，並指出後果在哪裏不同。這種相似本身不能證明出埃及記作者直接抄用；相似可能有多種原因，現存見證也需要獨立的歷史評估。11

課堂表格可以分為聖經文本、比較文本及詮釋推論三欄。前兩欄只列兩份文本實際說出的內容；第三欄提出可能關係、支持證據及替代解釋。這種分法防止有吸引力的類比變成未經證明的直接依賴，也使文本的缺漏可見。現存法律文集可能本身有所選擇，不能以某個案例沒有出現就斷定古代不存在其他規定。12

這組比較邀請神學反思，卻不會替讀者完成反思。例如，可以思想人在可預見的傷害面前沒有防範時的責任；但由牛主轉到現代機構，仍須注意知識、權力、因果和補救之間的差異。教會討論安全時，不能把古代經文直接當作適用法律的替代，也不能忽略實際受傷的人。應用必須說明類比及其界線；若現代案例有重大差異，差異就應成為論證的一部分。13

最後要檢查每個摘要省略了甚麼。「主人對其動物一律受罰」忽略二十八與二十九節的對比；「只有動物重要」忽略對主人的警告及其責任；「所有受害者待遇完全相同」則抹去三十二節的區別。每個摘要都抓到某個特點，卻沒有交代案例如何推進。把條件與結果一起閱讀，才能更清楚理解經文的法律推理。14

Reading Hebrew narrative as narrative 按敘事的方式閱讀敘事

A story communicates through sequence, selection and perspective. It may tell readers what a character says and does without reporting every motive, or it may disclose information to the reader that a character does not receive. A narrator's evaluative comment has a different evidential status from an inference about an unspoken thought. The distinction is especially important in sparse narrative: an interpreter may responsibly propose a motive, but should identify it as a proposal and consider what else could explain the same action.15

Genesis 22:1–19 provides a demanding illustration. The opening tells the audience that God tests Abraham; the subsequent divine command identifies Isaac and directs Abraham to the land of Moriah. The next morning Abraham prepares for the journey. The narrator reports action and dialogue with remarkable economy, leaving the reader to consider not only what happens but what is not narrated. The introductory statement gives the audience information about the test; it does not authorize us to assume that Isaac is given the same information at that moment.16

The journey's pacing matters. The text can move from preparation to the third day in a few lines, then slow to recount speech and successive actions at the place of offering. This is a description of relative narrative presentation rather than a claim to recover precisely what any ancient audience felt. Repetition contributes to the movement: Genesis 22:6 and 22:8 both say that the two went together. A reader can ask how the repeated wording frames Isaac's question about the missing lamb and Abraham's answer without assuming that repetition has only one possible emotional effect.17

The exchange between father and son is especially important. Isaac names the fire and wood and asks where the lamb is; Abraham answers that God will provide the lamb for the offering. Is the answer a disclosure of confidence, a way to protect Isaac from fear, or something more complex? The text reports the words, not an explicit explanation of Abraham's private reasoning. The exegete should distinguish the options, show how each accounts for the later provision of the ram, and resist presenting an attractive psychological reconstruction as a fact narrated in the passage.18

At the climax the angel calls to Abraham, who responds, and commands him not to harm the boy. A ram is found and offered in place of Isaac. The narrative ends by recounting Abraham's return to his servants; it does not in that sentence describe Isaac's movements. Silence invites questions, but the fact that a character is not named in a final sentence does not prove a hidden event. An interpretation that proposes an explanation for the omission must account for the text's selective narration and for the absence of explicit support.19

The episode also raises serious theological and ethical questions. The narrator's designation of the episode as a test does not make the threat to the child insignificant, nor should a contemporary teacher use the passage to validate a private claim to divine authorization for harming another person. Interpretive responsibility includes attending to the story's interruption of violence and to readers for whom such a text may be distressing. Literary analysis does not remove the ethical problem; it clarifies which details an ethical and theological account must address.20

Finally, literary and historical questions should be related without being collapsed. A carefully argued account of repetition or characterization in the received narrative does not by itself prove the date of every component, the source history or the precise practices of the setting. Conversely, a proposal about composition must still explain the form in which readers encounter the story. The best classroom discussion makes each question explicit and allows a conclusion at one level to be tested without treating it as a substitute for evidence at another.21

故事透過次序、選擇和觀點傳達意義。敘事可能只告訴讀者人物說了甚麼、做了甚麼，卻沒有報告每個動機；也可能讓讀者知道某些人物尚不知道的事情。敘事者直接作出的評語，與讀者推論人物未說出的想法，具有不同的證據地位。在精簡敘事中特別要緊：詮釋者可以提出合理動機，但必須標明它是推論，並考慮同一行動是否容許其他解釋。15

創世記二十二章一至十九節提供一個不容易處理的例子。開頭告訴讀者上帝試驗亞伯拉罕；隨後的命令指明以撒，並吩咐亞伯拉罕前往摩利亞。第二天早上，亞伯拉罕預備出發。敘事以精簡方式呈現行動和對話，促使讀者既留意發生的事，也留意沒有敘述的事。開頭向讀者提供試驗的資訊，並不授權我們假定以撒在同一時刻也獲得了相同資訊。16

旅程的節奏值得注意。文本可以用短短幾節由預備推進到第三日；到了獻祭地點，卻放慢速度，逐一記述言語和行動。這是對敘事呈現速度的相對描述，不等於我們已準確重建每位古代聽眾的感受。重複也參與推進：二十二章六節和八節都說二人一同走。讀者可以詢問這句重複如何框住以撒關於羔羊的問題與亞伯拉罕的回答，卻不應假定重複只可能產生一種情感效果。17

父子對話尤其重要。以撒指出有火和柴，詢問羔羊在哪裏；亞伯拉罕回答上帝會預備供獻的羔羊。這回答是信心的表達、避免以撒恐懼的說法，還是更複雜的言語？經文記下說話，沒有直接解釋亞伯拉罕內心的全部推理。詮釋者應分辨不同可能，檢查各自如何解釋後來公羊的出現，避免把吸引人的心理重建當作敘事明說的事實。18

高潮處，天使呼喚亞伯拉罕；他回應後，天使吩咐他不可傷害孩子。隨後他看見並獻上一隻公羊，代替以撒。結尾記述亞伯拉罕回到僕人那裏；該句沒有描述以撒的行動。沉默可以引發問題，但最後一句沒有提及某人物，並不足以證明另有隱藏事件。若要提出解釋，必須同時考慮敘事的選擇性及直接證據的缺乏。19

這段故事也引發嚴肅的神學及倫理問題。敘事者稱其為試驗，並不使孩子所面臨的威脅變得不重要；現代教師也不能以此肯定私人聲稱上帝授權傷害他人的做法。負責任的解釋應留意故事如何阻止暴力，也應留心可能因經文受困擾的讀者。文學分析不會消除倫理問題，而是幫助我們說明任何倫理及神學解釋必須處理哪些細節。20

文學和歷史問題應當對話，卻不能混為一談。對現存故事的重複和人物塑造提出有根據的說明，本身不能證明每部分的年代、來源歷史或處境中的具體實踐。反過來說，成書過程的重建也仍需解釋讀者實際面對的文本形式。良好的討論會明確提出每個問題，使一個層次的結論可以受檢驗，而不被用來取代另一個層次所需的證據。21

04 · LAW & NARRATIVE 04 · 律法與敘事

Gospel narrative: the healing and the controversy 福音書敘事：醫治與爭議

Mark 2:1–12 begins in a crowded house. Jesus is speaking the word, and four people carry a paralyzed man toward him. Because the crowd blocks their approach, they open the roof and lower the mat. The spatial details are not mere decoration: the crowd creates an obstacle, the carriers' action overcomes it, and the man's subsequent departure before everyone reverses the initial difficulty of access. The narrative moves from arrival and obstruction to a public action whose witnesses respond at the end.22

When Jesus sees “their faith,” he tells the man that his sins are forgiven. The plural pronoun raises a question about the group to which it refers; at minimum, an interpreter should not silently reduce the story to an isolated individual and ignore the people who carried him. The words to the man also generate a new conflict. Some scribes reason internally that only God can forgive sins. The narrator discloses their reasoning before Jesus responds, so readers know more than would be available from the scene's outward actions alone.23

Jesus asks which is easier to say: that sins are forgiven, or that the man should rise, take his mat and walk. His subsequent statement about the Son of Man's authority and his command to the man bring the controversy and healing together. The man rises, takes the mat and departs in view of all; the crowd is astonished and glorifies God. One possible synthesis is that Jesus' public healing accompanies his claim of authority to forgive. The sentence must retain both elements rather than making physical healing irrelevant or treating the forgiveness controversy as an incidental preface.24

At the same time, an interpreter must resist explaining the man's disability as punishment for a personal sin merely because forgiveness precedes healing. The story does not identify a particular wrongdoing as the cause of his condition. Nor does the expression “their faith” license a rule that every person who remains ill lacks sufficient faith. Such applications go beyond the stated narrative and can injure vulnerable readers. A reading can affirm the narrative's theological claims while distinguishing them from assumptions it does not establish.25

The scribes' words deserve careful treatment as well. Their question about divine forgiveness is intelligible within the narrative's theological world; the story presents a disagreement about Jesus' authority, not permission to caricature Jewish people as a whole. Identifying these particular scribes as characters does not justify transferring blame to an entire community. Historical context and ethical application both require the interpreter to name the passage's participants accurately and avoid inherited generalizations that exceed the text.26

A literary boundary can begin with the return to Capernaum and conclude with the public response, yet the adjacent episodes still matter. The following call of Levi and meal controversy continue questions about Jesus, social relations and the response of religious interlocutors. The reader should ask whether repeated questions or responses link these episodes while allowing each scene its own distinctive movement. An outline can be revised if new evidence shows that a transition belongs to both the close of one episode and the opening of another.27

A second Gospel example, Matthew 8:5–13, can be compared without flattening each account into a single generic healing story. Its centurion speaks about authority and trust; its immediate neighbors and internal dialogue have their own contribution. Where a parallel account differs, the interpreter should record the difference before explaining it. Literary comparison, claims about historical sequence and theological interpretation are related but distinct tasks. The appropriate conclusion depends on which question is being asked and what evidence is available.28

馬可福音二章一至十二節由一間擠滿人的房子開始。耶穌正在講道，四個人抬着一位癱瘓者到祂面前。因群眾擋住去路，他們拆開屋頂，把褥子縋下。空間細節不是裝飾：群眾形成障礙，抬人的行動克服障礙，而那人最後在眾人面前離去，與最初難以進入形成反轉。故事由來到與受阻，推進至一個人人可見並作出回應的行動。22

耶穌看見「他們的信心」，就對那人說他的罪得赦免。複數代名詞引出一個問題：它指向哪一群人？至少，詮釋者不應悄悄把故事縮減為一個孤立個人，忽略抬他來的人。耶穌的話也引起新的衝突。有幾位文士心裏議論，認為只有上帝能赦罪。敘事者在耶穌回應以前已向讀者透露他們的想法，因此讀者知道的比現場外在行動所能顯示的更多。23

耶穌問說「罪得赦免」或說「起來、拿褥子、行走」，哪一樣較容易。祂隨後提到人子赦罪的權柄，又命令那人起來，將爭議和醫治連接。那人起來，拿着褥子在眾人面前離去；群眾驚奇並歸榮耀給上帝。一個可能的綜合是：耶穌公開的醫治伴隨祂對赦罪權柄的宣告。摘要應保留兩者，而不能把身體醫治當成無關，也不能把赦罪爭議只當成偶然序言。24

同時，不能只因赦罪先於醫治，就推定那人的障礙是某項個人罪行的懲罰。故事沒有指出某項過犯是其病況的原因。「他們的信心」也不能變成一條規則，斷定持續患病的人必定缺乏足夠信心。這些應用超出了故事所說，並可能傷害弱勢讀者。詮釋者可以肯定敘事的神學主張，同時拒絕文本沒有確立的假設。25

文士的話也應被謹慎處理。他們對上帝赦罪權柄的問題，在故事的神學世界中可以理解；故事呈現的是對耶穌權柄的爭論，並未授權讀者醜化所有猶太人。辨認這幾位文士是敘事角色，不等於可以把責任轉移到整個群體。歷史處境與倫理應用都要求我們準確稱呼人物，避免沿用超出文本的概括。26

段落界限可由重返迦百農開始，以群眾公開回應結束，但相鄰故事仍然重要。接着的利未蒙召與筵席爭議，繼續涉及耶穌、社會關係及宗教對話者的回應。讀者應問重複的問題和回應如何連接場景，同時讓每幕保留獨特的推進。若新證據顯示過渡句同時參與前段結尾及後段開端，原先的分段也可以修訂。27

另一福音書例子是馬太福音八章五至十三節，可以比較，卻不可把不同敘事壓平成一個籠統的醫治故事。百夫長談論權柄與信任，其前後故事和內部對話各有作用。平行記載有差異時，詮釋者應先記錄，再提出解釋。文學比較、歷史先後次序的主張和神學解讀互相關聯，卻不是同一項工作。適當結論取決於問題是甚麼，以及手上有甚麼證據。28

05 · LAW & NARRATIVE 05 · 律法與敘事

From narrative description to theological and ethical judgment 由敘事描述走向神學與倫理判斷

A narrative can be authoritative for a faith community without making every action of every character a command. The first task is to identify whose action is described, what the narrator says about it, and what follows within the story. A deceptive act, for example, may advance the plot without being explicitly commended. A heroic figure can act inconsistently. The interpreter should examine the narrative's evaluation and its wider setting rather than deriving an instruction by copying an isolated behavior.29

Neither should a reader move too quickly from a desirable outcome to the approval of every preceding means. A story might report deliverance after a difficult decision, yet its structure may invite questions about that decision. Ask what the characters know, what choices are available to them, and whether the narrator offers a comment or leaves a gap. If the account does not settle every ethical issue, acknowledge the gap. Supplying an elegant moral can make a lesson appear decisive while silencing the ambiguity the passage asks readers to face.30

Theological synthesis begins with the particular episode and then explains its relationship to a larger argument. In Genesis 22 the repeated address and response, the demand concerning Isaac, the interruption and the provision of the ram all belong to the narrative movement. A proposal focused only on Abraham's initial departure must still explain the prohibition against harming the boy and the later blessing. A proposal focused only on the ram must likewise account for the threat and the journey. A defensible synthesis does not list every detail, but it preserves the principal relations among them.31

Narrative's relationship to historical claims requires a separate set of questions. A story may present places, rulers, customs and sequences as part of its account; the historical investigation asks what independent witnesses, material evidence or critical comparisons can establish. The narrative's literary artistry does not, by itself, prove that its events did or did not occur. Likewise, a historical judgment about one feature does not automatically settle how the text characterizes a person or structures a scene. Clear writing identifies when it moves from one inquiry to another.32

Comparing two narratives of an apparently similar event can sharpen rather than blur their differences. First describe each in its own sequence, noting speakers, audience, setting, quotations and conclusion. Then compare the features relevant to a particular question. A difference may reflect selectivity, a distinct tradition, literary shaping or another factor, but none of these explanations should be announced before the evidence is weighed. A harmonization is an interpretive proposal, not an observation that can replace the two accounts.33

The same caution applies to intertextual proposals. A later narrative may quote earlier Scripture explicitly, evoke it through distinctive wording or share a broad motif that appears widely elsewhere. These degrees of connection should not be treated as identical. Readers can test a proposed echo by asking whether the earlier passage was available, how distinctive the verbal and thematic correspondence is, whether the surrounding contexts cohere, and what alternatives remain. Even a persuasive relationship does not mean every feature of the earlier story maps mechanically onto the later one.34

Application requires knowledge of the community addressed today. A classroom exercise should ask who has authority, who is vulnerable, what harms are possible, and how the proposed action could be evaluated. In the Mark 2 example, friends remove a barrier that prevents a man from approaching Jesus; a contemporary discussion might examine barriers to access and the role of accompaniment. That analogy is a starting point, not a license to speak over disabled people or assume that everyone wants the same form of assistance. Listening to those directly affected belongs to the work of responsible application.35

The resulting proposition should be specific enough to be tested and modest enough to reflect its evidence. Compare “Jesus responds to faith by forgiving and healing the man amid a dispute about authority” with “faith always guarantees immediate physical recovery.” The first follows the episode's narrated sequence; the second converts one event into an unrestricted universal promise the passage does not state. A responsible teacher can affirm hope, pray for healing and care for the sick without claiming that an outcome has been guaranteed by this story.36

The label “law” can mislead when it causes a reader to assume that every ancient collection was a comprehensive statute book in the modern sense. A collection may select representative cases, use established formulations and arrange topics in ways that invite readers to infer relationships. The surviving material has its own transmission history. Before concluding that a missing case was legally irrelevant, ask whether the document claims to be exhaustive and whether other kinds of evidence survive. John H. Walton discusses formal distinctions and comparative materials, but an interpreter should also test the specific comparisons rather than turning a classification into a theory of direct influence.37

A similar caution belongs to the reading of narrative conventions. Robert Alter describes repeated patterns, dialogue and reticence as features that invite close attention in biblical storytelling. That approach encourages interpreters to notice how a story controls the distribution of knowledge. Yet identifying an artistic feature does not mean that every repetition carries one predetermined meaning. The reader should show what a repetition does in its immediate setting, compare plausible alternatives and avoid treating a modern literary term as though it were a label supplied by the ancient narrator.38

The ox provisions illustrate what such care looks like in practice. Exodus 21:28 opens with a fatal goring; verse 29 adds a history of the animal's behavior, warning to the owner and a failure to guard it. The difference between the cases matters because the passage itself states it. A comparison with another ancient legal text should give its own conditions and outcomes in equally precise terms. It is methodologically weak to reproduce the biblical distinctions in detail but summarize the comparative material simply as “the same law.” The difference may be the most informative part of the comparison.39

The story in Genesis 22 provides a different kind of test. The narrator tells the audience that God is testing Abraham, then gives a command whose implications for Isaac are grave. The audience's knowledge is not identical to the characters' knowledge. In verses 7–8 Isaac asks about the lamb and Abraham responds; in verses 11–13 the angel stops the action and a ram is provided. One can trace the development of information through these scenes without claiming access to every participant's thoughts. A theological interpretation must then explain how its proposition accounts for command, silence, interruption and provision together.40

Mark 2 invites a complementary exercise in narrative perspective. The scribes reason in their hearts, and the narrative reports those thoughts to the reader. Jesus then addresses their reasoning; the man acts publicly, and the crowd responds. The reader sees an interior dispute and an outward event placed in one sequence. Asking who knows what at each point protects the interpretation from flattening different perspectives. It also helps a reader see why the concluding reaction matters to the narrative rather than treating it as an irrelevant appendix.41

A responsible comparison never ends with the discovery of a resemblance. It asks what the resemblance can and cannot bear. Similarity in a legal condition might support a comparison of social problems while leaving the route of transmission uncertain. Similarity in a story's plot might illuminate a literary convention while leaving a precise historical relationship unresolved. In both cases an interpreter can offer a useful and even theologically significant proposal without overstating the evidence. The strength of the claim should rise and fall with the strength of the reasons made available to readers.42

敘事可以對信仰群體具有權威，卻不代表每個人物的每項行動都是命令。首先要辨認誰在行動、敘事者如何描述，以及故事接着發生甚麼。例如，一項欺騙可能推動情節，卻沒有被明確稱許；受尊敬的人物也可能前後不一致。詮釋者應檢查故事的評價和更廣的處境，而不是複製孤立行動來推導命令。29

同樣，不可太快因結果令人滿意，就認定先前所有手段都得到肯定。故事可能在一個困難決定之後記述拯救，其結構卻仍邀請讀者追問該決定。應問人物知道甚麼、有哪些選擇，敘事者有否評論，還是留下空白。若經文沒有解決每一個倫理問題，應承認其界線。過於整齊的道德教訓可能看似果斷，卻使文本要求讀者面對的模糊之處失聲。30

神學綜合應從個別故事開始，再交代它與更大論證的關係。創世記二十二章中，反覆的呼喚與回應、關於以撒的命令、中止行動以及公羊的預備，都屬於故事推進。只聚焦亞伯拉罕出發的命題，仍須解釋不可傷害孩子的吩咐與後來的祝福；只聚焦公羊的命題，也要交代威脅與旅程。可靠的綜合不必逐一列舉細節，卻應保留主要關係。31

敘事與歷史主張的關係需要另一組問題。故事可能呈現地點、統治者、習俗與事件次序；歷史研究則問獨立見證、物質證據或批判比較可以確立甚麼。敘事具有文學技巧，本身不能證明事件必然發生或沒有發生；同樣，對某一歷史細節的判斷，也不會自動解決人物如何被塑造或場景如何安排。清楚的寫作應標明何時由一種問題轉到另一種問題。32

比較兩篇似乎記述相近事件的故事，可以讓差異更清楚，而不是使它們模糊。先按各自次序描述人物、聽眾、場景、引文和結尾，再比較與特定問題相關的細節。差異可能反映選材、不同傳統、文學安排或其他因素，但不能在衡量證據之前就宣布原因。把兩份記載調和，是一項詮釋提議，不是可以取代兩篇文本的直接觀察。33

提出文本互涉時也須謹慎。較後的故事可能明確引用先前經文，也可能藉獨特措辭喚起它，或只是共享一個廣泛出現的主題。三種關係不能視為完全相同。檢驗可能的回響時，可以問先前經文是否可供作者使用、詞語和主題的對應有多獨特、上下文是否相稱，以及是否仍有其他解釋。即使關聯令人信服，也不代表早期故事的每個細節都機械地對應後期故事。34

應用需要認識今天所面向的群體。課堂練習可以問：誰有權力？誰處於弱勢？可能造成甚麼傷害？如何評估所提議的行動？馬可福音二章中，朋友移除阻礙那人接近耶穌的障礙；當代討論可以由此檢視無障礙環境和陪伴。但類比只是起點，不能用來替身心障礙者發言，或假定每個人都需要同一種幫助。聆聽直接受影響的人，是負責任應用的一部分。35

最後的命題既要具體到可以檢驗，也要謙遜到符合證據。比較「耶穌在權柄爭論中回應信心，赦免並醫治那人」與「信心總會保證立即恢復身體健康」。前者追蹤故事所記載的次序；後者把單一事件轉成經文沒有說出的無限制普遍應許。教師可以肯定盼望、為醫治禱告並照顧病人，同時不聲稱此故事保證了某個結果。36

「律法」一詞若使讀者以為每份古代文集都像現代法典一樣完整，反而會造成誤導。文集可能選擇具代表性的案例，使用既有公式，並以特定次序引導讀者看見關係；現存材料本身也有傳抄歷史。斷定沒有列出的案例在法律上毫無意義之前，應先問文獻是否聲稱無所不包，以及是否保存其他類型證據。John H. Walton 討論形式區分與比較材料；詮釋者仍須核對每一組具體比較，不能把分類直接變成相互影響的理論。37

閱讀敘事慣例時也需要同樣謹慎。Robert Alter 討論重複模式、對話和節制的敘述，提醒讀者注意聖經故事如何分配資訊。這種方法使我們更留心故事中誰知道甚麼，但辨認出文學特色，不代表每一次重複都有預先決定的唯一意思。讀者仍須指出重複在直接上下文中的作用，公平比較其他可能，並避免把現代文學術語誤當成古代敘事者自己使用的標籤。38

牛觸人的規定說明這種謹慎如何實踐。出埃及記二十一章二十八節由致命觸人事件開始；二十九節加入動物過去的行為、對主人的警告和沒有看管。這些差異重要，因為經文本身把它們寫出來。與其他古代法律比較時，也應同樣仔細交代對方的條件和結果。如果詳細列出聖經的區分，卻只用「相同法律」概括比較材料，方法便不夠穩健；差異可能正是比較中最有資訊價值的部分。39

創世記二十二章提供不同的測試。敘事者告訴讀者上帝試驗亞伯拉罕，接着呈現一項對以撒有嚴重後果的命令。讀者與人物知道的並不完全相同。七至八節以撒問及羔羊，亞伯拉罕回答；十一至十三節天使阻止行動，隨後出現公羊。讀者可以追蹤資訊如何在各場景中展開，卻不必宣稱掌握每位人物的全部心思。神學解釋仍須說明其命題如何同時交代命令、沉默、中止與預備。40

馬可福音二章則可作補充練習。文士心中議論，敘事把他們的想法告訴讀者；耶穌接着回應，癱瘓者公開行動，群眾作出反應。讀者在一個次序中看到內心爭議和外在事件。逐步問誰知道甚麼，可防止不同觀點被壓平，也有助理解最後群眾回應為何是故事的一部分，而不是無關緊要的附錄。41

負責任的比較不會在發現相似時便結束，而會追問相似可以支持甚麼、又不能支持甚麼。法律條件相近，可能有助比較社會問題，卻仍不能確定傳遞路徑；情節相近，可能照亮某種文學慣例，卻仍不能解決精確的歷史關係。兩種情況下，詮釋者都可以提出有用、甚至有神學意義的解釋，但不應誇大證據。主張的力度應與向讀者展示的理由相稱。42

06 · LAW & NARRATIVE 06 · 律法與敘事

A repeatable workshop for law and story 可重複使用的律法與敘事研習

Begin with a fresh reading and a provisional boundary. For a law, mark each condition, action, consequence and exception in different colours. For a story, mark setting, participants, changes in scene, reported speech and narrator comment. Write a one-sentence description of the unit's movement before consulting a commentary. This first sentence is a hypothesis, not a possession to defend. A source that challenges it may be more valuable than one that repeats it.43

Next, create a record of the textual witnesses used. Identify the edition or translation, and if a textual difference affects the interpretation, explain the competing readings and why a decision is difficult. A translation may make a necessary grammatical choice, but readers should not mistake that choice for an explicit feature of every ancient witness. Where original-language tools are available, verify the form in context; a dictionary entry alone cannot determine how a word functions in its clause.44

For Exodus 21:28–32, students can produce a row for each verse and identify the victim, the condition of the animal, the owner's prior knowledge and the prescribed outcome. Comparing rows shows how verse 29 changes the scenario of verse 28. A further row for verse 32 exposes a difference in treatment related to status. The table is not a complete reconstruction of ancient adjudication; it is a disciplined representation of what this literary sequence says and does not say.45

For Genesis 22, a similar chart can track what the narrator tells the reader, what Abraham says, what Isaac says and what remains unstated. Repeated phrases can be underlined; the shorter account of the journey can be compared with the detailed report at the altar. Ask students to label a sentence as observation or inference and to give at least one rival interpretation for a significant silence. This practice does not make all interpretations equally plausible. It creates a fair basis for explaining why one interpretation better accounts for the data.46

For Mark 2, map the entrance, the crowd, the roof, the man's position before Jesus and his movement out before everyone. Then map the shifts in speech: the announcement of forgiveness, the scribes' internal reasoning, Jesus' question, his claim and command, and the crowd's response. These two maps disclose different but related dimensions of one episode. A synopsis that attends only to the miracle may overlook the authority dispute; one that attends only to the dispute may neglect the embodied and communal features of the healing.47

The next step is scholarly dialogue. Choose a critical commentary or study that presents evidence for a reading, and record its claim, argument, textual evidence and significant counterargument. When using a secondary source's report of an earlier scholar, state that relationship instead of implying that the earlier source was directly consulted. Avoid turning a list of prestigious names into a substitute for argument. When two scholars disagree, identify whether they differ about the words of the text, the relevant historical context, the text's literary function or a theological conclusion.48

A useful peer-review question is, “What finding would make you revise this claim?” For a proposed legal comparison, the answer might be a closer parallel from another collection or a different reading of the cited provision. For a narrative interpretation, it might be a contextual feature that weakens the proposed link or an unnoticed speech by another character. The question encourages intellectual honesty without requiring artificial neutrality: an interpreter can hold a reasoned conviction while identifying evidence that matters to its assessment.49

Finally, present the result in layers. Start with the passage's movement in plain language. Show the key textual observations and identify any contested decisions. Place carefully limited comparisons and historical claims where they help the reader assess the argument. Then formulate a theological proposition and an application, explicitly explaining how each step follows. End with the question the interpretation leaves open. A well-designed lesson gives readers a way to return to the text, not merely a conclusion to memorize.50

A good seminar discussion makes the relation between observation and judgment explicit. One student may propose that Exodus 21 presents responsibility solely in terms of ownership. Another can point to the prior warning and failure to guard in verse 29, then ask why verse 28 describes a different outcome. The first student can revise the proposal without giving up the broader ethical question. The aim is not to win by finding a single word, but to account for the text's distinctions before considering how they matter beyond its original setting.

In Genesis 22 a group might initially describe Abraham as entirely silent. A second reading corrects that claim: he speaks to his servants and to Isaac, though the narrator does not report all the thoughts readers might wish to know. The revised observation opens better questions about what Abraham says to each audience and what remains undisclosed. Students should not confuse the absence of a psychological explanation with proof that no explanation is possible; nor should they mistake their preferred explanation for a narrated fact. Precise restraint makes interpretation more interesting, not less.

In Mark 2 the first summary may be “a man is healed.” That is true but insufficient as a summary of the whole episode. The roof, the carriers, the announcement of forgiveness, the scribes' thoughts, the question about authority, the command to rise and the crowd's praise all shape the story. A better summary chooses the central relations while recognizing that a single sentence cannot contain every detail. The ensuing discussion can recover what the summary compresses and ask why the author places the dialogue where it appears.

A teacher can invite each group to retain a version history: its first reading, one objection grounded in the passage, a revision and one remaining question. The exercise evaluates an argument's responsiveness to evidence rather than rewarding the first confident answer. Where the debate concerns a larger doctrinal conclusion, the class should identify additional passages and theological commitments that enter the discussion. This prevents the burden of an entire doctrinal system from being silently placed on one short unit while allowing the unit to contribute genuinely to a wider conversation.

These habits are transferable because they are concrete. They do not require every interpreter to use the same theological vocabulary or arrive at the same conclusion. They require each to show the text, state the inference, name a plausible alternative and explain why the conclusion follows. For ministers, teachers and students alike, such transparency allows a community to disagree without confusing a disputed inference with a direct quotation. It also keeps interpretation open to correction when a forgotten detail or a better source comes to light.

首先重新閱讀，暫定段落界限。閱讀法律時，用不同顏色標出條件、行動、後果與例外；閱讀故事時，標出場景、人物、轉場、對話及敘事者評論。查閱註釋書之前，先用一句話描述段落如何推進。這個初稿是假設，不是需要守護的財產。能挑戰初稿的資料，有時比重複它的資料更有價值。43

接着記錄使用了哪些文本見證。指出版本或譯本；若異文影響解釋，應交代不同讀法及其困難。譯本可能必須在語法上作出選擇，讀者卻不應把該選擇誤認為每份古代見證都明說的內容。若有原文工具，應在上下文中核對詞形；字典條目本身不能決定某詞在子句中的功能。44

在出埃及記二十一章二十八至三十二節，可讓學生逐節列出受害者、動物的情況、主人事先所知及規定的結果。比較表格便可看出二十九節如何改變二十八節的情況；三十二節則使與身分相關的待遇差異可見。這張表不是古代審判程序的完整重建，而是有紀律地表達該段文學次序說了甚麼，以及沒有說甚麼。45

在創世記二十二章，也可以列表追蹤敘事者告訴讀者的事、亞伯拉罕所說的話、以撒所說的話及未被說出的內容。把重複句子劃線，再比較旅程的簡短記述與祭壇上的細節。請學生標明每句屬於觀察還是推論，並為重要沉默至少提出一個替代解釋。這不表示所有解釋都同樣有力，而是建立公平基礎，說明哪一種更能交代資料。46

閱讀馬可福音二章，可畫出入口、群眾、屋頂、那人在耶穌面前的位置，以及他最後如何在眾人面前走出。再畫出言語的轉換：赦罪宣告、文士心中議論、耶穌的問題、權柄宣告與命令、群眾的回應。兩張圖呈現同一故事不同而相關的層面。只注意神蹟的摘要可能忽略權柄爭議；只注意爭議的摘要，則可能忽略身體與群體在醫治中的位置。47

下一步是與學術研究對話。選擇提出證據的批判註釋書或研究，記下其主張、論證、文本根據及重要反對意見。若使用的是後來學者對早期學者的轉述，應說明這層關係，不可假裝直接讀過原作。不要以名家名單代替論證。兩位學者有分歧時，應指出他們究竟對字詞、相關歷史處境、文學功能，還是神學結論有不同看法。48

同儕審閱時可以問：「甚麼發現會使你修訂這個主張？」對法律比較而言，答案可能是另一文集有更接近的平行例子，或所引用條款有不同讀法；對敘事解釋而言，答案可能是削弱所提關聯的上下文細節，或一段先前被忽略的人物對話。這個問題鼓勵誠實，卻不要求假裝沒有立場：詮釋者可以持有有理由的信念，同時指出哪些證據會影響判斷。49

最後分層呈現結果。先以清楚語言說明段落如何推進，再展示關鍵文本觀察並指出有爭議的選擇。把範圍適當的比較和歷史主張放在有助讀者評估論證的位置。然後提出神學命題與應用，明確交代每一步如何得出；最後保留尚未解決的問題。好的教學應給讀者一條返回經文的路，而不只是要求他們記住一個結論。50

好的研討應清楚交代觀察與判斷的關係。有學生認為出埃及記二十一章只按擁有權決定責任，另一位可以指出二十九節的事先警告與沒有看管，再問為何二十八節結果不同。第一位學生因而可以修訂說法，卻不必放棄較廣的倫理問題。目的不是靠找到一個字取勝，而是在思考經文對其他處境的意義之前，先交代文本本身的區分。

閱讀創世記二十二章時，一組可能先說亞伯拉罕完全沉默；重新閱讀會修正這句話，因為他曾向僕人和以撒說話，只是敘事沒有交代讀者想知道的全部心思。較準確的觀察讓我們進一步問，他對不同聽眾說了甚麼，又有哪些事沒有明說。缺少心理解釋不證明不能提出解釋，但讀者喜歡的解釋也不等於敘事已經明說的事實。

閱讀馬可福音二章時，「一個人得醫治」雖然正確，卻不足以概括整段。屋頂、抬人的朋友、赦罪宣告、文士的想法、權柄問題、起來的命令與群眾讚美，都塑造故事。更好的摘要應選擇主要關係，同時承認一句話不能容納每個細節。接下來的討論可以補回濃縮的部分，並問作者為何把對話放在這個位置。

教師可以請各組保留版本記錄：最初讀法、一項以經文為根據的反對理由、修訂後的句子，以及仍然存在的問題。這項練習評估論證是否回應證據，而不是獎勵最先作出自信答案的人。若討論涉及較大的教義結論，也應指出進入論證的其他經文與神學承諾，避免把整個教義體系的重量悄悄壓在一個短段落上，同時讓該段能真正參與更廣的對話。

這些習慣之所以可以轉移，是因為它們具體可行。它們不要求每位詮釋者使用同一套神學詞彙或得出相同結論，卻要求各人展示文本、說明推論、指出合理替代方案，並解釋結論如何得出。對牧者、教師與學生而言，透明使群體可以有分歧，卻不致把有爭議的推論誤認為直接引文；當被忽略的細節或更好的資料出現時，解釋也仍然可以受修正。
